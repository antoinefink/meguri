# Suspend protocol

Spec: engine/suspend
Requirements: R14, R1453, R1454, R1491, R2006, R2007, R2011, R2014
Anki: none (Meguri design, U32)
Scenarios: tests/suspend.rs, tests/external_change.rs, tests/kill_loop.rs
Status: Approved 2026-11-09 (R2066) for S1 and S4's original-SDK Ended completion amendment only. Every other section is in force and unchanged. The new internal S1/S4 resource-drain and cutoff amendment was independently reviewed 2026-11-09; implementation and unit acceptance remain pending.

## S1. Control lane

Serialize engine lifecycle controls. Count suspend's control-lane acquisition against the caller's timeout; contention reports SuspendDeadline("control lane"). Allow resume at most five seconds to acquire that lane. Opening owns it only during initial and final transitions. Suspend during Opening/Upgrading therefore returns unavailable promptly, and close in either phase also returns unavailable. Ordinary job admission tries the lane without blocking and refuses if a control owns it, including reentry from ops or participants. J2.1.1 separately defines short registration for cleanup of original resources. During `flush_for_background()`, flush every SuspendParticipant with writes still admitted, drain accepted writes, and full-sync the WAL. Undo remains and subsequent edits are accepted. Each participant must submit its pending writes to the executor before returning.

The final operation `suspend(timeout)` returns Suspended or Deferred{job_id}; repeating it after success is idempotent. Request cancellation of all ordinary jobs. Continued-processing requires explicit selection when a job starts and is permitted only for OneWaySync, ColpkgImport and ApkgImport. While any continued job remains, return Deferred with admission still open and participants unsuspended. Even a cancelled continued job remains deferred until its connections are actually released. Final quiescence admits neither ordinary jobs nor original-SDK Ended jobs. Their direct registry starts also return an already-cancelled handle and a Cancelled completion, executing no body. Resume opens that gate again. The separate original-resource selector in J2.1.1 continues to accept DisposalOnly work.

Paused admission rejects new original-SDK Ended completion starts even if the original SDK remains valid. This is a new admission rule for that queued selector; the former synchronous activity report did not consult the registry gate, and its behavior is not redefined. Production lifecycle ordering must finish original completion producers before pause, preserving their normal ability to report. Suspend already drains operations through `backgroundAfterLocalFlush` before freeze; freeze waits for handlers, networks and applying work before Rust suspension. Close quiesces the backend and joins original operations/results before backend close. Preserve and verify these orderings. A prior native call alone grants no post-pause admission permission.

Already-registered required completions (`engine/jobs#J2.1.1`) participate in cancellation enumeration but run their bodies despite cancellation. Drain them under the existing deadline before Runtime suspension, Host cleanup or connection release can destroy resources they use. This early drain does not move ordinary participant flushes or change ordinary job ordering. No wait holds a Driver, Runtime, media or ordering lock needed by the completion. A missed drain reports incomplete teardown; successful suspend/close leaves no such work or connection in use. Direct or deliberately late starts take the closed-gate refusal path, not an exception to it.

Before Host cleanup, Runtime suspension or release can destroy needed resources, drain Full completions for original resources that were already registered. Pausing later does not by itself revoke accepted Full mode. Execute their bodies and exact original cleanup despite cancellation. For new calls, original liveness, generation and registry admission determine whether DisposalOnly must latch. Such DisposalOnly calls address the Runtime already retained and perform no provider, tracking, reader, writer or SDK access. Neither mode receives authority from a successor, and resume or reopen cannot upgrade a call. Keep existing paused refusals for ordinary and original-SDK Ended starts.

Otherwise flush participants, close admission, drain accepted ops, quiesce participants, wait for cancelled jobs, close the derived writer connection and every reader, TRUNCATE-checkpoint, save a clean sidecar and close the writer's SQLite connection. Keep the engine, undo history and advisory process lock. The thread registry must report every core thread idle before success. The process event dispatcher may remain registered under owner "events": it only delivers value envelopes and never touches SQLite. Runtime threads are registered atomically with their owner; idle blocking-pool threads are idle, while an active lookup remains busy until its closure ends even after cancellation. Any remaining busy thread makes the attempt fail at its deadline; it cannot silently escape enumeration. Reader leases and participants have deadline-aware waits. Default participants must return promptly; implementations with lengthy work override suspend_until. Deadline failure never claims success or a clean sidecar and leaves admission closed for retry or resume.

Connection release has a separate `release_connections_until` participant hook: quiesce workers first, close engine readers and their attached databases, then release participant-owned connections. In particular, the derived writer must outlive its readers so its final close can remove its WAL. A flush failure closes admission too and remembers that flushing is incomplete; a retry repeats the write-admitting flush phase before final quiescence.

After existing drains and the writer/checkpoint idle wait, atomically establish the final resource cutoff under the job registry mutex. Recheck **both** the running-job map and ThreadRegistry busy state while holding that mutex. Only when both are empty may teardown mark its cutoff there; that mark is the linearization point. A worker removes its job before its pool busy guard ends, so an empty running map alone cannot prove drain. If either recheck fails, release the locks and retry within the existing deadline. Never wait under registry or thread-registry locks, or retain them across Driver, Runtime, reader, writer, provider or callback work. Other maintenance producers remain quiesced; no busy thread is exempted.

Resource registration takes the same registry mutex. Before marking the cutoff, drain all work registered before it, including DisposalOnly and its worker's busy tail. A later explicit call performs new cleanup in memory only, even after generic admission reopens. It cannot use released stores or connections. The original Runtime mutex serializes removal of exact map entries with teardown. It cannot retire a successor. Successful teardown proves drain at the cutoff and closes connections. Original handles retain later cleanup authority over memory only. A deadline before the cutoff means incomplete teardown. Reader close waits for `engine/lifecycle#L11` lifetime pins under the existing reader-lease deadlines too.

## S2. Resume

`resume()` reopens SQLite if suspended and durably marks the sidecar unclean, then performs external-change checks and rollover, in that order, before admitting public ops. It returns ResumeReport { external_change, rolled_over, next_day_at } (epoch seconds). It invokes no sync, queue rebuild or widget work: U151 schedules those. Already-active resume still checks the writer data_version and rollover. Participants resume after the core checks; U26 can then rebuild invalidated indexes. A failed resume never admits writes.

## S3. External change

On change, reset undo/redo with ChangedExternally, discard cached day values and optimistic touched-table assumptions, invalidate U26's entire derived store, and publish a ChangeSet marked whole_collection. Notify in-core observers with the same set before publishing. This is a conservative invalidation, not a generation change. Open uses the prior sidecar mod/scm; before-write checks run after BEGIN IMMEDIATE, so a foreign commit cannot slip between the check and an op's transaction. An op planned before an external change must replan (Stale) rather than overwrite values planned from stale caches. The next op may run against current rows.

A failed reopen retains the original suspended-file stamp for the next resume attempt. Its held file descriptor is released before opening SQLite again, so a later descriptor close cannot drop SQLite's POSIX locks.

## S4. Close and deadlines

Close uses the same participant flush/quiesce, job cancellation and connection drain, then terminates the writer. Timeout errors identify the phase. A caller retries suspend or resumes to recover; no detached helper may keep SQLite work running after a successful suspend. The app must supply its remaining background budget and call again after Deferred completes.

Close cancels continued-processing jobs too and uses a five-second quiescence budget. Cancellation precedes participant flush/quiescence; admission closes and accepted writes drain before participants quiesce. Close retains the collection on quiescence failure so the caller can retry or resume.

S1's closed admission and early drain for already-registered original-SDK Ended completions also apply to close. The five-second budget and incomplete-close retention remain unchanged; required completion grants no old-owner authority or new SDK work.

Close must perform S1's early drain of Full resource completions and its cutoff check of jobs and busy threads. Reopening the generic registry when engine close ends cannot renew apply authority for an older resource. Determine DisposalOnly from the original Host's Prepared/Closing/Closed state and the retained generation and identity, also when replacement has returned Ready. Continue admitting explicit later cleanup of original resources on the existing pool, without connection access. Apply J2.1.1's exact failure for refused apply and its cancellation/result rules. `sync/policy#P24`, `conformance/cloudkit-acceptance#Q11` and final acceptance evidence remain mandatory.
