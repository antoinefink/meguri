## P1. Terms

- **Policy**: the state machine of this file. It takes inputs (P3) and returns actions (P4).
- **Gate**: the conditions under which cloud work may run against a binding (P6).
- **Barrier**: an op on the writer that runs between two ops and writes no collection row (P7.1).
- **Held run** (R): taken at a barrier for the active session. It is the contiguous run of steps at the top of the undo history that are neither uploaded nor upload-pending, down to and including the newest answer of that session among them. Steps of no session above that answer belong to R (R88: "dependent top-of-stack changes"). R is empty when no session is active or no such answer is reached. R is `engine/undo#M8.1`'s `cloud_held_run(session)`; when every step of the run is the session's, it equals M8's `held_back_run(session)`.
- **Detached hold**: the steps of held runs that an incoming change dropped from the undo history during the active session (P8.3). They are no longer undoable, and their rows stay held until the session ends.
- **Rows in R**: the rows of the journals of R's steps and of the detached hold's steps (M8's value). The notes that M1's touched set adds for changed cards are not rows in R.
- **Incoming-held set**: the entities that a `CloudIncoming` or `CloudRekey` op wrote during the active session while one of their rows was a row in R (the detached hold's included), and the new key of such an entity that a `CloudRekey` moved (P8.3). They stay deferred until `SessionEnded`, whatever later barriers find (P7.2).
- **Eligible image**: the image of an entity the policy allows to be sent (P7.2). An entity is **held** when its eligible image differs from its live rows or it is deferred because of R, the detached hold or the incoming-held set.
- **The binding**: K10.2's binding of the running build: the active binding of its container and environment when one exists, otherwise the newest of its bindings by creation instant; a salvage binding is never it (No binding, below). So a binding that a Resume makes active again (V9.2) is the binding, also when a newer binding of another account exists.
- **Image seq**: the collection seq an image represents: S for an image taken from the snapshot, and the seq just before the oldest step that changed one of its rows for a before-image (P7.2).
- **Cloud flush**: a request that the engine sends the collection zone now. Durability flushes of the collection file are "WAL flush" (`storage/open-and-connections#C9.2`); "flushed" has K1's meaning.
- **Exchange**: one Sync Now run (P12).
- **Device settings**: the device-local settings iCloud enabled, Pause (V16, R2046), Sync automatically (R123, "auto" below) and Sync audio and images (R122, "media" below), held in U151's `DeviceSettings` (V2.1).
- **Tracking summary**: the counts that the core computes from tracking after each tracking commit (P3 `TrackingChanged`).
- **Policy token**: a counter in the policy state (P4). It is not an epoch (K1).
- **No binding** (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V11`): no binding in any container or environment other than a V8.1 salvage binding (reason `tracking_damaged`). A salvage binding only holds conflict copies (V17.2). It is never the binding of a P6, P12.1, P14 or P17 row, and it adds no `isolated` item.
- **Local change**: a tracking commit that holds dirty marks (K11.1) made for a local op, that is an op other than `CloudIncoming` or `CloudRekey`, or made from media journal feed items when tracking consumes the feed (K11.1, "When the feed is read"), other than the items that are a media apply's own (K9.3).
- **Parked**: an entity, a blob, a due control record (K12.6) or a due record deletion (P10.3 items 1 and 2) whose last result from the current engine instance was an error after which the engine drops the name from its pending list (K12.4): `quotaExceeded`, an asset error (`pa`, `ma`), a `limitExceeded` for a batch of one record (K12.3), or any other error that K12.4 does not list as kept. A name that K12.4 adds back at once (`batchRequestFailed`, a `limitExceeded` of a larger batch, a `serverRecordChanged` result that left it sendable) and an unknown item (K17.4) are not parked. The core holds the parked marks in memory for the current engine instance; they are not tracking rows, and a relaunch and every engine start (`EngineStarted`, a re-creation by `ConfigureAutomatic` included) drop them. Only `UpdatePending{full}` hands a parked name back (P4); the one that follows every engine start (P4 `StartEngine`, `ConfigureAutomatic`) covers the names whose marks the start dropped.
- **In flight** (decided 2026-10-27): a collection entity, blob or control record whose record the core handed to the current engine instance in a batch, while no per-record result for it has arrived and the send that asked for the batch has not ended. Any per-record result (success or failure) ends it, and so do the end of that send, `StopEngine`, a re-creation by `ConfigureAutomatic` and a relaunch. The core holds these marks in memory, beside the parked marks; U114 reports each send's end to the core before its `EngineActivity{send, ended}`. The durable in-flight list of K10.3 is another thing: it keeps each handed revision for K12.3's in-flight digest row and P12.4's image seq, and it never makes an entity unsendable or keeps its name out of the engine's pending list.

## P2. Layering

- The policy runs in the core, in `core/crates/meguri-sync/src/cloud/policy/`. Its decision function is pure: `step(state, input) → (state, actions)`. It reads only its state (P5) and the input. The current instant is a field of every input. The policy reads no clock, no tracking row and no collection row, and holds no timer. What it needs from tracking reaches it as the tracking summary.
- The CloudKit policy is separate from the manual-transfer storage under `meguri-sync/src/policy/` (frozen, P16). They share no state.
- Session state comes from U43's session registry inside the core (`sched/review-session#C17`: `SessionStarted`, `SessionEnded`). No FFI call can supply or override it.
- The Swift CloudKit actor (U114) owns `CKSyncEngine`, Apple network operations, retry and backoff. The policy never sets a delivery deadline or a CloudKit retry time.
- App timers: U151 holds the only app timer, the flush timer for the foreground cloud flush target (P10.2). Decided 2026-10-27 (plan U113: "No Rust policy pretends it can enforce a delivery interval or CloudKit retry timer"): Meguri holds no retry timer and no quota pause, in the core or in U114. CKSyncEngine owns scheduling and backoff. After a quota or tracking failure the policy acts again only at event triggers: a local change, a foreground return, an account change, Sync Now, or a state change the engine reports (P15.1, P15.2; `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V3.3`, `#V10.3`). Decided 2026-10-27: the state changes the engine reports that count as triggers are an engine start (`StartEngine`'s update), a drained fetch of the bound collection zone (`FetchDrained{collection}`, either `fetched`) and an engine account event (`Account`). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): `FetchDrained{root}` alone is not an event trigger. It runs no full update and no `ApplyReady{retryFailed}` (P10.1); the full update that a root drain needs runs at the `RootConfirmed` that follows its confirmation (P10.1), and an engine-scheduled fetch that changes only `meguri-root` has nothing else to hand back. An `EngineError` or an `EngineActivity` only recomputes the status, so a failure never hands its own names back at once and cannot start a send loop (P4 `UpdatePending`). The core reports a recovery op's due own operations (`RecoveryWriteDue`) again only after these same event triggers, and after a start's account check, a resume of the op, the end of a pause or a tracking stop, the clearing of the publication fence, and each `Network{reachable: true}` that follows `false`, never from a timer (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`, "Due again"). Each of these is one report per transition.
- Deadlines are not timers. Decided 2026-10-27: `SyncCoordinator` holds the deadline of a running operation: the 30 s gate wait of an exchange (P12.3 step 1) and the `until` of a background or quit flush (P11.2, P11.4). The core's waits for a covered seq are deadlines of one operation too: the builder's wait of at most 1 s for the WAL flush to cover S (P7.1) and the tracking update's wait for the seq of an apply (K13.2, K14.2), both `storage/open-and-connections#C9.2`'s wait with a timeout. A deadline only ends the operation it belongs to and ends with it; it never starts, repeats or delays cloud work. There is no other timed wait: an exchange never waits out a retry-after (P12.5), and a retry of a record read (an inbox entry with reason `asset`, which counts its attempts under K13.1, or a blob read, P13.2) runs only at an event trigger. Nothing polls (P3 `VolumeSpaceAvailable`).
- `SyncCoordinator` (U151) executes each action, then reports the outcome as an input. It calls `sendChanges` and `fetchChanges` only from its own tasks, never from inside the engine's event handling (R2048, KTD25).

### P2.1 Internal Core execution

Policy projections and calls that can wait behind them follow `engine/jobs#J2.1.1`. That contract preserves typed results, original ownership, cancellation, drain and synchronous final enqueue validation. The policy's inputs, actions, guards, status and exchange semantics remain unchanged.

- **The start of a fetch call** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4; `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.2`, "Which value the mark records"). ## P4. Actions

Every action carries a guard: the lifecycle generation, the binding id and the policy token. The token increases on every `StopEngine`, on every account change that changes the account status or identity (P3 `Account`), and on every `Binding` input (except the step changes below), replacement and close. It does not increase on `StartEngine` or `ConfigureAutomatic` (decided 2026-10-27): starting an engine or changing its scheduling changes no binding and no account, so a running exchange and the pending update that follows `StartEngine` keep a current guard. Decided 2026-10-27: nor does it increase on a `Binding` input that only moves a `publish` op's step to `zones`, `sent`, `complete` or `done` (V15.2) for the binding the policy holds, with the same binding id and state. Such an input changes neither the binding nor the account, so an exchange that waits at P12.3 step 1 during a publication, or runs while the step changes, keeps a current guard. Every other `Binding` input increases it: one that creates, isolates, resumes or deletes a binding, and a step change to `cancelling` (V15.3). Each core call that `SyncCoordinator` makes for an action passes the guard. A call whose guard is not current fails with `StaleGuard` and changes nothing.

Facts from an engine (save results, fetched records, state serializations, account events) also carry V3.4's instance id. The core accepts a fact only from the current instance in P5 (`EngineStarted`). Together, the guard and the instance id make a late callback of a stopped engine, of an engine recreated by `ConfigureAutomatic`, or of another account have no effect (U114 test). An exchange uses the engine instance that is current at each of its steps, not the one current at `RunExchange` (P12.3).

## P7. The eligibility frontier

### P7.1 The barrier

Every batch of collection-zone records is built from one barrier, whichever path asked for it: automatic engine scheduling, a cloud flush, an exchange, a background or quit flush, or a retry after a restart. The batch request names the record names the batch may hold. On the writer, before the next op is taken, the barrier:

1. begins a read transaction on the cloud reader and runs one read, fixing the snapshot. The builder leases the cloud reader off the writer and submits the barrier with the lease, so the writer never waits for it (`engine/lifecycle#L11`). The cloud reader is a reader role of its own, so a manual transfer's long snapshot on the sync reader never blocks it (P23);
2. records S, the seq of the last committed op (`engine/op-model` O4);
3. takes R for the active session (P1) and the detached hold (P8.3). This reads no row image;
4. flags undo steps as P8.2 says.

Then, off the writer, the builder waits until the WAL flush covers S (`storage/open-and-connections#C9.2`). If S is not covered within 1 s, the batch returns no record. The entities stay pending, and the next request builds again (K12.1 rule 4). So every image comes from flushed commits. Decided 2026-10-27: no input reports a missed wait, and the policy asks for no send because of it. The names stay in the engine's pending list, and the next request is the engine's own scheduling, the flush timer that the local change armed (P10.2), or the send of the next event trigger. A slow flush can so delay a change past the 60 s target of P10.2; nothing is lost. Several batch requests made together may share one barrier.

### P7.2 Eligible images

For each pending entity (K1) named by the request, the builder computes its eligible image E row by row:

| The row at the snapshot | Its image in E |
|---|---|
| not a row in R | the snapshot's row |
| a row in R, with a before-image | the before-image from the oldest step (of R or the detached hold) that changed it |
| a row in R that R or the detached hold created | absent |

| E | Sendable (K12.1 rule 2) |
|---|---|
| every row from the snapshot | when E's row digest differs from the acked row digest. The image seq is S |
| some row from a before-image, or absent | held. When E has a row: sendable when E's row digest differs from the acked row digest, otherwise deferred. The image seq is the seq just before the oldest step that changed one of its rows |
| no row, because R or the detached hold created every row | held and deferred: no record, no tombstone |
| the rows R deleted, from their before-images | held; sent live as row 2, with no tombstone |

- "Deferred" keeps the entity pending and not handed to the engine. K12.4 removes it from the engine's pending changes.
- A deferral holds only until the next `UpdatePending{full}` (decided 2026-10-27), except a deferral of the incoming-held set, which holds until `SessionEnded` (below). A barrier decides only the names its request names, and the engine asks only for names it holds, so a deferred entity would otherwise never be decided again when R moves. R moves only through a local op (a new answer, an undo, an edit after the answer) or the session's end, and each runs a full update (P10.1, P9). The full update clears every deferral and hands the names back; the next barrier decides them with the R of that time: a card answered before the newest answer is then below R and is sent live. A deferral is held in memory by the core, with the barrier that set it; a relaunch drops it, as it drops R (P8.4). An entity whose deferral was cleared counts as `sendable` until a barrier decides it (P3); the cost is one extra barrier decision per full update.
- An entity named by an apply or re-key intent in progress (`applyInProgress`) is not sendable until the intent settles (K13.2, K14.2).
- An entity of the incoming-held set (P1, P8.3) is held and deferred at every barrier until `SessionEnded`, with no record and no tombstone, whatever its image digest and whatever the tables above give (decided 2026-10-27). Its images can no longer be taken from the journals: the incoming change made its live rows and its acked digest newer than every before-image of R or the detached hold, so such an image would differ from the acked one, count as sendable and overwrite the other device's version with a stale one; and after a re-key the journals name only the old key, so the new key's live rows would carry the held step's effect (P20 invariants 2 and 14). The full update does not clear this deferral (P4), so the engine is not asked for the entity; a barrier asked for it anyway defers it again. At `SessionEnded` the set is dropped and the entity is sent live (P9). The op's own local write always uses L = the live row (K13.2, K12.3); a held image is never L.
- An entity whose E references an entity that is deferred is held by K12.1 rule 5. Example: a card answered in R keeps its earlier image, and the review-log row R added is absent, so nothing references it.
- Media-name entities come from the media journal, not from collection rows, so R never holds them (K9.4). A note held by R that references a new media name holds nothing else back; the name is sent alone (K12.1 rule 6 applies only to the note).
- A held card image carries the `last_review_id` of E, not of the live rows (K6.1): a review-log row that R or the detached hold created is left out of it, and one they deleted counts from its before-image. So a held image describes one history, and it never wins K15.3's scheduling rule with a review it does not carry.
- The builder records with each in-flight entry its image seq and whether the image is held (P12.4, P23).

|---|---|
| 30 answers; R = the newest answer | 29 cards, 29 review rows, the deck as after answer 29 |
| answer of #c1, answer of #c2, then a flag on #c2; R = the two newest steps | #c1 and its review row; the deck as after answer #c1 |
| answer of #c1, then Delete Note of its note; R = both | nothing of the note, its cards or the review row |

| answer of #c1, then an incoming change to #c1 drops R (P8.3) | nothing of #c1; the other rows of the dropped run as their before-images, until the session ends |

| no session | every pending entity, live |

## P8. Hold-back during a review session (R2047, R88, R726)

### P8.1 Scope

- While a session is active, every record built for the collection zone follows P7. No path skips the barrier: there is no "send live rows" route, also not for the background, quit or retry paths. A record name that the engine saved in its pending list before a restart is built with the R of the batch time (K12.4).
- Committed changes below R keep syncing during the session (R1550). A change made outside the session after its newest answer, such as a Browse edit on Mac, is part of R until the next answer of the session or the session's end. Only committed changes enter tracking; an open editor draft is not in the collection.
- Hold-back adds no latency to answers: answers commit through the executor and never wait for a barrier (R2006).

### P8.2 Undo steps and hand-off

- At the barrier, on the writer, every step with a seq at or below S that is not in R, is not yet flagged, and whose touched set (`engine/undo` M1) holds a row of an entity the batch request names, becomes `UploadPending`. No undo can run between the snapshot and the flags (M9.1's rule). The flags stay when the batch then returns no record or the save fails. Redo steps at or below S whose touched set holds a row of a named entity are dropped (`engine/undo#M9.6`).
- A flagged step becomes `Uploaded` once every entity of its touched set that the batch named is acked or clean.
- Neither state is undoable (M4.1), whatever the save result: a save whose result is lost may have landed (K12.3).
- R's steps are never flagged, so the newest answer stays undoable (R365). The detached hold's steps are no longer in the history (P8.3).
- For CloudKit this replaces M9.1's "every other step up to the snapshot" with the steps that meet the request, and replaces M9.2 and M9.3 (`finish` sent, failure before `finish`), which stay for manual transfers. `engine/undo#M9.6` and `#M9.7` give the flags and the acknowledgement.

### P8.3 Incoming changes and undo (R2047)

- A `CloudIncoming` or `CloudRekey` op reports its targets: every key its journal changed, a grave mapped to the key of the card, note or deck it deletes (as M9.4 does), `col` left out. When the op commits, on the writer:
  - the history is cut below the newest step whose touched set holds a target: that step and every older step are dropped, as in M7 (`engine/undo#M9.8`). Steps newer than it stay;
  - redo steps are cut the same way: the redo step nearest the top whose touched set holds a target, and every redo step below it (M9.8);
  - R is dropped whole when a target is in the touched set of any of its steps, and the cut then reaches every older step too. M9.4's first rule ("every step up to the snapshot is dropped") does not apply to CloudKit.
- A dropped R joins the detached hold until `SessionEnded`. Its steps are not undoable any more, but the rows of their journals stay rows in R, and the entities the op did not write keep the before-images P7.2 gives. M8's value keeps the journals while the policy holds it.
- Every target of the op that has a row in R or in the detached hold joins the incoming-held set (P1), whether or not this op dropped R, and so does the new key of such a target that a `CloudRekey` moved (decided 2026-10-27). The set stays until `SessionEnded`; P7.2 defers its entities at every barrier, and no full update hands them back. A target with no row in R or the detached hold (for example a note that only M1's touched set adds for a changed card) does not join it.
- The next barrier takes R again from the remaining history. Its rows and the detached hold's together decide P7.2's images; the oldest before-image of a row wins.
- Undo then reports what M3 gives for the remaining history; there is no new reason.

### P8.4 Lifetime

- R, the detached hold and the incoming-held set exist only while the session is active.
- Background with the session open keeps them: suspend keeps the undo history (`engine/suspend` S1), and the background flush sends under P7.
- Close, a crash, an OS termination and `CollectionReplaced` drop the history (`engine/undo` M6), the detached hold and the incoming-held set, and end the session. After a relaunch nothing earlier is held: the lost session's newest answer is sent like any change.
- On Mac quit the session is ended before the quit flush (P11.4, KTD6), so the newest answer is sent if the flush gets the time.

## P9. Release at session end (R1549)

`SessionEnded` (`sched/review-session#C16`) ends hold-back: the core drops the detached hold and the incoming-held set, the next barrier's R is empty, and every pending entity's live image is eligible. The full update below then clears every deferral (P7.2), so each deferred entity is handed back and sent live.

| open | on | `CancelFlushTimer`, `UpdatePending{full}`, `RequestSend{collection}` when an entity is sendable |
| open | off | `UpdatePending{full}` |

`startSession` while a session is active ends the old session first (C1): `SessionEnded` then `SessionStarted` arrive in that order, and the row above applies between them.

So after a relaunch with an unchanged root zone, which is the normal case, the root fetch of P10.1's opening rows confirms the epoch and the device sends.

A fetch that the engine schedules itself reports no `FetchDrained` for a root zone with no changes (P3), so when the opening `RequestFetch` fails (offline, throttled), or its root drain cannot confirm and the `RootReadDue` read then fails, nothing else confirms the root of that instance. So while the bound epoch is not confirmed for the current instance (P5), with the gate open and auto on, and the binding is not a `publish` op's new binding before `done` (which needs no confirmation), each of these inputs also emits `RequestFetch{root}`, when no root fetch runs (P5 engine activity): `Network{reachable: true}` after `false`, a `TrackingChanged{cause: local}`, and a `FetchDrained{collection}`. The drain it gives makes the core confirm the epoch, or make the record-ID read again when the binding records no `MGActive` (above), and a confirmation runs the table's `RootConfirmed` row, which sends. Each is an existing event trigger, and a fetch or a read that fails emits nothing, so this cannot loop.

### P10.2 The foreground cloud flush target

- R88's target: in the foreground with auto on, an eligible change waits at most about 60 s before the app asks the engine to send it. The engine may send it earlier on its own schedule.
- The timer is armed at the first `TrackingChanged{cause: local}` that finds a sendable entity while no timer is armed. When it fires, one `RequestSend` follows, with no `UpdatePending`; the next local change arms it again. It is not armed while nothing is sendable, and never by a commit that is not a local change. It is the upper bound of R88's target: with auto on, the engine also schedules a send of its own whenever an update adds names to its pending list (P10.1), so most changes go earlier. After `quotaExceeded` it is armed as usual: it follows a local change, one of P15.1's event triggers, and is not a retry timer. It never hands a parked name back: only the local change's own full update did (P4).
- The target applies to the whole foreground, not only during a session. During a session it sends what P7 makes eligible.
- It is a request time, not a delivery deadline (R88: no fixed completion deadline). The policy never repeats a send because the engine did not finish.

### P10.3 Cleanup (K9.5, K17.4, K18.5, V17.3)

Decided 2026-10-27: the time-based deletions of the CloudKit specs run from one core action, `RunCleanup`, at a drained fetch. No timer runs them (P2).

- **When.** At `FetchDrained{collection}` while the gate is open and the current engine instance has confirmed the bound epoch (P5, K2 "Confirmed root"): the first such input in this process, and then the first one at least 24 h after the last `RunCleanup` (P5 holds that instant; the input carries now). At most once per drain. A drained fetch of the bound collection zone, with `fetched` true or false (P3), is the "completed a fetch" that K9.5 needs. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 3): an open gate does not include the root confirmation (K12.1 rule 3 makes it a condition of each instance), and K9.5, K17.4 and K18.5 need a device bound to the active epoch, so items 1 to 3 wait for it. A collection drain that comes before the confirmation (an engine-scheduled fetch gives no order between the zones, P3; a binding waiting for its `RootReadDue` result) runs no `RunCleanup`, and the first one after it does. A `publish` op's new binding before `done` is never confirmed, so it runs none.
- **What it computes**, in one tracking transaction, from tracking only:
  1. tombstone purges (K17.4): acked tombstones whose `deleted_at_ms` is more than 180 days before now, only while A5 and A9b hold (K17.4, K23; A9b is taken as false, so purging stays off until the signed runs show both hold). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4, K17.4): while purging is off, a completed absence pass makes no `deletion` entry either: no Meguri build deletes an `MGEntity` record from a bound zone then, so every absence it finds is a false one (A9a is not proven); it logs the count and record names for Copy Debug Info. Turning purging on also needs K17.4's record-ID read of each absent entity's `e`, returning unknown item, before its `deletion` entry;
  2. blob deletions (K9.5): blobs that meet every K9.5 condition. None while media is off, while `mediaZoneMissing` is set, or while another epoch zone is retained. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4, K9.5): those conditions include the inbox and the intents, since a fetched name waits in the inbox before it applies and some never apply: no blob is marked whose content an inbox `record` entry of the binding (any reason, any source) or a `refused` row names as a media-name version, while any inbox entry or `refused` row holds a version whose kind or content this build cannot read (a newer format, a payload in an asset not yet read, a refused envelope), or while a media apply intent or an apply intent with a media-name `entry` key exists. The batch builder checks the conditions that a later fetch can change (no name in K9.5's sense, no such inbox entry or `refused` row, no such intent) again when it builds the batch, and skips the deletion when one fails (K9.5, "Fresh deletions"). So a `RunCleanup` that runs before the drain's `ApplyReady` has applied a fetched name never deletes that name's blob, and the order of P10.1's `FetchDrained{collection}` row needs no change for this. Decided 2026-10-27 (K9.5, "Fresh deletions"): the mark records the instant of the drained fetch whose `RunCleanup` computed it, and the deletion is due only while the device clock reads at least that instant and less than 1 h after it. The batch builder skips it otherwise, and the next update clears the mark; a later `RunCleanup` computes it again from a newer fetch;
  3. zone deletions (K18.5): the previous epoch's zone 30 days after the later of the switch (`MGActive.activated_at_ms`) and its `zones` row's first-seen instant (K10.3; decided 2026-10-27, review round 3: the switch instant is the publisher's clock, so a slow publisher clock never shortens the 30 days), and a never-active collection zone whose age passes K18.5's 30 days. A cancelled publication's zone needs no computation (decided 2026-10-27): the V15.3 cancel commit gives the binding before a `zones` row for it with deletion due, and that mark's condition holds until the deletion's result, so nothing clears it and the first `DeleteZones` of that binding deletes the zone (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4: no update hands a zone deletion to the engine, P4). The same holds for a collection zone that survived an encrypted data reset unreadable, which the publisher's binding marks due after its switch (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.1`, V26.1);
  4. resolved conflict copies 30 days after resolution (V17.3), of every binding in the tracking file, isolated and salvage bindings included (V17.2). This is a tracking deletion only; nothing goes to CloudKit. The staged file a media copy holds (K16) is deleted after that commit (V6). Decided 2026-10-27: item 4 needs no gate, so the core also runs it alone at each collection open, after V2.4 step 6, whether or not the gate ever opens; so the resolved copies of a local-only device and of a salvage binding are deleted on time too. The same commit deletes the binding row of an isolated binding that holds no work row (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V17.6`: its work was deleted by a recovery op, or it is a salvage binding; review of the P24 corrections), once this commit deletes its last conflict copy (P24 review; scoped in the review of the P24 corrections). No other binding row is deleted here: an active binding, or an isolated one that still holds its work, keeps its row with no copy.
- It marks items 1 to 3 due in tracking. The `UpdatePending{full}` that follows hands items 1 and 2 to the engine as record deletions, with the normal sends. A send carries only what lies in its scope (K12.1, "Send scope"), and with auto off the engine sends nothing by itself, so Sync Now sends them too (decided 2026-10-27): step 7's `ResumeMedia` sends the due blob deletions (P13.1 step 4). A deletion result drops the tombstoned entity (K17.4) or sets the blob's server state `deleted`. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): unknown item on such a deletion is a deletion result too (another device deleted the record first): the entity is dropped, or the blob's server state becomes `deleted`. A failed deletion stays due. When it failed with an error the engine keeps (K12.4), the engine retries it; otherwise it is parked (P1), and the next full update at an event trigger hands it back. A due mark whose condition no longer holds at an update (a new name references the blob, the binding changed, or 1 h has passed since the drain that computed a blob deletion, item 2) is cleared without a deletion.
- **Zone deletions** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4; K18.5). Item 3's deletions are never engine work: the engine sends a pending database change by itself, with no batch request, so neither the gate (P6), nor a send scope, nor the root confirmation (K12.1 rule 3), nor a Pause could hold one back, and the engine would keep one that failed for the network, also in its saved state, and send it on reconnect before any root fetch. So `DeleteZones` (P4) runs them as an own operation (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), from the `FetchDrained{collection}` row of P10.1 (the drain whose `RunCleanup` marked them, or a later drain while one is due), and so from Sync Now's fetch steps too, with auto on or off (P12.3). Each run reads `MGActive` first and deletes a zone only when that read names the bound epoch: a zone that `MGActive` names, for example a never-active zone whose publisher switched to it while this device was offline, is never deleted, and a device that is no longer bound to the active epoch deletes nothing. The read is newer than any drain, so a zone deletion has no 1 h window of its own (item 2's window exists because no single record shows a blob's references). Its guard (P4) is checked by `zone_deletion_check`, so a run whose guard an input that closes the gate made stale (Pause, an account change, a stop) deletes nothing; the deletion starts at once after that check returns. Results (`zone_deletion_result`): deleted, or zone not found (another device deleted it first, which is the normal case, since every device bound to the active epoch computes the same deletions), deletes that zone's `zones` row, its due mark included, in one tracking transaction; a zone-not-found result that carries the encrypted-data-reset flag is also handled as K12.5's first row (P24 review). Any other error leaves the mark due; the next `DeleteZones` tries it again. A zone that the read did not allow to delete stays due as well; its condition is read again at the next run. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4, K12.5): a fetched zone deletion of a `c-` zone other than the bound one, whatever its reason, deletes that zone's `zones` row, its due mark included, in the K13.1 step 2 transaction that records the fetch. So the row of a zone another device deleted never stays, and K9.5's "no other epoch zone is retained" becomes true again once each retained zone is gone (P23).
- It runs no exchange step, and the results of the deletions an exchange sends do not change that exchange's result. Nothing in it deletes local data other than resolved conflict copies, and nothing recreates a zone.

## P11. Foreground, background and quit

### P11.1 Foreground return (U151, KTD15)

U151 runs, strictly in this order: `resume()` (`engine/suspend` S2) → the review queue refresh → the `Foreground` input → the actions it returns. During a tracking stop, `Foreground` first emits `ProbeTracking` (P15.2).

| Condition | Actions |
|---|---|
| gate open, auto on | `UpdatePending{full}`; `RequestFetch{root, collection}`; `RequestSend{collection}` when sendable; `ResumeMedia` when media is on (its read round, P13.2; the download queue starts after `FetchDrained{collection}`, P13.1) |
| gate open, auto off | `UpdatePending{full}`, which keeps the engine's list current and sends nothing (P6) |
| an engine runs, the gate is closed only for a scan or an unsettled intent, auto on | `RequestFetch{root, collection}`; fetched records go to the inbox (P6) |
| otherwise | none |

An external change found by `resume()` sets the scan-required flag (K11.4), so the gate closes until the scan ends. When the scan ends, the gate opens and P10.1's opening row sends.

### P11.2 Background (U151, KTD15, R1552, R1554)

U151 runs, in this order: review pause and session record → the editor's `flush()` → `flush_for_background()` (`engine/suspend` S1) → the `Background{deadline}` input and its actions, inside U154's background-task assertion → `suspend(deadline)`.

| Condition | Actions |
|---|---|
| an exchange runs | `CancelFlushTimer`, `BackgroundFlush{until}`. The exchange continues until `until`, then ends `cancelled` (P12.6) |
| gate open, auto on, and an entity is sendable, an apply is in progress or a ready inbox entry waits | `CancelFlushTimer`, `BackgroundFlush{until}` |
| otherwise | `CancelFlushTimer` |

- `until` is the earlier of `deadline` − 5 s and now + 25 s.
- The background flush: settle ready inbox entries and intents in progress (K13.2), run `UpdatePending{full}` (so names parked since the last trigger go too), then send the collection zone under P7 (hold-back applies when a session is open), await the results, and persist the engine state. It fetches nothing new and starts no media transfer. Media transfers already running continue under the engine's own scheduling.
- At `until`, or on `BackgroundExpiring`, the flush and any exchange are cancelled: records without a result stay pending and keep their durable in-flight entries (K12.3), stop being in flight when the send ends (P1), and are handed again by the next update; acked records stay acked (R1554). Then U151 calls `suspend`.
- From `suspend` until the next `resume()`, the engine instance stays, but nothing reaches the suspended core: a batch request returns no record, and every other engine event (save results, fetched records and deletions, account events, state updates) waits in the actor, unsaved, in the order the engine delivered it (decided 2026-10-27, review round 3). So at `resume()` the core gets a send's results before the state that reflects them (A6) and fetched records before the state that advances past them (A7). If the process ends first, the cursor is older and they are fetched again (V5 R1), and an unsaved save result leaves its entity pending with its in-flight entry (V3.4).
- Assets of a waiting event (decided 2026-10-27). The system may delete a fetched asset's file from its own staging area once the handler returns. So while it handles each event that carries fetched records, and before it returns, the actor moves (or clones) every asset file of the event (`pa`, `ma`) into the cloud staging directory, also while the core is suspended, and queues the event with the staged paths. This is a file operation, not a tracking fact: the core inserts the inbox rows at `resume()` (K13.1 step 2, with the file already in place), and the start sweep of V6 deletes a staged file that no committed inbox row names, for example after the process ended first. Cloud work that the system starts in the background (P11.3) reaches the core only after U151 has run `resume()`; U154 owns that wake path.
- The next `Foreground` or `Lifecycle{Ready}` sends what is left.

### P11.3 Opportunistic background work (R1553)

The policy registers no background task. The engine's automatic scheduling and remote notifications run cloud work when the system allows. U154 owns every registration; no background task starts an exchange or an Anki transfer.

### P11.4 Mac quit (R127, R1557)

U175 ends an open session first (`sched/review-session#C16`), then U151 sends `QuitRequested`.

| Condition | Actions |
|---|---|
| an exchange runs | `QuitFlush{until: now + 10 s}`, whatever auto says. The exchange continues until `until`, then ends `cancelled` |
| gate open, auto on, and an entity is sendable, an apply is in progress or a ready inbox entry waits | `QuitFlush{until: now + 10 s}` |
| otherwise | `QuitNow` |

- The quit flush works as the background flush (P11.2). U161's sheet shows it with "Quit Without Syncing", which cancels it and any exchange.
- At `until` or on cancel the app quits; pending work stays durable and is not called synced.

### P12.2 The boundary

At P12.3 step 2:

- On the writer, a barrier op records S_c, the seq of the last committed op.
- The exchange waits until the tracking observer has processed every op up to S_c (K11.1).
- Then tracking consumes the media journal's feed up to its current counter (K11.1, "When the feed is read"), and its commit lands before the read below. So a removal or a new file made before the press is selected, also when the store's feed hook (`media/media-files#M16`) has not yet run for it.
- Then, in one tracking read, it reads B, the binding's `change_seq` counter value (K10.3), and selects every entity that is pending with `change_seq` ≤ B, and every pending entity with no `change_seq`. Entities that are not pending for the policy (P3: `file_missing`, `withheld`, `superseded`) are never selected; their media state is the result's `media` field.

So every change committed before the capture is selected, a change the reconcile scan of step 1 found included (R2048). P12.1 row 2 prevents accepting a new request during a review session. A session starting after an available request emits `RunExchange` does not stop its preparation, gate wait or later capture. Capture still requires durable accepted epoch `crt` equal to `col.crt` and the full P6 gate, and follows the S_c/B sequence above. P6’s existing publication path with its own acknowledged `publishing` epoch remains unchanged. Selected entities may already be held. Batches follow P7 with the current R; P12.4’s image-seq and result rules remain unchanged. Preparation applies no collection records. Existing guards, cancellation and undo rules remain in force. Changes after the capture are not selected. A selected entity is satisfied by the ack of any image with an image seq at or above S_c (P12.4), whether or not it was edited again.

**The acked set** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4). Tracking deletes an acked in-flight entry, and every older one, at Saved and at the K13.2 settle (K10.3, K12.3), so step 6 cannot read such an ack back from tracking. So from the capture until the exchange ends, the core keeps in memory, with the capture, the set of selected entities for which a tracking commit recorded, while the exchange ran, the ack of an in-flight entry whose image seq is at or above S_c: a Saved result or the in-flight digest row (K12.3), or an own landed save (K13.2). With each it keeps whether that image was held. Acks through any engine instance that is current during the exchange count, and so do acks of sends the exchange did not ask for. P12.4's first row reads this set. It is dropped when the exchange ends; a crash loses it with the exchange (P12.7).

### P12.3 Steps

All steps run from `SyncCoordinator`'s task, outside the engine's event handling. Each step awaits the durable persistence of the engine's event handler (K12.3, K13.1) before the next. Each step uses the engine instance current when the step starts (P4); an engine started or recreated while the exchange waits does not end it.

**Epoch crt preparation.** Before step 1, an otherwise available Sync Now request blocked solely by unavailable authoritative epoch `crt` performs one verification read (V26.1, "Sync Now epoch verification"). It runs with automatic sync on or off. Every other P6 condition must hold, and no apply may run. If needed, start the binding's instance with the current automatic setting. Do not capture a boundary, send or apply collection records before accepted authority commits and the full gate opens. This preparation makes no SDK fetch solely to qualify the read. It adds no retry or deadline. A durable accepted equal value continues at step 1; a valid unequal value ends `failed{conflictRecovery}`. Apply P12.6's existing failure and cancellation precedence to errors and changed guards. P15 errors with an existing failed result end `failed{class}`; all other read errors end `waiting{class, retryAfter?}`, using P15's class and reported retry-after. A result that leaves authority unavailable without a classified error ends `waiting{class: none}`. Missing epoch or zone and K20 refusals use V26.1's outcomes. These early results preserve pending work and record only an attempt (P12.7). The verification creates no fetch drain, confirmed-root mark, completed exchange or quiet point.

1. **Gate.** Wait until the gate (P6) is open, an engine instance runs for the binding (not a stopping one, P5), and no reconcile scan or apply runs, at most 30 s (a deadline of the exchange, P2). A scan that ends, a probe that succeeds or an engine that starts during the wait lets the exchange go on (actions of later inputs run during the wait, P4). The wait goes on while the gate is closed only for `ExclusiveBusy`, a scan, an unsettled intent, a `publish` op step before `zones` (P6), or a tracking stop whose `ProbeTracking`, emitted with this exchange (P12.1), has not yet been answered by its `TrackingChanged` or `TrackingWriteFailed` (decided 2026-10-27); it ends after 30 s. The exchange ends at once when the gate is closed for any other reason, a failed probe included (P12.1). P12.6 gives the result.
2. **Capture** the boundary (P12.2).
3. **Fetch** the root zone, then the collection zone, until each drains. Wait for the record-ID reads that the drain started for entries with reason `asset` (P4 `RetryAssetEntries`). Apply every ready inbox entry (K13.2), and, since Sync Now is an event trigger, retry the entries with reason `apply_failed` once (`ApplyReady{retryFailed}`, P4); wait until the applies are flushed and tracking is updated. Decided 2026-10-27: the step waits only for drains its `RequestFetch` (P4) can give. The root zone counts as drained also at a `RootConfirmed` of the current instance (P3). When `RequestFetch` has returned (after its second call, when the first proved no read, P3) and a zone of its scope has no drain, the zone is not drained and the exchange ends `waiting` after the results in hand persist (P12.6): with class `noAccount` when the engine skipped both calls (the SDK headers name no signed-in account as the reason it skips one); otherwise, for a zone that P3's no-state check holds back, once that check's record-ID read, if one is made, has returned: with the class P15 gives its error, or with class none (P15) when no read is made (a `publish` binding before `done`). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): a read that finds the zone's record gives the zone a `FetchDrained{zone, fetched: false}` for that call (P3), so it drains; one that returns unknown item or zone not found isolates as P3 and K12.5 say, and the exchange ends `failed` (P12.6).
4. **Send** the collection zone. After step 3's applies, run `UpdatePending{full}` (K12.4), so names the engine dropped after an earlier failure (parked, P1) and deferrals are handed back; then call `sendChanges`, and await its return, the persistence of its results, and U114's first read of each save-conflict entry of this send whose payload is in an asset (K12.3, P4 `RetryAssetEntries`), and the deferred adds of its events (K12.4). Decided 2026-10-27 (review of the P24 corrections): when those adds handed back a name that this call had handed (K12.4 "Failed saves": `batchRequestFailed`, or `limitExceeded` in a batch of more than one record), call `sendChanges` again and await it the same way, at most 8 calls in one step (K12.3: at most 7 halvings before a batch holds one record). The engine sends its whole pending list. Entities changed after the capture may be sent with the selection; they are not selected, so the result does not wait for them, and those without an ack stay pending (R2048). A selected entity with no result when the exchange ends counts as outstanding (P12.4), whether or not its record was handed to the engine in a batch: the step waits only for `sendChanges` to return, never for an entity the engine was not handed (decided 2026-10-27). A server-record-changed result merges under K12.3; a merge result that differs from the server version is sent again in the next round. Removed (2026-10-27, review round 4): the step's `RequestSend{zoneDeletions}`. The due zone deletions run from the `FetchDrained{collection}` of step 3 (`DeleteZones`, P10.1, P10.3), with auto on or off; the exchange does not wait for them, and their results do not change its result.
5. **Fetch** the root and collection zones again and apply, as step 3. If this makes a selected entity pending again, run step 4 again, then step 5.
6. **Account** for the selection (P12.4). The core returns the result; `SyncCoordinator` reports `ExchangeEnded{result}`, and the policy emits `RecordExchange{result}` (P12.7, which also covers an exchange that stops before this step).
7. **Media**, when media is on: `ResumeMedia` (P13.1), whether or not media already runs: its read round finds blobs to upload and blobs another device uploaded, and its `RequestSend{media}` sends the blobs that are ready, also with auto off. Its uploads and downloads continue after the exchange ends, under P13.2 and P13.3. The exchange's collection result does not wait for them.

A session that starts during an exchange does not stop it. Every batch follows P7 with the new R. Incoming applies continue.

|---|---|
| `ready` | must be applied durably |
| `generation`, `reference`, `structure` | waits for another device's work; does not prevent `succeeded`; counted in `incomingWaiting` |
| `format`, `asset` | prevents `succeeded`; counted as blocked incoming |
| `apply_failed` (K13.2, an entry fetched in these steps or before) | prevents `succeeded`; counted as blocked incoming. Step 3's apply retries it once, as an event trigger does (P4 `ApplyReady`) |

- The collection result is `succeeded` only when every selected entity is done, every ready entry is applied durably, no entry waits with reason `format`, `asset` or `apply_failed`, and the engine state is persisted.

### P12.5 Bounds

| Bound | Value | When reached |
|---|---|---|
| waiting for the gate (step 1) | 30 s | the exchange ends `waiting` |
| `fetchChanges` calls of one `RequestFetch` that prove no read (P3, P4) | 2 | the zone is not drained; the exchange ends `waiting` (step 3) |
| send rounds (step 4 runs) | 3 | the remaining selected entities count as outstanding |
| waits for a retry-after | Removed (2026-10-27) | a throttled result ends the exchange `waiting` at once |
| conflict merges of one entity | 3 | that entity is outstanding |

- Removed (2026-10-27): the exchange's waits for a retry-after. Decided 2026-10-27 (P2, V10.3): when a step gets `throttled{retryAfter}`, the exchange ends `waiting{throttled, retryAfter}` after the results in hand persist. It never sends again itself; the engine honours the retry-after, and status shows the retry time.
- There is no overall time limit. Every step either makes progress or reaches a bound, so the exchange cannot loop under continual edits or throttling (KTD25).

### P12.6 Results

| Result | When | Pending work |
|---|---|---|
| `succeeded{heldBack, newerPending, incomingWaiting, media}` | P12.4 | kept: entities edited after the capture, held entities, media |
| `incomplete{outstanding, blocked}` | some selected entity is outstanding or blocked, or an entry waits for `format`, `asset` or `apply_failed` (P12.4) | kept |
| `waiting{class, retryAfter?}` | offline, throttled (P12.5), a transient authentication error from an engine operation (P15), the gate still closed after 30 s (step 1), or a fetch step that left a zone not drained (step 3) | kept |
| `failed{class}` | account change, an account status of no account or setup needed, quota full, local storage full or a tracking error, zone deleted, encrypted data reset, an epoch switch, a `crt` mismatch (P6), unsupported schema (P15) | kept; isolation per `sync/cloudkit-recovery` |
| `cancelled` | `SyncNowCancelled`, close, `CollectionReplaced`, the background or quit `until`, a guard that became stale for another reason | kept; acked records stay acked |

- When several rows apply, the first of `failed`, `cancelled`, `waiting`, `incomplete`, `succeeded` is the result. A guard that became stale through an account or binding change gives `failed`.
- `media` is one of `done`, `pending{files, bytes}`, `paused` (R2050). A collection result never says media is done when it is not.
- Success means this device finished its requested exchange. The result text never says that other devices have the changes (R2050).
- Cancel stops after the engine operation in progress returns and its results persist.
- An `incomplete` result sets the attention item `exchangeIncomplete` (P14.4) until a later exchange succeeds or every entity it counted is done (AE91).

### P12.6.1 Retaining exchange-incomplete obligations

An accepted `incomplete` result records work still to finish, per binding (K10.2). Keep durable facts about that work until a rule below clears it. These are facts about an ended exchange. The running capture and selection still live in memory (P12.7).

- **Outgoing:** keep each selected entity that P12.4 counted as outstanding or blocked, and the facts needed to check its original boundary. P12.4's done row decides when this work is done. An ack accepted after the exchange ended counts when it meets that boundary. Once done, that work stays done through newer edits, including held edits. A later incomplete can count those newer edits as new work. Tombstones and dropped unsent entities follow P12.4. Local row absence alone proves neither case.
- **Incoming:** keep the identity and version of each `ready`, `format`, `asset` or `apply_failed` entry that prevented success under P12.4. Include ready entries absent from older result counts. For this item only, that entry is done when tracking durably records its apply, or K13's accepted newer version or deletion that settles the original version. It is also done when tracking durably changes that same entry's reason to `generation`, `reference` or `structure`. A newer body in an unsupported format does not count. Another entry with the same record name does not count unless K13 records that the original version was settled.

Other tracking facts keep their own rules. This does not clear `incomingWaiting`, reference or generation attention, or unsupported-schema attention. Apply restrictions, session holds and recovery state still apply.

- **All done:** clear the condition when durable accepted facts prove all its work done under the rules above. Memory counts, inbox row absence, API return and later local edits alone prove nothing done. Keep the done facts across reopen and repeated callbacks.
- **Later success:** an accepted `succeeded` result clears earlier incomplete work of this binding when the success commits durably. Accepted durable facts must prove that the original successful exchange came after the incomplete exchange. Result delivery order, result-write order and end instants alone prove no such order. This applies to an older success's first late `RecordExchange`, as well as repeated delivery. Neither may clear newer work. If the original exchange order is unknown, keep the incomplete condition. Stale or refused results clear nothing.
- **Other results:** `waiting`, `failed` and `cancelled` keep earlier incomplete work. Another accepted `incomplete` adds its work. Keep outgoing and incoming facts separately when each needs its own proof, even for the same entity. Done in one direction does not mean done in the other.
- **Count:** show `exchangeIncomplete` with count 1 while a known incomplete condition remains. This count does not count entities or versions. Repeated results and the same entity in both directions do not increase it. P12.7's last completed exchange and last attempt still serve their display purpose. Neither record alone shows all this condition's work.
- **Reopen:** keep the work, done facts and accepted original exchange order across reopen. K10.2's binding, account, publication, container and environment rules apply. Another binding's result or done fact cannot clear this work. Existing guards still apply, including P12.7's no-write rule after close or replacement.
- **Older data:** unknown work or original exchange order stays explicitly unknown. A table that is absent or empty by default proves no work done. Keep a known incomplete condition when done cannot be proved. Work accepted after the update can clear unknown facts only by proving a rule above. Unknown history creates no incomplete result. It must not become zero attention or complete attention knowledge.

Rust chooses the physical schema under K10 and must version these new facts. Keep every distinction above. Do not give older data these facts under its existing version. This section adds no send, apply, account, setup or recovery authority.

### P12.7 Recording

- Every `ExchangeEnded` input emits `RecordExchange{result}` once, whichever step the exchange ended at (decided 2026-10-27): step 6, or an early stop such as the step 1 gate wait, a cancel before the capture, a background or quit `until`, or a guard that became stale.
- `RecordExchange` writes a `succeeded` result as the binding's last completed exchange: its end instant, source and counts. Any other result is written as the last exchange attempt: its kind, instant and error class. U161 shows both (R1560).
- With `RecordExchange` the core runs the day clock's `checkRollover()` once, whatever the result (R597, `sched/day-clock#D8`); the check is idempotent, so it writes nothing when the day did not change.
- When the exchange ended because the collection closed or was replaced (`CollectionReplaced`), the action's guard names a lifecycle generation that is no longer current (P4): `RecordExchange` writes no row and runs no check. The first `checkRollover()` of the next open or of the new generation runs instead (`sched/day-clock#D8`).
- The exchange's capture and selection live in memory. A crash ends the exchange without a result; its pending work stays in tracking.

## P13. Media (R2057, R122, R124, R1592)

### P13.1 Order and the media setting

- Collection records go first: across requests, and within a batch request whose scope includes both zones, where the builder puts every sendable collection record before any blob record (K12.1 order). A request scoped to one zone holds only that zone's records; zones are never mixed to keep this order (K12.1, "Send scope"). Blob names join the engine's pending changes when the blob is ready to upload (P13.3); a collection entity that waits for the flush timer does not hold them back.
- Asset downloads start only after the collection zone's fetch in progress has drained (`FetchDrained{collection}`); with no collection fetch in progress they start at once.
- Media off (R122): `PauseMedia`. No blob is uploaded or downloaded, and `UpdatePending{full}` removes blob names. Media-name entities are collection records and keep syncing. Status shows media paused.
- Media on: `ResumeMedia`, then P13.2 and P13.3 when the gate is open. With auto off, `ResumeMedia` waits for the next Sync Now (P12.3 step 7), as other cloud work does (P6).
- `ResumeMedia` (decided 2026-10-27), executed by `SyncCoordinator` through U114, in this order, each step after the one before returns: (1) read `root` by record ID, with `e` (P13.2), before any blob work, every time (K9.2). When the binding holds no acked media key, record the key read as acked (K9.2, V10.1). When it holds one and the read returns another key, unknown item or zone not found, set `mediaZoneMissing` and stop: no later step runs, now or at a later `ResumeMedia` (K2, K9.2, K12.5). Unknown item or zone not found with no acked key sets it too (V10.1). A read that fails with any other error ends this `ResumeMedia` with no blob work; the next `ResumeMedia` reads again (P2). (2) `ReadBlobs{missing}` (P4, P13.2) unless a read round runs, and await it; (3) `UpdatePending{full}`, which now adds the names of blobs ready to upload (P13.3); (4) `RequestSend{media}` when a blob is ready to upload or a blob deletion is due (P10.3 item 2), with auto on or off; (5) start the download queue (P13.2), when it is not running, once the collection zone's fetch in progress has drained, as the second bullet says. It is idempotent: with media already running it repeats steps 1 to 4, so the `root` read of step 1 runs at every `ResumeMedia` (decided 2026-10-27). A device with `mediaZoneMissing` never creates the zone or the root (K2).
- `PauseMedia`, executed by `SyncCoordinator`: stop the download queue and the record-ID reads. A download in progress is cancelled after its staged bytes are left as V6 says, so only that blob restarts later. Then `UpdatePending{full}` removes blob names. Uploads the engine has already started end under its own scheduling, as in P11.2.
- After `quotaExceeded`, blob uploads stay pending in tracking, as collection records do: the engine drops the failed blob names, which are parked (P1) until the next full update at an event trigger. Downloads continue (P15.1).
- A missing local file is not a deletion (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/cloudkit-records#K9.4`, `media/media-journal#J6`). Only an explicit Meguri removal makes a media-name tombstone: a feed item whose absence cause is `removed` (`media/media-journal#J6`; K9.4 lists its callers, among them Delete Unused, a Check Media fix and the old name of a Check Media rename). Whether the journal record itself is deleted does not decide; the cause does. A name the J6 feed reports absent with the cause `missing` (its file facts were dropped: deleted or evicted outside Meguri, an incomplete restore; a removal item for dropped facts included) is `file_missing`: a download candidate (P13.2), and `mediaMissing` when the media zone holds no blob for it. A file that leaves the size range is `unsyncable`. Neither makes a tombstone. A `removed` item while J6 reports the feed not complete gives the mark `removal_held` (K9.4): the name is neither sent, downloaded nor reported missing, counts in `blocked`, and is sent as a tombstone once J6 reports the feed complete.

### P13.2 Downloads

- Mechanism (decided 2026-10-27). The engine's fetch options have no way to leave out asset fields, so every record the engine fetches arrives with its assets downloaded. A fetch of `meguri-media` through the engine would therefore download every blob at once, outside every bound below, and production allows only one engine per database. So:
  - The engine's fetch scope is exactly `meguri-root` and the bound collection zone. U114 sets that scope in the options the engine asks for before its own scheduled fetches and in every `RequestFetch` (P4). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 3; SDK headers): the engine asks for the next fetch options (`nextFetchChangesOptions`) between the server requests of every fetch, scheduled or manual, and the request's context carries the options of the fetch in progress. U114 returns those options with their zone scope reduced to the zones that are both in it and in {`meguri-root`, the bound collection zone}, and keeps their priority zones and their operation group (O19 review, P3 `FetchDrained`). So a manual call's narrower scope (`RequestFetch{root}`) stays as `RequestFetch` set it, and P3's inputs, one per zone of the call's scope, match what the engine fetched. `meguri-media`, the previous epoch's zone and every other `c-` zone are excluded, which is how K20's first row ("not fetched after a switch") holds. The engine tracks the zones whose server changes it has not fetched, and each blob upload of another device gives `meguri-media` such changes again, so the signed runs check that a zone left out of the scope for good with unfetched changes does not make the engine schedule fetches again and again (P21). Decided 2026-10-27: if it does, the scope stays, since an engine fetch of `meguri-media` downloads every asset; the extra wakes are then a known cost, recorded with that run and settled here before U114 ships.
  - Media-zone records are never fetched as changes. U114 reads them by record ID with its own record fetch operation, naming the fields it wants: `MGMediaRoot` (`root`) with `e` at `ResumeMedia` (P13.1); a blob with `e` only to learn whether it exists and to check its envelope, before an upload decision (P13.3) and before a download is queued; a blob with `e` and `a` in the download queue below. The blob's record name comes from the content that a live media-name entity names (K4.2), so no listing of the zone is needed.
  - A read result sets the blob's server state (K10.3): present, or `deleted` for unknown item. A `deleted` blob is uploaded again when the local file holds its content (K9.5); otherwise its live names count in `mediaMissing` (P3, P14.4), and the blob is read again by each `ResumeMedia` read round (scope `missing`: each `Foreground` with auto on, each Sync Now media step, P12.3 step 7), so a blob another device uploads later is found. These reads are event triggered. Zone not found, or the engine's database changes reporting `meguri-media` deleted, sets `mediaZoneMissing` (V10.1).
  - **Read rounds** (decided 2026-10-27). A blob in state `unknown` that a live media-name entity names is read in a `ReadBlobs` round (P4), never one by one. A round runs at `ResumeMedia` (step 2), and, with auto on (read as on at step `zones`, P6), media on and `MGMediaRoot` acked, at `FetchDrained{collection}` and at a `TrackingChanged{cause: local}` (a new picture, P10.1) while `blobsUnknown` is above 0. At most one round runs at a time; a trigger during a round adds nothing. With auto off a round runs only from Sync Now's media step, as other cloud work (P6). Each round is reported as `EngineActivity{media, read}`; a result that makes a blob ready to upload reaches the engine through the update that follows it (P10.1, `ResumeMedia` step 3).
  - A read that fails with a retryable error is tried again only at the next round (P2), and the round counts as a failing cycle of kind `read` (P15).
  - **Network return** (decided 2026-10-27). A blob read or a download that failed for the network is tried again only at a later trigger, and an app that stays in front with nothing new to fetch may meet none for a long time. So at each `Network{reachable: true}` that follows `false`, with the gate open, auto on (read as on at step `zones`, P6), media on and no `mediaZoneMissing`, the policy emits `ResumeMedia` (P13.1): its read round (step 2) and its start of the download queue (step 5) restart the media work the network loss stopped. It is one action per transition, so it cannot loop. With auto off, media waits for Sync Now, as other cloud work does.
  - Blob uploads and the blob deletions of P10.3 still go through the engine's sends to `meguri-media` (K12.4); sending needs no fetch scope.
  - So Assumption A4 (K9.3, K23) is false for the engine's fetch and is relied on only for record-ID reads that name their fields. The signed runs check it (P21).
- Downloads and record-ID reads run only while the gate is open (P6) and media is on; an install waits while the gate is closed.
- Priority, highest first:
  1. blobs named in `VisibleMedia`: the current card and the review head (`head(3)`), and the cards and notes on screen;
  2. blobs named by cards due today;
  3. all others, in record-name order.
- A `VisibleMedia` input re-sorts the waiting downloads (`PrioritizeMedia`); it cancels no download in progress.
- At most 2 asset downloads run at once.
- Staged bytes (K9.3) never exceed 256 MiB, except that one file larger than that may be staged alone.
- A download starts only when the volume's free space after it stays at or above 512 MiB. Otherwise media downloads pause and status sets the `noSpace` flag of P14.4's `media` field. Decided 2026-10-27: this pause is not the `localStorageFull` attention item, and collection sync goes on (`sync/status-strings#T5`). The pause ends at `VolumeSpaceAvailable`, `Foreground` or `ResumeMedia` when the free-space rule holds again.
- Assets are streamed to files, never held whole in memory.

### P13.3 Uploads

- A blob is ready to upload when a live media-name entity names its content, the local file holds that content, and its server state is `deleted`: a record-ID read (P13.2) found it absent. A blob in state `unknown` is read first, in the next read round (P13.2), so bytes already in the media zone are never uploaded again (K9.1); a save that still meets an existing blob counts as success (K9.3). A media-name entity marked `file_missing`, `withheld` or `superseded` (P3) never makes its blob ready.
- Blob uploads go through the engine's sends to `meguri-media`. With auto on the engine schedules them once the names are pending; with auto off only `ResumeMedia`'s `RequestSend{media}` sends them (P13.1), so a picture added with auto off is uploaded at the next Sync Now.
- A media-zone batch holds at most 8 blobs and at most 128 MiB of asset bytes; one larger blob goes alone.
- The asset file is an APFS clone of the media file in the cloud staging directory, checked against the entity's SHA-256 after cloning. On a mismatch the clone is dropped and the file is hashed again (`media_hashes`, K9.1). When the file's SHA-256 still differs from the entity's, tracking durably sets the entity's mark `media_changed` (K9.3, K10.3): the blob is not uploaded and status lists the file under the attention item `mediaChanged`, also after a relaunch, until the next feed item of that name, or a reconcile scan that reads other facts for it, clears the mark and the name compares as any local change (K9.3, K9.4).
- A `present` blob can be stale (K9.3, "Stale `present`"): tracking makes it `unknown` again when a local change names its content anew, at the first drained collection fetch after that version's ack, and at the first drained collection fetch at least 24 h after that ack (K9.3 case 3: a deletion another device computed before the ack is sent within 1 h of its drain, P10.3 item 2, so it has landed by then), so it counts in `blobsUnknown` and the next read round reads it before any upload decision.

## P14. Status (R1558, R1559, R1560)

### P14.1 Sources

Status is a function of the latest tracking summary (P3) and the policy state (P5): account, binding, fence, device settings, reachability, engine activity, error class and tracking stop. The last completed exchange and the last quiet point come from P19's rows.

It never reads `col.mod`, `col.scm`, `col.ls`, `usn` columns or graves, and never sends an Anki `meta` request (R1559). Anki transfer status is separate and appears only in Settings Anki Transfers (R1560).

### P14.3 Quiet point

At an input after which both zones' fetches have drained since they last started, every ready entry they fetched is applied durably (no ready entry, no apply in progress), and the summary shows no pending entity that is not held and nothing in flight (P1; a durable in-flight entry alone does not count), the policy emits `RecordQuietPoint{now}` once for that drain. "Both zones" are the engine's fetch scope, `meguri-root` and the bound collection zone (P13.2). Media, `file_missing`, `withheld` and `superseded` entities (P3) and held entities do not affect it.

### P15.1 Quota exceeded (V10.3)

Removed (2026-10-27): the quota pause, its 1-hour delay and `QuotaRetryDue`. The rest of this section is in force. Decided 2026-10-27 (P2, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.3`): quota exceeded sets no pause and no timer.

The error emits no `UpdatePending`, and `UpdatePending{changes}` never hands a parked name back, so a failure cannot start a send loop: nothing is sent again until an event trigger.
- The full update hands every parked collection and blob name back at the event triggers (P2): a local change (`TrackingChanged{cause: local}`, P10.1), `Foreground` (P11.1), an `Account` input (P10.1, P15), Sync Now (P12.3 step 4), an engine start, and a drained fetch of the collection zone (`FetchDrained{collection}`, P10.1; not `FetchDrained{root}` alone, P2); also at `SessionEnded` (P9) and in the background and quit flushes (P11.2, P11.4). ### P15.2 Tracking failures (V3.3)

- `TrackingWriteFailed` sets the tracking stop: `StopEngine{trackingFailed}` (the actor stops the instance without saving its state, V3.3), `CancelFlushTimer`, and a running exchange ends `failed{localStorageFull}`. The attention item is `localStorageFull` when `noSpace`, else `trackingError`.
- No timer retries the stop (V3.3, P2). Its triggers are `TrackingChanged` (a local change whose dirty mark committed), `Foreground`, `SyncNowRequested`, an `Account` change and `VolumeSpaceAvailable`. No engine runs during the stop, so no engine report is a trigger. At `TrackingChanged` that commit is the successful write. At each other trigger the policy first emits `ProbeTracking` (P4), which reports `TrackingChanged` or `TrackingWriteFailed`.
- A `TrackingChanged` that is a local change clears the stop at most once between two `Foreground` inputs (P5 holds the flag; `Foreground` resets it). After that, during the same foreground period, only the `TrackingChanged` that answers a `ProbeTracking` at one of the other triggers clears it (P5 holds a probe in progress, so the policy tells the two apart). Decided 2026-10-27: a failure that repeats on every commit (a constraint error on an inbox insert, a nearly full disk) then costs one engine restart and refetch per foreground period, not one per answer.
- At a `TrackingChanged` that clears the stop, the attention item clears with it. A reconcile scan that the stop held (a scan whose commit failed, or a scan-required flag set during the stop, K11.4 "Failed commits") starts then, and runs before any engine: the gate stays closed for it (P6). When it ends and the rest of the gate holds, the gate opens (P10.1): `StartEngine` from the last saved engine state.
- A probe, or a commit of the new instance, that fails sets the stop again (`TrackingWriteFailed`). Nothing retries until the next trigger.
- So no cursor advances past an inbox write that failed (K13.1, V3.1).

## P20. Invariants

1. No action starts, resumes or schedules a manual Anki transfer.
2. While a session is active, no record handed to the engine contains the effect of a step of R or of the detached hold.
3. Every collection-zone record is built from a barrier, from commits covered by the WAL flush.
4. Nothing is sent or applied while the gate is closed or a fence is set.
5. An exchange reports `succeeded` only when P12.4 holds; it never reports media done when media is pending.
6. Status never reads `col.mod`, `col.scm`, `col.ls`, `usn` or graves, and never makes an Anki request.
7. At most one flush timer, one exchange, one background or quit flush, and one engine per binding exist at a time. `StartEngine` is never emitted while an engine runs (a stopping instance does not count, P5; that `StartEngine` waits for its `EngineStopped`), and no new engine object is created before the previous one has reported `EngineStopped` (P4). No other app timer exists; a deadline only ends its own operation (P2).
8. No `sendChanges` or `fetchChanges` call is made from inside the engine's event handling.
9. A call with a stale guard, and a fact from an instance that is not current, change nothing.
10. `step` is deterministic and reads only its state and the input.
11. Every undo step whose effect a handed record contains was flagged at that record's barrier, before the writer took another op. A flagged step is never undone.
12. Every `RequestSend` except `FlushTimerFired`'s follows an `UpdatePending{full}` in the same action list or in `StartEngine`. No `EngineError` and no `TrackingChanged` other than a local change hands a parked name back (P4).
13. No deferral outlives a full update, except a deferral of the incoming-held set, which ends at `SessionEnded`; so every other entity deferred under an R that has since moved is decided again by a later barrier (P7.2).
14. While a session is active, no record handed to the engine is built for an entity of the incoming-held set, so a held image never replaces a newer server version (P7.2, P8.3).
15. Nothing is sent or applied while the open collection's `col.crt` differs from the binding's epoch `crt` (P6).
16. The engine's pending list never holds a zone deletion. A collection zone is deleted only by `DeleteZones`, right after an `MGActive` read that names the bound epoch, with a current guard (P4, P10.3).

## P22. Decisions for review

- R includes steps of no session above the session's newest answer (a Browse or editor edit during the session), so they wait for the next answer or the session's end. This follows R2047, R88 and R365; KTD6's wording ("the active session's contiguous top-of-stack steps") covers only the session's own steps. The alternative is that an outside edit ends hold-back, which publishes the newest answer.
- An incoming change that drops R moves it to the detached hold: its rows stay held until the session ends, and the entities the change wrote that have a row in R join the incoming-held set, deferred until the session ends. The answer is then no longer undoable (R2047: incoming conflicts invalidate affected undo entries). Decided 2026-10-27: an incoming change cuts the history below the newest step it touches (`engine/undo#M9.8`, as M7); safety comes before undo depth.
- Undo after such a drop reports the remaining history's reason; there is no new reason.
- Barrier flags cover only the steps that meet the batch request (P8.2): an undo step becomes non-undoable when a record built after it is handed, even if that save then fails.
- The foreground cloud flush target (60 s) covers the whole foreground, not only review sessions.
- Bounds: background flush ends at the earlier of the deadline − 5 s and 25 s; Mac quit flush 10 s; Sync Now waits at most 30 s for the gate and runs at most 3 send rounds. Decided 2026-10-27: Sync Now never waits out a retry-after; a throttled exchange ends `waiting` at once (P12.5, V10.3).
- Media: 2 concurrent downloads, 256 MiB staging, 512 MiB free-space reserve, upload batches of 8 blobs or 128 MiB.
- Sync Now stays available with auto off, while offline, while media is paused, after `quotaExceeded` and during a tracking stop (it probes tracking once, P15.2).
- Decided 2026-10-27: `checkRollover()` runs once after every Sync Now exchange, whatever its result, early stops included (P12.7); the check is idempotent.
- Decided 2026-10-27: a deferral holds only until the next full update, which every move of R causes (P7.2); the cost is one more barrier decision per full update.
- Decided 2026-10-27: an `EngineActivity` that ends carries its outcome and, for a send, the saved count; U114 reports an operation's errors before its end. The transient authentication state, the failing-cycle count and `quotaFull` clear on these fields (P3, P14.2, P15, P15.1).
