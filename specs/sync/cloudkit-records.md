# CloudKit records

Spec: sync/cloudkit-records
Requirements: R2043, R2044, R2045, R2052, R2053, R2057, R2058, R1572, R1592, R2066 (record contract); R1844, R1583 (CloudKit first download), R1584 (CloudKit placeholder), R1588 (CloudKit mass change), with `sync/policy#P13` (K9.3); supports R2047, R2048, R2049, R2050, R2051, R2054, R2055, R2056
Anki: none (Meguri design)
Scenarios: none yet
Status: Approved 2026-10-27 (R2066). Implement U113 and U114 against this contract; first settle the open items of `sync/policy#P24`.

How Meguri represents an open collection in the user's private CloudKit database: zones, record types, record names, the payload of each entity kind, media assets, the local tracking store, revisions and acknowledgement, incoming apply, conflict rules, tombstones, epochs and structural generations. Eligibility, hold-back and the Sync Now boundary are `sync/policy`. Crash windows, account changes, conflict-copy operations and replacement flows are `sync/cloudkit-recovery`. Where this file leaves a decision to them, it names it.

The Anki sync specs (`sync/normal-sync`, `sync/hold-back`, `sync/media-sync`, `sync/media-tracking`, `sync/one-way`) describe manual Anki transfers only (KTD26). Nothing here reuses their USN, `col.ls`, `col.scm`, graves-upload or media-USN rules as routine-sync state.

Lines marked **Assumption** state Apple behavior this design depends on and has not verified. K23 lists them as open questions.

## K1. Terms

- **Entity**: one logical collection object that maps to one CloudKit record (K6). Its **kind** is one of K6's nine kinds; its **key** identifies it inside the collection.
- **Row image**: the stored column values of an entity's rows, read under `model/row-records`.
- **Payload**: the canonical encoding of a row image plus the kind's Meguri fields (K5.3, K6.1). A version with no payload (some tombstones, K17.2) has the empty payload: zero bytes.
- **Row digest**: SHA-256 of the payload with every Meguri field (K6.1, "Meguri" fields) cleared. **Payload digest**: SHA-256 of the full payload bytes. The empty payload's digest is SHA-256 of the empty byte string.
- **Epoch**: one published cloud collection (K18). **Structural generation**: one coherent group of changes inside an epoch (K19).
- **Binding**: the pair of one local collection and one epoch, under one CloudKit environment, container and Apple account (K10.2).
- **Acknowledged** (acked): the server holds a version that this device has seen and recorded, through a save result or a fetch.
- **Pending**: the local row digest differs from the last acked row digest of the entity, or a local deletion is not yet acked.
- **Flushed**: a collection commit is flushed when the writer's flush (`storage/open-and-connections#C9.2`) has covered it, so it survives a power loss.
- **Later**: for two versions X and Y of one entity, X is later when its stamp (K15.1) is greater, or the stamps are equal and its payload digest is greater as an unsigned big-endian number. Every "later wins" rule below uses this order, so it is the same on every device.

## K2. Database and zones

- Meguri uses only the private database of the app's CloudKit container. Container identifiers, environments and schema promotion are user setup (R2065); development and daily-driver builds use separate containers.
- Zones:

  | Zone | Name | Records | Lifetime |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | root | `meguri-root` | `MGActive` | one per Apple account; never deleted by Meguri |
  | collection | `c-` + epoch id (K4.1) | `MGEpoch`, `MGEntity`, `MGGeneration` | one per epoch; deleted after K18.5 |
  | media | `meguri-media` | `MGMediaRoot`, `MGBlob` | one per Apple account, shared by all epochs |

- Why separate zones: collection records can be fetched and sent before bulk media (R2057, R1592); media progress is independent of collection progress (R1572, R2050); a new epoch reuses the uploaded media (K9.1); an old epoch is removed with one zone deletion.
- Creating a zone that exists is not an error (**Assumption A20**, decided 2026-10-27: a modify-record-zones operation that saves a zone that exists succeeds and leaves the zone and its records unchanged; the SDK headers do not say so, so the signed runs check it, K22.3, and K23 gives the fallback). Only the publisher of an epoch (K18.3, the `publish` op of `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15`, a device's first publication at connection included, V13.3) creates a zone or a root record. Decided 2026-10-27: between V15.2 steps 2 and 5 it always creates its collection zone, `meguri-root` and `meguri-media` in one zone creation, whether or not they exist, since no read tells it that one is missing (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 3), and it always saves `root` (K9.2) as a new record with a new media key, whatever it found, except after an encrypted data reset (below): a `root` that exists makes the save fail with server record changed and gives the binding the server's key (K9.2). Decided 2026-10-27: outside the encrypted-data-reset path, a server `root` that this build cannot read (a newer `format`, a decode error, an `e` that CloudKit fails to read (a decryption error)) deletes nothing: the op waits at its step with needs attention, as `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`'s "any other error" row and V15.2 step 3 say, since that `meguri-media` may hold the only copy of other devices' files. It saves `MGActive` as K18.3 step 5 says. Decided 2026-10-27: after an encrypted data reset (its binding before is isolated `encrypted_data_reset`) it reads `root` by record ID after the zone creation, before any `root` save. A `root` whose `e` this build reads and decodes gives the binding its media key (K9.2), and nothing is saved or deleted: another device recreated the zone after the reset, and its blobs are the active epoch's. Unknown item (the creation made the zone, or the zone holds no `root`) makes it save a new `root` (K9.2). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): the media key is plain bytes inside `e` (K5.2) and Meguri decrypts nothing itself, so the only sign that a reset left the zone unreadable is CloudKit's own: the read returns the `root` record with its encrypted field `e` absent (A18 false). Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): a read that fails, with any error code, is not that sign: no `CKError` code names an undecryptable value (SDK headers, checked 2026-10-27), so such a failure is V26.1's "any other error" and deletes nothing, until the signed runs record the exact error domain and code CloudKit gives for such a record and this contract names it (`sync/cloudkit-recovery` A18). Meguri never saves a `root` without `e`. Only that result, on this path, makes it delete `meguri-media` and create it again with a new `MGMediaRoot` and media key, as V15.2 and V26.1 say. Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): it does so only when two more reads hold. First, a record-ID read of this op's own `MGEpoch`, which the op saves `publishing` (acked) before it acts on the sign, returns its `e` and it decodes. Second, a second `root` read made right before the deletion still gives the sign. Decided 2026-10-27 (review of the P24 corrections; SDK headers: an operation's resource timeout is `NSURLSessionConfiguration.timeoutIntervalForResource`, 7 days by default, so a submitted deletion can wait for the network for days): the deletion is submitted within 10 s of that read's return, measured on a clock that counts sleep, in the same process, with an operation configuration whose `timeoutIntervalForResource` is 30 s, and it is never long-lived. A deletion that cannot be submitted in time (a suspension, a kill) is not made: the op makes the `root` read, the own `MGEpoch` read and the second `root` read again. A deletion that ends with a timeout or another error is V26.1's "any other error", and the op then starts again from the `root` read. When the own `MGEpoch` read gives the same sign, this device cannot read encrypted values yet (its keys may not have reached it after the reset): the op waits at its step as V26.1's "any other error" says and deletes nothing. A second `root` read that decodes gives the binding its media key, as above, and nothing is deleted. Decided 2026-10-27 (review of the P24 corrections): another publisher may be acting on the same sign meanwhile, so the second read's other results are fixed too, and none of them deletes anything. Unknown item (it deleted and created `meguri-media` again and has not saved its `root` yet): the op saves a new `root` as a new record, so a `root` saved first makes that save fail with server record changed and gives the binding the server's key (K9.2). Zone not found (it is between its deletion and its creation): the op creates `meguri-media` again, which is not an error when that zone exists by then (A20), and then acts as for unknown item. Any other error is V26.1's "any other error". A binding whose zone another publisher still deletes in the remaining window (at most about 40 s after that publisher's second read) learns it at its next media start (K9.2, `media_zone_missing`), and only a later V15 publication gives it media again. So two publishers keep the active epoch's blobs readable only when each deletion lands before the other's zone creation. A present `e` that fails Meguri's decode, or that decodes to a newer `format`, deletes nothing on this path either: it is V26.1's "any other error". The user's explicit V15 choice is what allows this; nothing else does. Decided 2026-10-27 (R2066 approval): these zone creates and saves are U114's own operations (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), never engine sends. An adopting device (K18.4) creates nothing: when `meguri-media` or `root` is missing, its binding sets `media_zone_missing` (K9.2, `sync/policy#P13.1`).
- A bound device that finds a zone missing creates nothing by itself, and what stops depends on the zone (K12.3, K12.5):
  - its bound collection zone, or `meguri-root` or `MGActive`: it stops sending, its binding becomes isolated, and `sync/cloudkit-recovery` asks for an explicit choice (R2054; V10.1, V10.2);
  - `meguri-media` while the collection zone exists: collection sync continues and the binding is not isolated. Media uploads and downloads stop for this binding for good: it never recreates the zone and never uses a media zone that another device recreated (K9.2 says how it tells). Status reports it until V15 publishes again (V10.1). The binding records this as `media_zone_missing` (K10.3), which never clears for that binding.
- One active collection per Apple account (R2043): the `MGActive` record names exactly one epoch. A device sends and applies collection records only for the epoch it is bound to, and sends only while `MGActive` names that epoch (K18.4). Exception: the publisher of an epoch sends to that epoch's zone during K18.3 steps 1 to 4, before `MGActive` names it.
- **Confirmed root** (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.2`). An engine instance sends to its bound collection zone only after a fetch of `meguri-root` by that same instance has drained and confirmed the bound epoch, after a record-ID read of `MGActive` for that instance confirmed it (its result reaches the core through `root_read`: the last sub-bullet below, K12.5 "A zone missing from a first fetch"), or, for a publisher, after its own `MGActive` save confirmed it during that instance (below; K12.1 rule 3, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.2`). The binding of a `publish` op is exempt while the op's step is before `done` (the exception above), except at `cancelling`: from that commit the core builds no batch for it (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2`). Decided 2026-10-27: while exempt, its instance gets no `RootReadDue`, no no-state read (`sync/policy#P3`, K12.5) and no isolation `active_missing` or `epoch_switched` (K12.5, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.2`); its `done` commit confirms its epoch (below). So a device that comes back online learns of an epoch switch before it sends.
  - Decided 2026-10-27: an incremental root fetch delivers no `MGActive` while it is unchanged, so confirmation reads tracking, not the fetch's records. A drained root fetch confirms the epoch when, after the core has handled its events, the binding's last applied `MGActive` (K10.3 `bindings`) names the bound epoch, and the fetch reported neither a deletion of `MGActive` or of `meguri-root` nor an `MGActive` version naming another epoch. A drained fetch that delivered nothing therefore confirms, and a device relaunched from a saved engine state sends its offline edits once its root fetch drains.
  - The last applied `MGActive` is set for an adopting device when its binding is created, from the version it read (K18.4); for a publisher, whose binding exists from `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 2, by the accepted result of its K18.3 step 5 save (V26.1: the "saved" row, or the row for an earlier attempt that landed), in the tracking commit that records step `done`; and at each `MGActive` version or deletion a root fetch delivers. Decided 2026-10-27: that `done` commit also confirms the bound epoch for the current engine instance and records V10.2's confirmation mark, since a conditional save that names the epoch is at least as current as a drained root fetch. So the publisher's instance, whose exemption ends at `done` and whose first root drain came before the switch, needs no later root fetch before it sends; `sync/policy#P10.1` asks for that send. It commits in the tracking transaction that handles that fetched batch (K13.1 step 2), before an engine state that includes the batch's cursor is saved (K13.1 step 3), so a cursor never moves past an `MGActive` change that tracking has not recorded.
  - A binding that records no last applied `MGActive` (not expected: the publisher's binding records none before its switch, but it is exempt and gets no read, above) is not confirmed by a drain that delivered nothing: U114 first reads `MGActive` by record ID (`RootReadDue`, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), and the result is recorded and decides as `sync/policy#P10.1` ("Confirmed root") says.
- At most one CKSyncEngine object exists per process, the current engine instance (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V3.4`).

## K3. Record types and fields

Six record types. Each has the field `e`, a CloudKit encrypted bytes value, and at most one asset field. No record type has any other custom field, and no custom field is plaintext.

| Record type | Zone | Fields | Mutable |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MGActive` | root | `e` | yes, conditional saves only |
| `MGEpoch` | collection | `e` | yes: state `publishing` to `complete` once |
| `MGEntity` | collection | `e`, `pa` (asset, payload when large) | yes |
| `MGGeneration` | collection | `e`, `ma` (asset, member list when large) | yes: state changes only |
| `MGMediaRoot` | media | `e` | no |
| `MGBlob` | media | `e`, `a` (asset, file bytes) | no |

This is the initial CloudKit schema (R2058). Adding a record type or field later needs a schema promotion, which is a user step (R2065); new data goes inside `e` instead, under a new envelope format (K5.4).

## K4. Record names

### K4.1 Identifiers

- An **epoch id** is 16 random bytes. Its text form is the bytes in RFC 4648 base32, lowercase, without padding (26 characters). The same text form is used for every 16-byte value named in this section.
- A **record key** is 32 random bytes made by the device that publishes the epoch and stored in `MGEpoch` (K5.2). A **media key** is 32 random bytes stored in `MGMediaRoot` (K9.2).

### K4.2 Names

| Record | Name |
|---|---|
| `MGActive` | `active` |
| `MGEpoch` | `epoch` |
| `MGEntity` | `e` + text form of the first 16 bytes of HMAC-SHA256(record key, kind code byte ‖ key bytes) |
| `MGGeneration` | `g` + text form of the generation id (K19.2) |
| `MGMediaRoot` | `root` |
| `MGBlob` | `b` + text form of the first 16 bytes of HMAC-SHA256(media key, content SHA-256) |

- Names carry no note text, file name, tag, deck name, config key or creation time (R2058, KTD25). One record type serves all entity kinds, so the service cannot count entities per kind.
- **Key bytes** per kind:

  | Kind | Key | Key bytes |
  |---|---|---|
  | note, card, revlog, deck, preset, notetype | the row id | 8 bytes, big-endian two's complement |
  | tag | the tag | UTF-8 of the tag after the case folding the `tags` table's `unicase` collation applies (`storage/schema-18`) |
  | config | the `KEY` | UTF-8 bytes as stored |
  | media name | the file name | UTF-8 of its case key (`media/media-files#M2`) |

- A receiver recomputes the name from the decoded kind and key. A mismatch refuses the record (K20).

## K5. The entity envelope

### K5.1 `MGEntity.e`

The field holds a protobuf message `meguri.cloud.v1.Entity`:

| No. | Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `format` | uint32 | envelope and kind-specific payload format together, 1 (K6.1) |
| 2 | `kind` | uint32 | kind code (K6) |
| 3 | `key` | bytes | key bytes (K4.2) |
| 4 | `origin` | bytes (16) | random identity given when the entity was first tracked (K11.1); changes only when K14.1 identifies the entity with a server entity |
| 5 | `erev` | uint64 | entity revision: the acked server `erev` plus 1; a new entity starts at 1 |
| 6 | `generation` | optional bytes (16) | the structural generation this version belongs to (K19) |
| 7 | `tombstone` | bool | true for a deletion (K17) |
| 8 | `deleted_at_ms` | int64 | tombstones only: deletion instant, epoch milliseconds |
| 9 | `payload` | optional bytes | the payload, when it is inline |
| 10 | `payload_sha256` | bytes (32) | payload digest, inline or asset; for the empty payload, SHA-256 of the empty byte string |
| 11 | `payload_size` | uint64 | payload length in bytes; 0 for the empty payload |
| 12 | `writer` | bytes (16) | the binding id (K10.2) of the binding that built this version |
| 13 | `base_sha256` | optional bytes (32) | the payload digest of the server version this save is conditional on: the entity's acked payload digest when the record was built; absent for a new record |

- Decided 2026-10-27: `origin` is shared by every device once an entity has synced, so it cannot tell which device wrote a version. `writer` does: a device knows its own landed saves by it (K13.2, "Own landed saves"). `base_sha256` tells a device that another device built a version on one of its own landed saves (K13.2, "Built on an own landed save"). Both are inside `e` and carry no content (K8).
- Every save is conditional on the server's change tag (K12.2), so the server's `erev` for one record grows by exactly 1 per saved version, and two saved versions of one record never share an `erev`.
- When the encoded envelope with the payload inline would exceed 786,432 bytes, field 9 is absent and the payload is the asset `pa`. A claim batch may also move an anchor's payload to `pa` to stay within the request budget (K7, K19.3). Otherwise `pa` is absent. The empty payload is inline: field 9 present and empty. A receiver accepts either form and checks the digest and size (K20).

### K5.3 Canonical encoding

- Messages are proto3. A field marked optional has explicit presence: it is written when present, also with a default value. A scalar field not marked optional is omitted when it holds its default value (0, false, empty bytes, empty string), as proto3 does.
- **`Stored` values** (decided 2026-10-27). A `Stored` field of a payload (K6.1) is never optional and is always written, also when its content is a default value. Inside it, the oneof member that holds the column's storage class is always written, also with a default value (integer 0, real 0.0, empty text, empty blob), as proto3 oneof presence does. So `Stored{integer: 0}` and `Stored{text: ""}` have different bytes, and a receiver always knows the storage class. A payload in which a `Stored` field is absent, or a `Stored` with no oneof member set, is non-canonical and refused (K20).
- Fields are written in field-number order, each present field once, no unknown fields, repeated scalars packed, repeated messages in the order this file gives.
- Two devices that encode the same row image produce the same bytes, so digests compare across devices.
- A receiver decodes the payload, encodes it again by these rules and compares the bytes. A difference refuses the record (K20). Payload format 1 has no compression.

### K5.4 Newer formats

For `MGEntity`, `Entity.format` selects the envelope and the kind-specific payload schema together. Value 1 uses K5.1 and K6.1. A payload has no separate format field: its fields and their numbers are exactly those of K6.1, with no change to format-1 bytes.

A record whose `format` is above what this build supports is kept in the inbox (K13) with reason `format`, never applied and never overwritten by this device: the entity is not sent while such a record is the server's version. Status reports unsupported schema (R1561) until Meguri is updated.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 4 | deck | `decks.id` | one `decks` row | parent deck, preset of a normal deck |
| 5 | preset | `deck_config.id` | one `deck_config` row | none |

| 7 | tag | tag (unicase) | one `tags` row | none |
| 8 | config | `config.KEY` | one `config` row | none |
| 9 | media name | file name (case key) | one live record of the media journal | blob (K9) |

- The `col` row is not an entity. `crt` and `ver` belong to the epoch (K5.2); `mod`, `scm`, `ls`, `usn` and `dty` stay device-local Anki bookkeeping (K11.3).
- `graves` rows are not entities; tombstones stand for them (K17.2).
- `usn` columns are not in payloads: they are device-local manual-transfer bookkeeping (K11.3). Every other column of every listed row is in the payload, so unknown Anki data in rows, blobs, card `data` and config values round-trips unchanged (R2045). - Device-only state never enters a payload (R2045, R1468): it is not in the collection (`storage/config-table#C8`).

### K6.1 Payload messages

Column meanings are `model/row-records`. A payload carries each column's stored value, never the value a read tolerance gives (a `csum` outside the unsigned 32-bit range is carried as stored, not as 0). Types:

- integer columns: `int64`;
- text columns: `bytes`, the stored bytes (SQLite TEXT need not be valid UTF-8, which a proto `string` requires);
- blob columns: `bytes`;
- columns whose read tolerance in `model/row-records` accepts another storage class (card `due`, `odue` and `data`; revlog `ivl`, `lastIvl` and `time`) and note `sfld` (text or integer): `Stored`, a message with oneof { 1 `integer` int64, 2 `real` double, 3 `text` bytes, 4 `blob` bytes }, which keeps the storage class;
- a column that can hold NULL (`tags.config`): optional, absent for NULL.

"Meguri" fields are computed when the payload is built and are not stored in the collection.

## K7. Sizes and limits

| Item | Limit | Above the limit |
|---|---|---|
| `MGEntity` envelope with inline payload | 786,432 bytes | payload moves to the asset `pa` (K5.1) |
| entity payload, inline or asset | 104,857,600 bytes | the entity is **oversize**: not sent, the server keeps its previous version, it stays pending, and status lists it under needs attention with kind and key. Entities that reference it are held (K12.1); others continue. A structural generation that contains it cannot be claimed (K19.3) |
| `MGGeneration` envelope with inline members | 786,432 bytes | the member list moves to the asset `ma` |
| media file | 104,857,600 bytes, the syncable size of `media/media-files#M7.1` | a file that never had an entity stays local only (no entity, no blob); a live entity whose file grows past the limit follows K9.4 |
| one batch (one request) in any zone | 250 records, saves and deletions together; and 1,572,864 bytes of non-asset data, counted as the sum over its records of the encoded `e` value plus 2,048 bytes per record for its name, type and system fields | the core builds every batch within both bounds (K12.1). A record that does not fit goes to the next batch; one record always fits alone. After `limitExceeded` both bounds are halved for the rest of the engine instance (K12.3) |

**Assumption A1**: CloudKit accepts at most 1 MB of non-asset field data per record; 786,432 bytes leaves room for system fields and encryption overhead. The SDK headers state this limit (`CKRecord`).
**Assumption A1.1** (decided 2026-10-27): a request holds at most 250 records and about 2 MB of non-asset data. The sync engine's headers state the 250-record limit, and `CKError` states the 2 MB guideline and says the server can change its limits. The batch budget keeps 25% below 2 MB, and `limitExceeded` halves it (K12.3).
**Assumption A2**: one `CKAsset` in the private database can hold 104,857,600 bytes.

## K8. Encryption and metadata

- Card text, templates, CSS, deck and preset settings, collection settings, tags, file names and all other content are inside `e` (CloudKit encrypted values) or inside an asset (CloudKit asset encryption) (R2058).
- Zone names, record names (K4) and record type names carry no content. The service still sees record counts, sizes, record types, zone names and its own system fields such as creation and modification times. Product copy says that end-to-end protection of encrypted values and assets needs Advanced Data Protection and does not cover this metadata (R2058).
- **Assumption A3**: encrypted bytes values hold any byte string up to the record limit and need no index; Meguri never queries records by field.

## K9. Media

### K9.1 Identity

- A media file's content identity is the SHA-256 of its bytes. SHA-1 is carried only to match the media journal's records (`media/media-journal#J1`).
- File names are collection data: media-name entities (kind 9) live in the collection zone. File bytes are `MGBlob` records in the shared media zone, named by content (K4.2). Two names with the same content share one blob; a new epoch with the same files uploads no bytes again.
- The media journal keeps SHA-1 only. Tracking caches each file's SHA-256 by name, size, modification time and SHA-1 (`media_hashes`, K10.3), so a scan hashes only files whose journal record changed.

### K9.2 The media root

- `MGMediaRoot` named `root` holds the media key. A device that creates `meguri-media` (K2) creates the root with a new random key, saved as a new record. If the save finds an existing `root`, the device takes the server's key and discards its own.
- No `MGBlob` is saved before the device holds an acked `root`, so no blob is ever named with a discarded key. The save of a new `root` is an own operation (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), run only after the creation of `meguri-media` succeeded.
- Media-zone records are never fetched as changes (`sync/policy#P13.2`). A binding reads `root` by record ID, with the field `e`, and stores the media key it acked with the binding (K10.3 `bindings`). It never takes another key.
- Decided 2026-10-27: a binding that holds an acked key reads `root` again at each media start (`sync/policy#P13.1` `ResumeMedia`), before any blob work. When the read returns another media key, or unknown item, or zone not found, the media zone was deleted and maybe recreated by another device: the binding sets `media_zone_missing` (K2, K12.5) and does no more blob work. So no blob named with an old key is ever saved into a recreated zone, and no download looks for one there.

### K9.3 Blobs

- A blob is immutable. Saving a blob whose name already exists counts as success: the same name means the same content.
- **Blob existence** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P13.2`). The engine never fetches `meguri-media`. A device learns whether a blob exists only from a record-ID read that names its fields (`e` alone, or `e` and `a` for a download) and from its own acked saves. The result is the blob's server state in `blobs` (K10.3): `present`, or `deleted` when the read reports unknown item. A blob never read is `unknown`.
- A device uploads a blob only when all hold: a live media-name entity of its binding names its content and is not flagged `file_missing`, `withheld`, `superseded` or `removal_held` (K9.4) or marked `media_changed` (below); the local file holds that content; and the blob's server state is `deleted`. A blob in state `unknown` is read first, so bytes already in the media zone are never uploaded again (`sync/policy#P13.3`).
- **Stale `present`** (decided 2026-10-27). A blob's `present` state can be stale: another device may delete the blob later (K9.5), and this device learns that only from a read. So a `present` row becomes `unknown` again, and the next read round (`sync/policy#P13.2`) reads it before any upload decision, in three cases:
  1. a local change of this binding makes a live media-name entity name that content where the entity's acked version did not: a new entity, a re-creation over a tombstone, or a content change;
  2. the first drained fetch of the bound collection zone after such a version is acked, so a deletion another device sent while that version was in flight is seen. At that ack the row takes the mark `recheck` with the ack instant (K10.3). The drain makes the row `unknown` in the tracking commit that tracking makes before U114 reports `FetchDrained{collection}`, so that input's read round, or the `ResumeMedia` round after it (Sync Now's media step), reads it;
  3. decided 2026-10-27: the first drained fetch of the bound collection zone at least 24 h after that ack (it may be the drain of case 2), in the same commit, which also clears the mark. A deleting device sends a blob deletion only within 1 h of the drained fetch that computed it (K9.5, "Fresh deletions"), and neither that fetch nor the inbox held a name of the content (K9.5), so it ran before this ack. A deletion that lands after the case 2 read therefore lands within about 1 h of the ack, and the case 3 read sees it; 24 h leaves room for clock changes on either device.

  A read that finds the blob absent sets `deleted`, and the upload rule above sends it from the local file. Versions applied from the server change no `blobs` row: the device that wrote that version reads and uploads its blob. So a device that names content again after another device deleted its blob uploads it again, and no other device is left with `mediaMissing` (K9.5).
- **Clone mismatch** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P13.3`). When the upload's clone of the local file, hashed again, still differs from the content the entity names, tracking durably sets the entity's mark `media_changed` with the SHA-256 found (K10.3). While it is set, the blob is not uploaded from that file and status counts the name under `mediaChanged`, also after a relaunch. The next feed item of that name, or a reconcile scan that reads other facts for it, clears the mark, and the name then compares as any local change (K11.4).
- A device downloads a blob when a live media-name entity names content that the local file under that name does not hold: the entity was applied from the server, its local file is missing (K9.4 `file_missing`), or it is `superseded` (K9.4). It reads the blob by record ID before it queues the download. When the read finds no blob (never uploaded, or deleted, K9.5), the name is reported as missing media: the attention item `mediaMissing` (`sync/policy#P3`, `#P14.4`).
- A media-name entity may reach a receiver before its blob. Until the blob is installed, rendering uses the pending-media placeholder and Check Media does not count the file as missing (R1584).
- Downloads go to a cloud staging directory on the media folder's volume, with bounded concurrency and bounded staging bytes (R2057; the bounds are `sync/policy#P13.2`). The receiver checks the SHA-256 and size against the blob envelope and the media-name entity, then installs the file through the media store's exact-name write (`media/media-files#M6`), which never replaces an existing entry without trashing it (M9). A failed check discards the download, keeps any existing file, and reports needs attention. An interrupted install leaves the existing valid file intact.
- **Media apply intent.** An install, a trash or a move that tracking makes for an incoming version is a media apply: an install here, a tombstone's trash (K9.4), a case collision below, and K15.9's move of a local file to its hash name. Before its first store call tracking durably records an apply intent (K13.2). For each name the apply touches (the hash name of K15.9 included) the intent lists its **expected states** in order: the state before; each state that one of the apply's own store calls leaves before the next call (absent, after a trash or a move away); and the state after. A state is the `MediaName` digest of the journal's facts, or the empty payload's digest for an absent name. Decided 2026-10-27, so that a local write is never replaced without a copy, and so that an apply never abandons itself at its own intermediate state:
  - **Before each store call** tracking reads the name's current journal facts (the store's change check, `media/media-files#M7.3`, has run). When they equal the state the previous call leaves (the before state, for the first call), the call runs. When they equal the state this call itself leaves, the call already ran (a replay) and is skipped. Otherwise a local write landed: the apply is abandoned, the intent is removed, the current facts are a local change of the entity (a dirty mark, K11.1), and the inbox entry stays ready; a K15.9 resolution also settles the parts that ran (K15.9, "One resolution, one intent"). Its next apply meets a pending entity, so K9.4 and K15.9 decide.
  - **Feed items** of a name of the intent (`media/media-journal#J6`) whose facts equal one of its expected states are the apply's own and are not local changes, so the `removed` item of a trash or a move it makes is never a deletion signal (K9.4). Any other item of that name is a local change. When it comes before the apply's own items for that name in the feed, a store call of the apply has replaced it (the write landed between the check and the call): its bytes are in the media trash (`media/media-files#M9`, M10.1), and tracking keeps a conflict copy naming that trash entry, `media_content` for an install or `edit_vs_delete` for a tombstone's trash (K16). The incoming version stands. When it comes after them, it is a local change made after the apply, compared with the after state as K13.2 says.
  - When the store calls return (for a K15.9 resolution, also once its note-rewrite op is flushed, K15.9), one tracking transaction deletes the inbox entry, updates the entity (and creates K15.9's new entity for the hash name) and removes the intent. Decided 2026-10-27: that transaction first consumes the feed up to the journal's current counter under the feed-item rules above, and commits that consume and the consumed counter (K11.1) with the rest, so every item of the apply's own store calls is read while the intent exists. A consume after one of the apply's store calls that fails (`TrackingWriteFailed`, `sync/policy#P15.2`) leaves those items unconsumed: the apply then stops with its intent in place, makes no further store call, and is settled as "At open" says. So an item of the apply's own trash or move is never read with no intent, and never becomes a deletion signal (K9.4).
  - **At open**, an intent left by a crash is settled before any send (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.4` step 8); a K15.9 resolution's intent is settled as one, its move first and its op keys only once the move has run (K15.9, "At open"). Tracking first reads the feed items of the intent's names after the consumed counter (K10.3 `media_feed`) under the rules above. Then, per name: when it holds the after state, finish; when it holds an earlier expected state, continue the apply from the call after that state (the staged download, or a new read of the blob); otherwise abandon the apply as the first rule says, and the entry is applied again under K9.4 and K15.9.
- **Case collision.** The install refuses with `case_collision` (`media/media-files#M6`) when a present file has the entity's case key under another exact spelling, and changes nothing. That file is the same entity (K4.2). When its SHA-256 equals the acked content or the incoming content, no local change is at risk: under the media apply intent, tracking moves it to the trash (`media/media-files#M10.1`) and installs again. Otherwise the file holds a local change, and K15.9 decides first. A crash between the trash and the install leaves the name absent under the intent, which the settle above completes. Decided 2026-10-27: the server's exact spelling wins (K15.9), so every device ends with one spelling, and the replaced bytes stay in the trash.
- **Progress and resumption** (R1583, R1584, R1588, R1844, with `sync/policy#P13`). Each blob's upload and download state is durable in `blobs` (K10.3). After an interruption or a relaunch, an installed or acked blob is never transferred again, an interrupted download restarts only that blob (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V6`), and the local work before transfers continue is bounded: the feed after the consumed counter and the `blobs` rows, and hashing only files whose journal facts changed (`media_hashes`, K9.1). The file count and bytes of a first full download are the live media-name entities whose content is not local, with the sizes of their `MediaName` payloads; status shows them with an estimate (`sync/policy#P14.4` `media`). Transfers run off the writer and never block study (R2057).
- **Assumption A4** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P13.2`): false for the engine's fetch, which downloads every asset of every record it fetches; so the engine never fetches `meguri-media`. Meguri relies on A4 only for record-ID reads that name their fields: a read of a blob's `e` alone downloads no asset, so a device learns which blobs exist without downloading them, and the download queue serves visible cards first (R2057). The signed runs check it (K22.3).

### K9.4 Media-name entities and the media journal

- A media-name entity starts for a live journal record that is syncable (`media/media-files#M7.1`). A tracked file that has never been syncable (empty, larger than the limit) and a file that is not tracked (ignored, not normalized) have no entity.
- A live entity whose local file stops being syncable (it became empty or larger than the limit) stays live and is marked **unsyncable**: not sent, the server keeps its previous version, and status lists it under needs attention. It becomes sendable again when the file is syncable again. It never makes a tombstone.
- **Tombstones** (decided 2026-10-27). Only an explicit Meguri removal of the name makes a media-name tombstone: a feed item whose absence cause is `removed` (`media/media-journal#J6`: the trash of `media/media-files#M10.1`, whether Delete Unused, a Check Media fix, a staged operation's recovery or a deletion that a manual Anki media transfer applies calls it, and the old name of a Check Media rename). A trash that a media apply makes under its intent (K9.3) is not one.
- **Adoption window** (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V14.4`). An adoption replaces the collection, not the media folder, so every media file on the device then belongs to the replaced collection. While the binding's adoption window is open (K10.3 `bindings`), the reconcile scan and the feed classify each name by what the adopted epoch holds for its case key, never as a local change (K11.4): clean, `superseded`, `file_missing`, `withheld`, or no entity. A `superseded` or `withheld` entity records the content digest it was classified with (K10.3 `entities`).
- **Withheld** (V14.4). A name the adopted epoch does not name, or holds as a tombstone, whose local file is present: a new entity, or the epoch's acked tombstone entity, flagged `withheld`. It is never sent and its blob is never uploaded (K12.1 rule 1, V22 invariant 11). It holds back no note (K12.1 rule 6). It is released, and becomes pending as a new entity (or as a pending re-creation over the epoch's tombstone, K11.4), when either holds:
  1. its local content changes to content other than the classified digest;
  2. a local op other than `CloudIncoming` and `CloudRekey` writes a live note whose fields reference the name (`text/media-references`) while the note's before-image did not (`engine/change-stream#S2.4`): the user chose the file. Inserting an existing file through the editor is such a write, although the media journal records nothing for it (`media/media-files#M3`). K12.1 rule 6 then holds that note until the name is acked.

  A removal (cause `removed`) drops the flag: a new entity leaves tracking, as an entity never sent does (K17.1), and a tombstone entity stays the epoch's acked tombstone. A name that goes absent with the cause `missing` stays withheld, so a file of the replaced collection that comes back unchanged is still not uploaded. A clean close and reopen, a scan that finds the same facts, and a new feed consumer (K10.3 feed floor) keep the flag.
- **Superseded** (V14.4). A name the adopted epoch holds live with content other than the local file's. It is not pending and not sendable, whatever its local digest (K12.1 rule 1): the reconcile scan, an apply and a merge never take its local file as the local version L (K11.4, K13.2, K15.9). Its acked content is a download candidate (K9.3); the install runs as a media apply, the exact-name write moves the old file to the media trash (`media/media-files#M9`), and the transaction that marks the apply done clears the flag. When the media zone holds no blob for the acked content, the name is reported as missing media, and the flag and the local file stay. When its local content changes to content other than the classified digest and the acked content, the user changed the file: the flag clears and the name compares as any local change. When it becomes absent, whatever the cause, the flag clears and it is a missing local file (next bullet): the bytes removed were the replaced collection's, so no tombstone is made.
- **Missing local file** (decided 2026-10-27). A name reported absent with the cause `missing` (its file facts were dropped while the journal record stays: the file was deleted, moved or evicted outside Meguri, or a restore left it out), and a name with no record and no retained removal item (J6), never makes a tombstone and is not a local change. The live entity is marked `file_missing` and is not sent (K12.1 rule 1). When acked, its content becomes a download candidate (K9.3); when the media zone holds no blob for it, it is reported as missing media. An entity never acked whose `sent` is false leaves tracking, as K17.1 does for an unsent deletion; one with `sent` true waits for its save result and then follows the acked case. A `withheld` entity is not marked (above). The mark clears when the name is present again, and the entity then compares as any local change.
- **Carried names** (decided 2026-10-27, K18.3 step 2). A publisher's new binding is a new feed consumer, and its scan finds no local row for a name whose file is absent with the cause `missing` (K11.4), so without this rule the new epoch would hold no entity for a name the old epoch held live, adopters would classify their copies `withheld` (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V14.4`) and never upload them, and no device would report the name missing. So K18.3 step 2's scan reads the binding before of the `publish` op (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 2, always of the same account and environment) for each media case key:
  - the binding before holds an acked live entity for it, and that entity is flagged `file_missing` or `superseded` there, or the new scan finds the name absent with the cause `missing`: the new binding gets a new entity marked `carried`, whose local version is the binding before's acked `MediaName` payload, held with the entity (K10.3). It is pending and sendable as a new entity, and K12.1 sends that payload; its local file is not read for it, and no blob is uploaded for it from that file. The Saved result clears the mark, and the entity is then `file_missing` (file absent) or `superseded` (file present, classified with its digest), so its content is a download candidate or missing media (K9.3), and an adopter that holds the file classifies it clean and uploads the blob when the media zone lacks it (K9.3). Before that result, a feed item or scan result that would lift those flags (the file present again, or its content changed to content other than the classified digest and the carried content) clears the mark, and the name is then a new entity with its local file;
  - the binding before holds the entity flagged `withheld`: the new entity is flagged `withheld` with the same classified digest, so a Replace iCloud collection never uploads a file of a collection that an adoption replaced (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V22` invariant 11);
  - otherwise K11.4's table decides.
- **Incoming versions over these flags** (decided 2026-10-27). An incoming version S (K13.2, a save conflict of K12.3 included) never merges with a file that is not a local change of the binding:
  - `file_missing` or `superseded`: the entity counts as clean, with L = its acked version. A live S: the entity takes S and keeps its flag, and S's content becomes the download candidate; when a `superseded` file already holds S's content, the entity is clean and the flag clears. A tombstone S: the entity takes the tombstone. A `file_missing` entity drops the mark; nothing is trashed (there is no file) and no copy is made. A `superseded` entity becomes `withheld`, keeping its classified digest: the epoch no longer names the name, and its file still belongs to the replaced collection, so it stays on this device and is not trashed.
  - `withheld`, live S: S wins without a merge. Its install runs as a media apply, the exact-name write moves the local file to the media trash, and a conflict copy (`media_content`) names that trash entry (K16), so the user can still recover the file; the flag clears when the apply is done. There is no move to a hash name, no note rewrite and no new entity (K15.9 never compares it). So a file of the replaced collection never comes back under another name, and the notes of the epoch keep the name another device fixed.
  - `withheld`, tombstone S: the entity takes the tombstone and stays withheld; its file stays.
- Tracking keys media names by case key (K4.2): items for two spellings of one case key update one entity, and the name that is present gives its exact spelling.
- CloudKit media state lives in the tracking store (K10), never in the media journal's dirty flag or media USN, which belong to manual Anki media transfers (R1572). A reset of Anki media state does not change CloudKit state.
- Local media changes reach tracking from the media journal's file-fact feed (`media/media-journal#J6`: name, content and live state changes after a given counter value, each absence with its cause); the reconcile scan (K11.4) compares every journal record and reads the same causes. While J6 reports the feed not complete, no media-name tombstone is made, and a name the journal has no record of is neither downloaded nor reported missing, so an unregistered local file is never replaced (J6, "Complete registration").
- **Removal before complete registration** (decided 2026-10-27). A feed item or a scan result with the cause `removed` for a live entity while J6 reports the feed not complete is an explicit Meguri removal, and it is never undone by a download. Tracking consumes the item as usual and records it on the entity as the mark `removal_held` (K10.3): the entity stays `live` in tracking, is not sent (K12.1 rule 1), is neither downloaded nor reported missing, and for K13.2, K15.9 and K9.4 ("Incoming tombstone") counts as a pending local tombstone. In the first tracking commit after J6 reports the feed complete (the commit that closes an adoption window included, K11.4), every entity with the mark becomes a pending tombstone (K17.1) and the mark clears; one whose name is present again by then drops the mark and compares as any local change. An incoming tombstone clears the mark (both sides deleted it); an incoming live version is handled as against a pending local tombstone (deletion wins, K17.3). A `withheld` entity follows the adoption-window rules instead (a removal drops its flag). So the mark is durable and the deletion reaches CloudKit once registration completes, also when the removal item itself is deleted by then.
- A media-name tombstone carries the `MediaName` payload of the last live version this device acked or applied (K17.2); after a K17.1 save conflict that is the server's version S.
- **Incoming tombstone** (decided 2026-10-27). An incoming media-name tombstone is compared with the receiver's own entity, never with the tombstone's payload, which can name content the receiver never held:
  - clean (the local file holds the acked content): the file moves to the media trash (`media/media-files#M10.1`) as a media apply (K9.3), with no copy;
  - pending with a local content change (the local file holds other content than the acked one, or the entity is a new or re-created one): deletion wins (K17.3). The file moves to the trash as a media apply, and a conflict copy (`edit_vs_delete`) names that trash entry (K16);
  - a pending local tombstone: both sides deleted it; the entity takes the incoming tombstone and nothing is trashed;
  - flagged `file_missing`, `superseded` or `withheld`: the bullet above.

  Media bytes are never deleted permanently by sync.

### K9.5 Removing blobs

- A device deletes an `MGBlob` record only when all hold: the device is bound to the active epoch and has completed a fetch of its collection zone; no other epoch zone is retained (K18.5, K10.3 `zones`); no media-name entity of the binding names its content, in its local version while that is live or in its acked version while that is live (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review: a pending content change or tombstone whose save is not acked, and an `unsyncable`, `media_changed`, `file_missing`, `superseded`, `withheld`, `carried` or `removal_held` entity, still name their acked content, which the server and every other device keep while the change cannot land, K9.4, K12.3 `quotaExceeded`); decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4), since a fetched name waits in the inbox before it applies and some never apply (K5.4, K13.1, K13.2): no inbox `record` entry (any reason, any source) and no `refused` row of the binding holds a media-name version that names this content, or a version whose kind or content this build cannot read (a newer format, a payload in an asset not yet read, a refused envelope), and no media apply intent and no apply intent with a media-name `entry` key of the binding exists; no unresolved conflict copy on this device names it (K16); and no entity has named it so for 30 days (`blobs` "unreferenced since", K10.3: when the last local or acked live version naming that content went away). When it runs, and how the deletion reaches the engine, is `sync/policy#P10.3` (`RunCleanup`) and K12.4.
- **Fresh deletions** (decided 2026-10-27). The conditions above are read from this device's tracking, which another device's new name for the content reaches only through a fetch. So a blob's deletion-due mark records the instant of the drained fetch whose `RunCleanup` computed it (K10.3), and the batch builder puts the deletion in a batch only while the device clock reads at least that instant and less than 1 h after it, and, decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4), only while the conditions above that a later fetch can change still hold when it builds the batch (no name in the sense of the first bullet, no inbox entry or `refused` row, no apply intent, as the first bullet says). Otherwise the builder skips it, and the next update (K12.4) removes it from the engine's pending changes and clears the mark: its condition no longer holds (`sync/policy#P10.3`). A later `RunCleanup` computes it again from a newer fetch. So a device that goes offline after it computed a deletion never sends it later from knowledge that may be out of date, and a device that named the content meanwhile reads the blob again after the deletion can have landed (K9.3, "Stale `present`", case 3).
- A device that holds a live media-name entity whose blob record is deleted (server state `deleted`, K9.3; a `present` state it may hold from before is read again as K9.3 "Stale `present`" says) uploads the blob again from its local file when the file has that content. Otherwise the name is reported as missing media (`mediaMissing`).

## K10. Local tracking store

### K10.1 File and ownership

- One SQLite file, `meguri-cloud.db`, in the collection directory (app-private Application Support, KTD12). It is never attached to the collection, never part of the derived store and never in a backup of the collection.
- The Rust core (meguri-sync) owns the file and its schema. The Swift CloudKit actor passes CloudKit facts to the core as opaque values: record system fields (from `encodeSystemFields`), engine state serializations, fetched records and save results. The core commits them together with the related tracking changes in one transaction. Engine state, inbox, acknowledgements and conflict copies therefore commit atomically with each other; only the collection file is separate (K13.2).
- Nothing for CloudKit is added to `collection.anki2`: no table, column, index, trigger or config key (KTD25). A local file with any tracking state still opens in Anki unchanged.
- Commits that record server facts (inbox rows, engine state, acknowledgements, sent marks, conflict copies, generation states, apply and re-key intents) are durable before any step that depends on them (K12, K13). Local dirty marks (K11.1) may commit without a full file sync; the reconcile scan covers their loss (K11.4).
- Tracking never records a fact that depends on a collection commit that is not flushed (K1): records are built only from flushed rows (K12.1), and an apply is marked done only after its commit is flushed (K13.2). A power loss can therefore lose collection commits, never tracking facts about them.
- A missing or unreadable tracking file is never rebuilt by inferring acknowledgements: the device treats it as a new connection to iCloud, which needs an explicit collection choice (R2055, `sync/cloudkit-recovery`).

### K10.2 Binding

- A binding row holds: a binding id (16 random bytes) and its creation instant; CloudKit environment, container identifier, Apple account identity (an opaque value from CloudKit), epoch id; the epoch's record key (K4.1, from the `MGEpoch` it published or adopted); the local collection's publication identity (`engine/collection-replace#R6.1`); state `active` or `isolated`, and, while isolated, the isolation reason (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.2`, which gives the reasons and which one replaces another; `environment_changed` is derived and never stored); and the media and recovery facts K10.3 lists. Every table of K10.3 is keyed by binding except `media_hashes` (a cache of the media folder, shared by all bindings) and `recovery_ops` (its rows name the bindings before and after, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.3`, and, decided 2026-10-27, review round 4, the container and environment of the build that created them: a build counts, settles, cancels and runs only the rows of its own container and environment, never writes the bindings another build's row names, and leaves that row to its own build, V2.3 "Rows of another build"). What isolates a binding on account change is `sync/cloudkit-recovery` (R2055).
- At most one binding is active per container and environment (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V9.1`). Builds that use separate containers can share one data directory, and so one tracking file; each has at most one active binding of its own. "The binding" of `sync/policy` is the active binding of the running build's container and environment when one exists, and otherwise the newest of its bindings by creation instant; a salvage binding (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V8.1`) is never it. Decided 2026-10-27: so a binding that a Resume makes active again (V9.2) is the one the policy runs, also when a newer binding of another account exists.
- A binding is **usable** only while all hold: it is `active`; the open collection's publication identity equals the binding's; `cloudPublicationPending` is false (`engine/collection-replace#R6.1`); the running build's container and environment are the binding's. Otherwise nothing runs against it: no reconcile scan, no send, no apply, no purge. Its pending rows, inbox and conflict copies are kept for `sync/cloudkit-recovery`.
- **The account check** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4). Usable reads stored state only, never the live account, so dirty marks (K11.1) and the feed consume follow it alone. The reconcile scan at open runs at `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.4` step 9, after step 8's intents, which wait for the verified identity; until that scan has committed, a close records `scan_state` not drained (K11.4; decided 2026-10-27, P24 review). So edits made before this process has read the account identity, or while the status is `temporarilyUnavailable`, are tracked, and a clean close records them. A send, an apply, a purge, an own operation and an engine instance also need the account status `available` with the binding's identity verified in this process (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V9.1`, V3.4, V26.1 condition 3; `sync/policy#P6`). Marks recorded for a binding that V9.1 then isolates cost nothing: they are local facts, and its restore runs the scan (V2.2).
- A binding of another container or environment is invisible to the running build: the build never starts an engine for it, isolates it, or writes any of its rows (V9.1, V22 invariant 13). Outside `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V17`'s local conflict-copy listing and V8.1's storage-only salvage below, only two reads see it: the feed floor (K10.3), which counts every active binding's consumed counter, and the derived `environment_changed` reason of the status (`sync/policy#P14.2`), when this build has no binding of its own.
- **Storage-only salvage exception (2026-10-28).** Only `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V8.1` may read a damaged source's conflicts and the binding metadata needed for their provenance across all build scopes. The source stays read-only except for V8.1's file moves and finalization. Salvage may insert copies under new, permanently isolated `tracking_damaged` bindings, one per original-binding group in each source, even when the preserved scope belongs to another build. Unreadable identity fields are absent in salvage-only metadata: the complete ordinary-binding identity above is not required, and no current-build/account value or generated epoch replaces a missing fact. A salvage binding never resumes or becomes the policy's binding. No ordinary binding, acknowledgement, engine state, pending work or recovery operation is reconstructed. When an older source is salvaged into a healthy current file, all existing bindings and rows stay unchanged; duplicate copy ids are skipped without reparenting. This grants no adapter, server fact or policy action ordinary cross-build access and permits no account handling, isolation, cancellation or work on another build's ordinary bindings. V22 invariant 13 continues to apply to account and service conditions.
- So a restore, a `.colpkg` import, a manual Anki import (FullDownload) or a switch back to an account whose local collection was replaced never sends the new collection's differences as edits or deletions to the old epoch (R1548, R2056).

## K11. Local changes

### K11.1 Dirty marks

- A tracking observer (an in-core `ChangeObserver`, `engine/change-stream#S2`) runs for every committed op. For each entity the op changed it computes the new row digest (absent for a removed row) and, if it differs from the last observed one, increments `local_rev`, takes a new `change_seq` and records the digest. A first-seen entity gets a random `origin`.
- It records marks only for the usable binding (K10.2). An isolated binding gets none, so edits committed while a binding is isolated are not in its tracking; the path that makes it active again runs the reconcile scan first (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.2`, "Restoring a binding"; K11.4).
- For a `CloudIncoming` op (K13) it records the op's seq in the apply intent and marks no key of the intent dirty, whether the key is an `entry` or a `side_effect` (K13.2): the settle makes `side_effect` keys pending as local changes after the WAL flush. The core also records the seq from the op's result, since the observer does not run for an empty change set (K13.2). For a `CloudRekey` op (K14) it records the seq in the re-key intent and marks the rewritten reference rows dirty (K14.2).
- Media-name entities get dirty marks from the media journal feed (K9.4). A feed item is consumed only after its result commits in tracking; the consumed counter (`media_feed`, K10.3) commits in the same transaction.
- **When the feed is read** (decided 2026-10-27). Tracking consumes the feed up to the journal's current counter, for the usable binding only (K10.2), at these points and no others (nothing polls, `sync/policy#P2`): after every media store operation or registration that commits a feed item, through the store's feed hook (`media/media-files#M16`, "Feed hook": `consume_media_feed`, called after the journal commit, with M16's list and M7.2); at open, once the binding's reconcile scan, or its setup as a new consumer (K10.3, feed floor), has committed; at `resume()`, after its external-change checks (`engine/suspend`); and at the Sync Now capture, before it reads B (`sync/policy#P12.2`). A consume commit that makes a dirty mark is a local change (`sync/policy#P1`, P3 `TrackingChanged{cause: local}`), like the marks of a local op: so a new picture starts its blob read round and arms the flush timer (P10.1), and a Sync Now selects a removal made before it was pressed. Items that are a media apply's own (K9.3) make no dirty mark, so their commit is not a local change.
- A tracking observer run that fails or panics (S2 lets the commit stand), its dirty-mark commit included, sets an in-memory **scan required** flag. While it is set, no send and no apply runs, `scan_state` is not recorded as drained, and a reconcile scan (K11.4) starts, except during a tracking stop: then it starts when the stop clears (K11.4, "Failed commits"). The flag clears when that scan's results have committed.

### K11.2 Base capture

- When an entity becomes pending while it was clean (its row digest before the op equals the acked row digest), the observer stores the op's before-image, as a payload, as the entity's base. `engine/change-stream#S2.4` gives the tracking observer the before-images of notes, decks, presets, notetypes (with their `fields` and `templates` rows), tags, cards, review-log rows and config values.
- Tracking keeps the acked payload of every acked entity (K10.3), not only of media names. Media names take their base from it.
- A pending entity keeps its base until it is clean again. After a merge the base becomes the server version merged with, in the tracking transaction that settles the apply after the WAL flush (K12.3, K13.2, K15), never earlier.
- Decided 2026-10-27 (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.2`, "The base of those edits"): a pending entity that holds no captured base merges with its acked payload as base. That covers edits committed while its binding was isolated, which the observer did not see (K11.1), and a base lost in a crash, which is not recreated. A clean entity's before-image equals its acked content, so the acked payload is the content those edits started from: the merge takes from L only the parts L changed, and a part another device changed meanwhile is kept. Only an entity that was never acked has no base (K15.1).

### K11.3 Anki columns

- Local changes keep Anki's stamping: `usn` -1 and the modification time (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#S2`). CloudKit tracking never reads `usn` as its pending marker and never writes `mod`, `mtime_secs`, `col.usn`, `col.ls` or `col.scm` for its own bookkeeping.
- `usn` is not in payloads (K6). A `CloudIncoming` op writes each changed or new row with the column values of the payload, modification time included, and `usn` -1, so the next manual Anki transfer from this device sends it (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#S2`). It sets `col.mod` to now when it changes something (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#S5`) and leaves `col.usn` and `col.ls` alone. It sets `col.scm` only when it applies a structural generation that changes local structure, as `notetypes/schema-changes` G1 says (`cloudkit_structure_applied`), so the next manual Anki transfer is one-way.
- A manual Anki transfer that rewrites only `usn` values changes no row digest, so it sends nothing to CloudKit. Manual transfers keep their own state; CloudKit never resolves or reads it (KTD26).

### K11.4 Reconcile scan

The scan makes tracking reflect the collection when the observer may have missed commits. It runs only against a usable binding (K10.2).

- At open, before the writer admits ops, tracking durably clears `scan_state`'s drained flag. At close and suspend, after the writer stops admitting ops, when the scan-required flag (K11.1) is clear and no reconcile scan this open needs is still waiting or running (the scan at open below, which runs at `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.4` step 9 behind steps 7 and 8, or one after an external change or a restore), tracking records `scan_state` durably before the sidecar records the clean shutdown. Otherwise the drained flag stays cleared, so the next open scans (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review: a dirty mark lost in a crash is covered only by a scan that has run).
- The scan runs at collection open unless all hold: the sidecar reports a clean shutdown (`storage/durability-and-files#F3`), and `scan_state` was drained at the last close and records the opened file's `col.mod`, `col.scm` and file identity.
- The **file identity** is that of `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V8.2`: the file system's device id, the inode number and the creation time (`st_birthtime`) of `collection.anki2`. Tracking records it at each drained close and suspend, and at the other commits V8.2 lists (a binding created for the open file, a resume, a completed adoption). A recorded identity that differs from the opened file's is V8.2's `file_mismatch`, not a reason to scan: the binding is isolated and no scan runs against it.
- It also runs after an external change is detected (F3), when the scan-required flag is set (K11.1), when publishing reaches K18.3 step 2, and when an isolated binding becomes active again (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.2`, "Restoring a binding", which sets the flag).
- It reads every entity on a reader snapshot, off the writer, and compares row digests with tracking. Dirty marks of ops committed after the snapshot take precedence. Sending waits for the scan; study does not.
- Each entity the scan makes pending, or finds pending with another digest, takes a new `change_seq` (K10.3).
- **Failed commits** (decided 2026-10-27). Any tracking commit that fails reports `TrackingWriteFailed` (`sync/policy#P3`; `noSpace` for SQLite full or `ENOSPC`), whichever writer made it: a dirty mark (K11.1), a scan result, an inbox insert or an acknowledgement. That sets the tracking stop (`sync/policy#P15.2`). A scan whose commit fails ends at once; the scan-required flag stays set, and the scan does not restart by itself. It starts again only when the tracking stop clears (a successful probe, or a local change whose commit succeeded, P15.2), and it runs before the engine starts again. So a full disk never makes the scan read the collection in a loop, and the closed gate always has an attention item (`localStorageFull` or `trackingError`).
- **Adoption window** (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V14.4`, K9.4). While the binding's window is open, the scan classifies every media name by what the adopted epoch holds, as V14.4's table says, and never compares a name as a local change: clean, `superseded`, `file_missing`, `withheld`, or no entity. The window closes in the first tracking commit, at or after the binding's first scan, that consumes the feed while `media/media-journal#J6` reports it complete; the window is durable, so a crash or a relaunch keeps it open.
- After the window, a `superseded` or `withheld` entity keeps its flag while its local facts equal its classified digest. Other facts lift it as K9.4 says; the scan never takes a `superseded` file as L.

| present | tombstone (acked) | id-keyed kind: re-key (K14.3). Name-keyed kind: pending re-creation with the tombstone as base |
| absent | live | pending tombstone; for a media name only when the absence cause is `removed`, otherwise a missing local file (K9.4). Cause `removed` while J6 reports the feed not complete gives the mark `removal_held` instead (K9.4). A `superseded` name becomes a missing local file whatever the cause; a `withheld` one follows K9.4 (cause `removed` drops the flag, `missing` keeps it) |
| absent | none, or tombstone | nothing, except a media name that K18.3 step 2's scan carries over from the binding before (K9.4, "Carried names") |

For media names the local row is the journal record of the name's case key, and "absent" follows the absence cause the journal reports (`media/media-journal#J6`); a name with no record and no retained removal item reads as `missing`.

## K12. Sending and acknowledgement

### K12.1 Building a record

An entity is **sendable** when all hold:

1. it is pending, and not oversize, unreadable, unsyncable, `file_missing`, `withheld`, `superseded` or `removal_held` (K9.4), or format-blocked. An entity with one of the flags `file_missing`, `withheld` and `superseded` is not pending for the policy either (`sync/policy#P3`); one with `removal_held` is pending and blocked until registration completes;
2. `sync/policy` makes it eligible (`sync/policy#P7`). The policy supplies the image to send: the live row, the held-back earlier image (R2047), or nothing (deferred);
3. its binding is usable and passes the account check (K10.2), and no scan is running or required (K11.1, K11.4); and, for a collection-zone record, the bound epoch is confirmed for the current engine instance: a drained fetch of `meguri-root` by that instance confirmed it, a `root_read` result accepted for that instance confirmed it, or the publisher's `done` commit confirmed it for that instance (K2 "Confirmed root", `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.2`, V26.1), except for the binding of a `publish` op while the op's step is before `done` and is not `cancelling` (the exemption ends at `cancelling`, and the core then builds no batch for that binding, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2`). Until that confirmation, the builder returns no collection record, whatever the engine schedules. Decided 2026-10-27 (`sync/policy#P6`): the builder also returns no record at all while iCloud is not enabled or sync is paused on this device, and none to an engine instance that is stopping (from the input that closes the gate in a P6 row whose engine is stopped, until its `EngineStopped`), so nothing more is sent after Pause or an account loss, whatever the engine asks for before its stop completes (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V22` invariant 4). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): zone deletions never pass the builder, so this rule cannot hold them; they are `DeleteZones` own operations, whose `MGActive` read and guard check hold them back instead (`sync/policy#P4`, `#P10.3`), and the full update removes every pending database change from the engine (K12.4);
4. the commit that last wrote its rows is flushed (K1);
5. a live version only: every entity it references (K6) is acked live on the server or is sent in the same atomic batch (batch rules below). An entity whose reference is held (oversize, unreadable, not eligible, waiting) is held too, and status lists it;
6. a live note only: every media name its fields reference (`text/media-references`) whose local entity has never been acked is held back with it until that name is acked (K15.9). A name flagged `withheld` holds back no note (K9.4): it is never acked, and a note that already referenced it is the epoch's own. A local op that adds a reference to it releases it (K9.4 lift 2), so it is then pending and holds the note as any unacked name;
7. it does not depend on a local structural generation that is not claimed (K19.7 `local`, `claiming`, `lost`; a `lost` one until it is `rolled_back`, review of the P24 corrections): it is not a member or anchor of one, and it is not a note or card of a notetype that such a generation changes (K19.2); and it is not an anchor, or a note or card of an anchor's notetype, that K19.8 holds until the restoring generation is adopted;
8. no server version of it is in the inbox, whatever the entry's reason (`ready` included, so a save conflict's server version is applied first, K12.3; and `generation`, `format`, K5.4), and no apply intent names it (K13.2); decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): and no `refused` row of the binding holds its record name (K20, fetched or met in a save conflict, K12.3), so no local version overwrites a server version this build refuses. Such an entity counts as blocked (`sync/policy#P12.4`) under the `refused` attention item (`sync/policy#P14.4`). The row is deleted in the K13.1 step 2 transaction that accepts a fetched version of that name that is not stale against the row (K13.1), or a record deletion of that name, and the entity then follows this rule as usual.

- At batch build time the core builds the envelope: `erev` = server `erev` + 1 (1 for a new record), the entity's origin, `writer` = the binding id, `base_sha256` = the acked payload digest when the entity has been acked (absent otherwise), the generation id while the entity is a member of a generation that is not yet complete on the server (K19.3), and the acked system fields when the entity has been acked.
- Before it hands a batch to the engine, tracking durably sets `sent` and adds to the entity's in-flight list an entry with the record's `local_rev`, its payload digest and payload, and the image seq and held flag the policy gave for its image (`sync/policy#P7.2`).
- The in-flight list is not a send state (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P1` "In flight", `#P3` `sendable`). No rule above reads it: an entity whose record was handed and never saved (a kill after its sent mark, a cancelled send, a failed save, a lost result) stays sendable while it is pending, and the next update hands its name again (K12.4). The list keeps each handed revision only for K12.3's in-flight digest row and `sync/policy#P12.4`'s image seq. Its entries leave at "Saved" (K12.3: that entry and every older one), and all of them leave in the K13.2 settle transaction in which an `entry` entity takes an applied server version's acked fields: every handed record was built on the older change tag, so none can be the server's version any more except by equal content. Decided 2026-10-27: an entity that becomes clean through a local change (an undo, a scan that finds the acked content again, K11.4) keeps its entries, because a save whose result was lost may have landed: a later save conflict then still meets the in-flight digest row and counts as saved, so the newer local content is sent over it and the undone change does not come back from the server. Decided 2026-10-27: a fetch that delivers that landed version meets the same entries: K13.2 records it as "Saved" (K12.3) and never applies it, so the clean entity becomes pending with that version as base, and its local content is sent over it. Decided 2026-10-27: an entry matches only a version whose `writer` is this binding (K5.1), so another device's version with the same content is never taken for this device's own and is merged as usual; and a version another device built on the landed save (its `base_sha256` equals an entry's digest) merges with that entry's payload as base (K13.2, "Built on an own landed save"), so the undone change does not come back through it either. Entries are kept with no time limit: only a version this binding wrote can match one.
- Order within the collection zone: a generation's claim batch first (K19.3); then live records of notetypes, presets, decks (parents before children), media names, notes, cards, revlog, tags, config; then tombstones of cards, notes, decks (children before parents), presets, notetypes, revlog, tags, config, media names. Blob records go to the media zone after collection records: across requests, and within one request whose scope includes both zones (`sync/policy#P13.1`). A request scoped to one zone holds only that zone's records (below).
- **Send scope** (documented in the SDK headers, 2026-10-27: a batch that includes a change outside the scope of the send context's options makes the send fail with `invalidArguments`). The builder reads the scope of each batch request (its zone ids, excluded zone ids and record ids) and puts in the batch only records and deletions within it. A sendable name outside the scope stays pending and in the engine's pending list for a later request. So a `RequestSend{collection}` never carries a blob record or a media-zone deletion.
- **Batches** (decided 2026-10-27). Every batch stays within K7's request budget, and the core builds each one itself (the engine's batch initializer with records and an atomic flag; the engine applies no size limit to it). Ordinary batches are not atomic (`atomicByZone` false), so one failing record never fails the others. A batch is atomic only when it is a claim batch (K19.3) or holds a **reference group**: a live record sent together with entities it references that are not yet acked live (rule 5), with their own unacked references in turn. A reference group is never split across batches, and an atomic batch holds only whole groups. A group that does not fit one batch is not sent as a group: its referenced entities go first in ordinary batches, and the records that reference them wait under rule 5 until they are acked. So a card is never saved without its note, whichever batch fails.

### K12.2 Save policy

Every save is conditional on the server's change tag of the version this device last acked (CloudKit's default save policy for records with system fields; a new record without system fields). Meguri never uses a policy that overwrites the server version unconditionally.

**Assumption A5**: saving a new record (no system fields) whose name exists fails with a server-record-changed error that carries the server record; saving with system fields for a record that no longer exists fails with an unknown-item error and does not create it.

Only an S that K20 accepts reaches the rows above.

When S's payload is in `pa` (or a manifest's member list in `ma`), the entry still goes into the inbox in the transaction that records the result, with reason `asset`, and its `nt_struct_gen` check and its apply wait for the payload. A send fails with `zoneNotFound`, whose user info says whether the user reset encrypted data, or with `userDeletedZone`. K12.5 maps both.

### K12.4 Engine pending changes

- Tracking is the only source of pending work. The sync engine's pending record-zone changes are a copy of it that may be stale after a crash (R2051).
- The **full update** (`sync/policy#P4` `UpdatePending{full}`): the adapter adds to the engine's pending changes the record name of every sendable entity, the record name of every blob ready to upload (K9.3; none while media is off or `media_zone_missing` is set), the control records that are due (K12.6), and the record deletions that `RunCleanup` marked due (`sync/policy#P10.3`: tombstones with `purge_due`, blobs with deletion due). It keeps each due control record and each due record deletion until its result. With auto off the engine sends none of them by itself: Sync Now sends them, the due blob deletions through `ResumeMedia` (`sync/policy#P13.1` step 4). It removes every other engine pending name. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4, `sync/policy#P4`): it adds no zone deletion and removes every pending database change (a zone save or deletion) the engine holds, also one restored from a saved engine state. The engine sends a pending database change by itself, with no batch request, so no gate, send scope or root confirmation could hold one back. The due zone deletions of `zones` rows (`sync/policy#P10.3` item 3) are `DeleteZones` own operations (`sync/policy#P4`, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), never engine work; a cancelled publication's zone deletion stays due until its result, with no condition that an update could find false (K18.5). It hands back the names the engine dropped after a failure (parked, `sync/policy#P1`).
- It runs where `sync/policy` emits it, and nowhere else (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P2`, `#P4`): `StartEngine` (P4), P9, P10.1, P11, P12.3 step 4, and P13.1's `ResumeMedia` step 3 and `PauseMedia`. Those are an engine start, the gate opening (the end of a reconcile scan included), a local change, a foreground return, Sync Now, an engine account event, a drained fetch of the collection zone, `RootConfirmed` (the first confirmation of the bound epoch for an engine instance, by a drained root fetch or a `root_read` result, `sync/policy#P3`), `SessionEnded`, `DeviceSettings{automatic: on}`, a media start or pause, and the background and quit flushes. The policy's rows decide; this list only names them. Never at an `EngineError`, an `EngineActivity` or a `VolumeSpaceAvailable` report.
- Between full updates, `UpdatePending{changes}` (at a tracking commit that is not a local change) adds a name when its entity becomes sendable, its blob ready, its control record or its deletion due, and removes a name that stops being sendable. It never adds a parked or deferred name, so a failed save is not handed back by its own result. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): it also adds again the name of an entity that a Saved result (K12.3, its in-flight digest row and K13.2's own landed saves included) left pending and sendable, since the engine drops the name of every record it saved; so an edit made while an older revision was in flight is handed with no other trigger. Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review; order added in the review of the P24 corrections): U114 runs the whole `UpdatePending{changes}` that a send's result commit emits (its adds and its removals), and the add-backs of "Failed saves" below, only after the handling of that sent-record-zone-changes event has returned, never inside it (as K12.3's save-conflict read, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V3.3`). It runs every `UpdatePending` of an instance, full or changes, in the order the core emitted them: a later one waits until each deferred one before it has run. So a deferred add never overtakes a later removal or a later deletion add (SDK headers: the engine discards a pending delete when a save of the same record is added after it). The engine removes a saved change and drops a failed one while it handles that event, and it deduplicates added changes. So a change added inside the event, while the saved change is still listed, could be merged into it and removed with it (**Assumption A23**, K23).
- **Failed saves** (documented in the SDK headers, 2026-10-27). The engine keeps a failed save in its pending list only for the errors it retries itself: `notAuthenticated`, `accountTemporarilyUnavailable`, `networkFailure`, `networkUnavailable`, `requestRateLimited`, `serviceUnavailable`, `zoneBusy`. It drops the name on every other error. Decided 2026-10-27: after each sent-record-zone-changes event, once K12.3 has run for every record of the batch, the adapter adds back at once the names that failed with `batchRequestFailed` or with `limitExceeded` in a batch of more than one record (K12.3: the next batch is built at the halved budget); for a claim batch, the manifest only while it is due (K12.6) and never an anchor by itself (K12.3; review of the P24 corrections); and a name whose `serverRecordChanged` result left it sendable. After a server-record-changed merge the entity is sendable again only once its inbox entry is applied (K12.1 rule 8); it is then added as any entity that becomes sendable. Names that failed with `quotaExceeded`, an asset error or any other error are not added back at once: the next full update adds them, at an event trigger, with no timer (`sync/policy#P2`). An unknown-item failure of a save follows K17.4.
- **Deletion results** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4, `sync/policy#P10.3`). A due record deletion that succeeds, or that meets unknown item (another device deleted the record first), is a deletion result: the tombstoned entity is dropped (K17.4), or the blob's server state becomes `deleted` (K9.3, K9.5); its due mark leaves with it, and it is not handed again. Unknown item on a deletion is never K17.4's absence and makes no `deletion` inbox entry. Any other failure leaves it due, as the bullet above says for its error.
- When the engine asks for the next batch, the core builds the envelopes then (K12.1) and skips names that are no longer sendable (clean, re-keyed, held, tombstone already acked) and names outside the request's scope (K12.1, "Send scope").
- So a crash between a dirty mark and the engine's pending add loses nothing: the next engine start adds it again.
- **Handed, never saved** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P1`, `#P3` `sendable`). Neither form of update removes the name of a pending entity because it was handed to the engine or holds in-flight entries (K10.3), and a record handed and never saved (a kill after its sent mark, V4 S5; a cancelled send; a failed save; a send that ended with no result for it) is handed again: `UpdatePending{changes}` never removes its name while it is pending, and the next full update (an engine start, an event trigger) adds it when the engine no longer holds it. A parked name waits for that full update (above).

### K12.5 Zone loss (decided 2026-10-27)

A device that only fetches learns of zone loss from a fetched zone deletion; a device that sends learns it from a send error. Both map to one outcome, by zone and cause. Before it isolates, the actor reads the account status and identity again (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.2`).

| Zone | Cause | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| any Meguri zone | fetched reason `encryptedDataReset`, or `zoneNotFound` with the encrypted-data-reset flag | isolate `encrypted_data_reset`: an encrypted data reset is account-wide (V10.1) |
| the bound collection zone, or `meguri-root` | fetched reason `purged`, or `userDeletedZone` | isolate `zone_deleted_by_user` (V10.1) |
| the bound collection zone | fetched reason `deleted`, or plain `zoneNotFound` | `MGActive` is read again ("The `MGActive` read after a lost collection zone", below): when it names another epoch, isolate `epoch_switched` (K18.4); otherwise isolate `zone_missing` (V10.1) |
| `meguri-root` | fetched reason `deleted`, or plain `zoneNotFound`; or a fetch reports the `MGActive` record deleted | isolate `active_missing` (V10.2), except the binding of a `publish` op whose step is before `done`: it is not isolated and saves `MGActive` itself (K2 "Confirmed root", `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`) |
| `meguri-media` | fetched reason `deleted` or `purged`, `userDeletedZone`, or plain `zoneNotFound` | no isolation; media work stops for this binding for good, and collection sync continues (K2, V10.1). A purge of all of Meguri's data also deletes the collection zone, whose row isolates |
| another `c-` zone | any | no isolation; it is not this binding's (K18.5). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4, `sync/policy#P10.3`): a fetched zone deletion of it, whatever its reason (also one that the first row isolates for), deletes that zone's `zones` row, its due mark included, in the K13.1 step 2 transaction that records the fetch, so the row of a zone another device deleted never stays and K9.5's "no other epoch zone is retained" becomes true again once each retained zone is gone |

- **The `MGActive` read after a lost collection zone** (decided 2026-10-27). The read of the bound collection zone's row is the bound binding's `MGActive` read of `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`: the core reports `RootReadDue{instance}` for the engine instance that met the loss, and the result comes back through `root_read`, accepted only from that instance (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V3.4`). An `MGActive` that names another epoch is recorded as the binding's last applied `MGActive` and isolates `epoch_switched`. Unknown item or zone not found is the `meguri-root` row (`active_missing`). Every other result isolates `zone_missing` at once, with nothing confirmed: an `MGActive` that names the bound epoch (the zone is gone all the same), and a read that fails (offline, any error). When the instance ends before its result is accepted, the core isolates `zone_missing` as it handles that end. So a device that is offline at that moment is isolated with no wait, and `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.1` still offers V14 from a fresh read (V2.2). The binding of a `publish` op whose step is before `done` gets no such read (K2): this row isolates it `zone_missing` whatever `MGActive` names, since before its switch `MGActive` names an older epoch by design. That covers the zone-not-found result of K18.3 step 5's pre-switch read (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`).
- No row deletes the local collection or its pending rows. Apple asks an app to delete locally cached data and not resend it after `purged` and `encryptedDataReset`. Decided 2026-10-27: Meguri's local collection is the user's own data, not a cache, so it stays and nothing is sent again without the user's explicit V15 choice. What tracking holds only as a copy of the server (the binding's inbox entries that no apply intent names, their staged files, and `refused` rows) is deleted with the isolation. Pending rows, intents and conflict copies are kept (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V1`).
- No row creates a zone again (K2).
- **A zone missing from a first fetch** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P3` `FetchDrained`, A7). For an engine instance created with no saved state, a zone of its scope that its first fetch did not fetch is only inferred to be gone, and an inference never isolates. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 3): the same holds after each engine account event of the current instance, `signIn` with the binding's identity included, since the engine may then have reset its change tokens (A12): each is a **no-state point** (`sync/policy#P3`), and "first fetch" here means the first `fetchChanges` call that returns after the latest no-state point and names the zone, one running at that point included (P24 review), with no zone fetch of the instance that started after that point having fetched it (a zone fetch started after the point when U114 handled its will-fetch-record-zone-changes event after it; review of the P24 corrections). Until a read decides (a record, unknown item or zone not found) or a zone fetch that started after the point drains it with `fetched` true, U114 makes it again at each later `fetchChanges` call of that instance that counts as a read and did not fetch the zone (`sync/policy#P3`). - Decided 2026-10-27 (R2066 approval): a `publish` op creates its zones and saves `MGEpoch`, `MGMediaRoot` and `MGActive` with own operations (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), and the new binding's engine instance starts only once the op's step is `zones` (V15.2), so the engine never meets a zone of that op before it exists. A zone-not-found result of an own operation maps through this table as V26.1 says, with V26.1's one exception for `meguri-root` at the `MGActive` save.

### K12.6 Control records (decided 2026-10-27)

Records that are not entities and that the engine sends are **control records**. The engine sends only what is in its pending lists, so each must stay there until its result, or the step that needs it stalls with no error. Tracking derives which control records are due from rows it already keeps durably:

| Control record | Due while | Source row |
|---|---|---|
| `MGGeneration` with state `claimed`, with its claim batch (K19.3 step 1) | decided 2026-10-27: the generation is the first, in claim order (K19.3), of the binding's local generations in state `local` or `claiming` (K19.7), and it is not stuck (K19.4); decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): and no anchor of it is named by an apply intent or has an inbox entry, except, while the generation is `claiming`, an entry that waits with reason `generation` (K12.1 rule 8, K19.4 row 3; decided 2026-10-27, P24 review: such a version may be built on this claim if it landed, so the resend's manifest decides, K19.4 "Claim landed"), no `refused` row of the binding holds an anchor's record name (K12.1 rule 8; P24 review), and none is held by K19.8 | `generations`, `inbox`, `apply_intents` |
| `MGGeneration` with state `complete` (K19.3 step 3) | the generation is `claimed` and every member is acked | `generations`, `entities` |

- Removed (2026-10-27): the rows for the zone saves of `meguri-root`, `meguri-media` and `c-<epoch id>`, for `MGEpoch`, `MGActive` and `MGMediaRoot`, and for a manifest with state `abandoned`. Decided 2026-10-27 (R2066 approval, with `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`): these are U114's own operations, outside the engine, with V26.1's conditions and outcomes; K12.3's rows never apply to them. The engine's send path carries only `MGEntity`, `MGGeneration` (states `claimed` and `complete`) and `MGBlob` records and the deletions `RunCleanup` marks due. So a `publish` op's zones and `MGEpoch` exist before its engine instance starts (V15.2, step `zones`), and no engine send waits on a zone that does not exist yet.
- The full update adds every due control record and keeps it until its result; neither form of `UpdatePending` removes it while it is due (K12.4). A crash loses nothing: the next engine start's full update derives them again from the source rows. A control record the engine dropped after a failure is parked like an entity (`sync/policy#P1`) and handed back by the next full update at an event trigger. No timer runs.
- When the engine asks for a batch, the builder builds a due control record from tracking at that moment, within the request's scope (K12.1), and skips one that is no longer due. In the collection zone a claim batch goes first (K12.1 order). A claim batch holds the manifest with its anchors, which rules 7 and 8 of K12.1 otherwise keep unsendable; the builder builds a claim batch only whole, every anchor included (review of the P24 corrections), whatever the halved record bound (K12.3 "Limit exceeded", fourth review of the P24 items).
- **New zones.** Removed (2026-10-27): no engine send creates a zone (above).
- **Failed zone saves.** Removed (2026-10-27): zone creation is an own operation (V26.1).
- Failed saves of control records follow K12.3 and stay due: a claim follows K19.4; a `complete` save that meets server record changed follows K19.4 ("Complete landed"), and one whose server manifest is `abandoned` follows K19.8, as `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18` says for a device whose own generation was abandoned.

## K13. Incoming records

### K13.1 Order

For each fetched batch, in this order:

1. Decode and check every record (K20). A permanently refused record goes to `refused`. A record with a retryable problem goes to the inbox with reason `asset`.
2. In one durable tracking transaction: insert every accepted `MGEntity` record into `inbox` (entry kind `record`), its asset moved into the cloud staging directory (an asset the actor already staged while the core was suspended, `sync/policy#P11.2`, is recorded at its staged path, not moved again); record a fetched `MGActive` version or deletion as the binding's last applied `MGActive` (K2), and the `crt` of a fetched `MGEpoch` of the bound epoch as the binding's epoch `crt` (K10.3); insert every record deletion the batch reports as an entry of kind `deletion` (record name, reason `ready`), unless neither an entity nor an inbox entry of the binding holds that record name; write the `refused` rows of step 1, and delete the `refused` row of a record name whose accepted version that is not stale (below, against the `refused` row's own `erev` too), or whose deletion, the batch delivers (K12.1 rule 8); a `refused` row whose envelope did not decode, or whose origin differs from the accepted version's, is deleted only by a deletion or by an accepted version whose `erev` is above the entity's acked server `erev` (review of the P24 corrections); and, while an absence pass of the zone runs, set `seen_pass` of every entity whose record name the batch delivered, as a record or a deletion, and record the pass id on every inbox entry the step inserts (K17.4).
3. Only after step 2 commits may an engine state that includes the batch's cursor be saved. A cursor never outruns the inbox, for records and record deletions alike (R2051).
4. Apply ready inbox entries (K13.2). Entries waiting for a generation, a reference, a structure, a newer format or an asset stay in the inbox.

- **Control records** (decided 2026-10-27). Only `MGEntity` records become `ready` inbox entries. A fetched `MGActive` or `MGEpoch` updates `bindings` only, as step 2 says. A fetched `MGGeneration` is recorded in `generations` in the step 2 transaction (its manifest and state, K19.5, K19.7). Decided 2026-10-27: one whose generation id is a local generation of the binding in state `local` or `claiming` is this device's claim that landed with its result lost (only this device makes that id, K19.2): the same transaction records it as K19.4's "Claim landed" says, and its anchors, when fetched, are this device's own landed saves (K13.2). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): one in state `complete` for a local generation in state `claimed` is recorded as K19.4's "Complete landed" says, and one in state `abandoned` for a local generation is recorded as K19.8 says. One whose `ma` fails K20's asset check waits in the inbox with reason `asset`, as an entity record does, and once a read passes it is recorded in `generations` and the entry is deleted; it never becomes `ready`. A fetched deletion of a control record makes no `deletion` entry: a deletion of `MGActive` follows K12.5, and a deletion of a manifest deletes its waiting `asset` entry, if any. So no control record leaves a `ready` entry that no apply takes (`sync/policy#P14.2`, `#P12.4`).

- Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): CloudKit does not promise the order of fetched changes, so order reads `erev` (K5.1), never fetch order; while purging is off (K17.4) no `MGEntity` record is deleted, so a record's `erev` only grows. A fetched `record` of the entity's origin whose `erev` is at or below the entity's acked server `erev`, or at or below that of an entry of that name and origin already held, is stale (an older page delivered after a newer version, or the same version again); so is one whose `erev` is at or below the `erev` of a `refused` row of that name and origin (decided 2026-10-27, review of the P24 corrections: the server already holds the refused version, so an older one is not its current state): it is not inserted, it is logged, and step 2 still sets its `seen_pass` (K17.4). Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): the record that a `RetryAssetEntries` read or K12.3's first read returns for an `asset` entry is not a newly fetched record. With that entry's `erev` it settles that entry in place under step 1, and is never stale: the entry leaves `asset` with the reason K13.1 gives and its staged asset recorded, or stays `asset`, or its `refused` row replaces it. With a higher `erev` it replaces the entry as a newer version. A version with a higher `erev`, or a deletion, replaces an earlier entry of that name that no apply intent names; otherwise it waits behind that entry, and entries of one record name apply in `erev` order (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V5` R2).
- An entry with reason `asset` is read again by record ID, with every field, only at `sync/policy#P4` `RetryAssetEntries`. That action runs at a drained fetch of the bound collection zone (`FetchDrained{collection}`, the drains of Sync Now steps 3 and 5 included), at most once per drain, with no timer (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/policy#P2`, `#P10.1`). Each run reads only the entries whose retry count is below 5, so each entry is read at most 5 times per collection open, whenever it entered the inbox (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review). A foreground return, an account change and Sync Now retry it only through the collection fetch they start. A save conflict's first read (K12.3) is not one of the 5. After the fifth failed attempt it stays in the inbox and status reports needs attention (`assetUnavailable`). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): each collection open resets to 0 the retry count of every entry with reason `asset` (P24 review: also one below 5, so every open gives each entry 5 reads), as K19.4 does for stuck claims, so `RetryAssetEntries` reads it again at the first drained collection fetch after the open, and an entity or a claim that waits on it (K12.1 rule 8, K19.4 row 3) is never held for good by an interruption of the asset service. It never becomes ready without a matching asset. Decided 2026-10-27: the commit in which a read passes and the entry leaves `asset` resets its retry count to 0, so K13.2's count of failed applies starts from 0 (K10.3).
- **Assumption A7**: the sync engine delivers fetched records and record deletions before the engine state that advances past them, and reports when a fetch has drained the zone. Decided 2026-10-27: U114 reports the end of a fetch per zone of its scope, as `sync/policy#P3` `FetchDrained{zone, fetched}` says: for a `fetchChanges` call also for a zone it had nothing to fetch for (`fetched` false), but only for a call that returned with no error after U114 handled, between the call and its return, a will-fetch-changes event of that call and then the did-fetch-changes event that pairs with it (`sync/policy#P3`: reason `manual`, with no fetch open at the call's start). Only such a call counts as a read of the database changes made after it. A call without both events (the engine may short-circuit it, or join a fetch that started before it and send only that fetch's did-fetch-changes event) proves no such read and gives no `FetchDrained`, and `RequestFetch` then calls `fetchChanges` once more (`sync/policy#P4`). `RequestFetch` (`sync/policy#P4`) is one `fetchChanges` call whose scope and priority zones are `meguri-root` then the bound collection zone, so a first fetch from no cursor reads one listing of the database changes for both. That listing names every zone that exists, so a zone of its scope that the call does not fetch is probably gone. It is reported not found only after a record-ID read of its record (`active` for `meguri-root`, `epoch` for the collection zone) returns zone not found, or unknown item for `epoch` (review round 4); then K12.5's plain `zoneNotFound` row applies (K12.5, K17.4). A read that finds the record gives that call a `fetched: false` drain of the zone (K12.5). The signed runs check all of this (K22.3).

### K13.2 Apply

- Before it submits a `CloudIncoming` op, the caller runs the day clock's `checkRollover()` and lets its op commit (`sched/day-clock#D8`), so a new day's unbury happens before another device's `lastUnburied` arrives (D7).
- A `CloudIncoming` op (`engine/op-model#O1`) takes ready entries in K12.1's order (live records, then tombstones and deletions). For each target it computes the current local row digest; when that differs from the acked row digest, the entity is pending with L = the current row, whatever tracking's marks say. The exception is a media-name entity flagged `file_missing`, `superseded` or `withheld`: its local file is not a local change, so K9.4 ("Incoming versions over these flags") decides, with L = its acked version for the first two. A media-name entity marked `removal_held` has L = a local tombstone (K9.4). It merges each entry with local pending changes (K15), using the base the entity holds (K11.2), and writes rows with the resulting values. A `deletion` entry applies as K17.4 says.
- Save-conflict entries (K12.3) apply the same way: the entity keeps its old acked fields and base B until this apply settles, so a replay merges B, L and S again, never S against itself.
- **Own landed saves** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V4` S7). A `record` entry whose envelope has the entity's origin, whose `writer` is this binding (K5.1), and whose payload digest equals one of the entity's in-flight digests (K10.3) is this device's own save that landed with its result lost. Decided 2026-10-27: the `writer` test is needed because another device can build a version with the same payload by itself (a tag's `collapsed`, a config value, an equal stamp); such a version is merged as usual. It is never merged or applied, whether the entity is clean or pending, acked or never acked: the op writes no row for it, and its intent lists it as an `entry` key with equal before and after digests (the current row's). The settle records it as K12.3's "Saved" for that in-flight revision, in place of the first settle bullet below: acked system fields, server `erev`, acked digests and acked payload take the entry's; that entry and every older one leave the in-flight list, and newer ones stay; when the current local row digest equals the entry's row digest the entity is clean, otherwise it stays or becomes pending with the entry's payload as its base and a new `change_seq`, and the local row is sent over it under K12.1. So a change undone after a lost result, or an edit made after it, is never overwritten by the device's own older version, and a never-acked entity makes no `field_conflict` copy of its own earlier save.
- **Built on an own landed save** (decided 2026-10-27). A `record` entry that is not an own landed save, whose `base_sha256` equals one of the entity's in-flight digests, was built by another device on this device's save that landed with its result lost. The op compares and merges against that in-flight entry's payload in place of the acked payload: the entity is pending when its current row digest differs from that payload's row digest, with L = the current row, and the merge uses that payload as B (K15.1), whatever base tracking holds. Nothing in tracking changes before the settle, so a replay computes the same B; the settle then runs as the first settle bullet below (the in-flight list is emptied). So a change undone after the lost result is not brought back by the other device's version, and the other device's own change is kept. This reaches one step: a version built on a version built on the landed save has a `base_sha256` this device never held and merges with the acked base, so the undone change can come back through it. That needs a lost result that landed, a local undo and two saves of other devices before this device fetches; what comes back is this device's own earlier content, and nothing else is lost.
- Before its COMMIT the op durably records an apply intent in tracking: the inbox ids and every key the op writes, each with its expected row digest before and after the op. Decided 2026-10-27: each key has a role. An `entry` key is the entity of one of the op's inbox entries. A `side_effect` key is any other entity the op writes: K13.3's reference results (a card moved to deck 1 or home from a filtered deck), K15.6's recomputed descendant deck names and its cycle and duplicate-name fixes, the cards a note tombstone removes (K17.3), and the notes and cards of a notetype tombstone. For a `side_effect` key whose entity is clean, the intent also holds its before-image as a payload, which becomes its base (K11.2).
- After COMMIT the core records the op's seq, taken from the op's result (`engine/op-model#O4`), in the intent, whether or not the change set is empty. The tracking observer records the same seq when it runs, which it does only for a change set that is not empty (`engine/change-stream#S2`); both writes are idempotent. Decided 2026-10-27: so an apply whose entries all equal the local rows (it writes nothing, `storage/ids-usn-mtime#S4`) still settles. The observer marks no key of the intent dirty, `entry` or `side_effect`; the settle below does what a dirty mark would. A later dirty mark on a key of the intent compares with the intent's after digest.
- Once the writer's flush covers that seq (`storage/open-and-connections#C9.2`; an op that wrote nothing is covered as soon as the commits before it are), one tracking transaction deletes the inbox entries (K10.3), updates their entities and removes the intent:
  - each `entry` entity takes the applied server version's acked system fields, server `erev`, acked digests and acked payload, and its in-flight list is emptied (K12.1). When the entry records the pass id of an absence pass of the zone that still runs, the entity's `seen_pass` takes that id in the same transaction (K17.4); this covers an entity the apply creates, and the apply under K after a re-key (K14.2). When the written row equals that version, the entity is clean and its base is cleared. Otherwise it stays pending: its base becomes that server version (K11.2), and it takes a new `change_seq` (K10.3);
  - each `side_effect` entity becomes pending as a local change: it takes a new `local_rev` and `change_seq` and records its new row digest (K11.1). An absent row makes a pending tombstone (K17.1). A clean entity takes the before-image the intent holds as its base; a pending one keeps its base. So these writes are sent onward (K13.3, K15.6, K17.3), Sync Now selects them (`sync/policy#P12.2`), and status never shows `upToDate` while they wait.
- At open, an intent left by a crash or a power loss is settled before any send, over all its keys, both roles: when every key matches its expected after digest (also when before and after are equal), finish the tracking update above; when every key matches its before digest, the op did not commit or was lost, and the entries are applied again; any other mix runs the reconcile scan (K11.4) and applies the entries again with K15.
- **Failed applies** (decided 2026-10-27). An apply op that rolls back for a reason other than a cancel (an error, a constraint failure such as `idx_decks_name`, a panic) settles its intent at once (`engine/op-model#O1.1`). In that settle transaction its entries take the inbox reason `apply_failed`, with failure `apply` and the error, and their retry count grows by 1 (K10.3). Such an entry is not ready: `sync/policy`'s `ApplyReady` does not take it at the `TrackingChanged` of that settle or of any other tracking commit. It is applied again only at an event trigger (`sync/policy#P2`), in an op of its own (one entry per op; a complete structural generation stays one op, K19.5), after the ready entries, so one entry that cannot apply never holds the others back. An op of one entry that applies settles as above. After the fifth failure the entry stays in the inbox and is tried again once per collection open, at its first event trigger; status reports the attention item `applyFailed` with its kind and key (`sync/policy#P14.4`). The entity is not sent while the entry waits (K12.1 rule 8), so no local version overwrites a server version this device could not apply. A cancelled op (O5) leaves its entries `ready`.
- Applying a version whose row digest equals the local row changes nothing (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#S4`), so replays are idempotent.
- **Size** (decided 2026-10-27). One plain `CloudIncoming` op takes at most 500 entries, which keeps its write phase near 200 ms (`engine/op-model#O6`). More ready entries are applied as a series of such ops, each with its own intent, in K12.1's order, so every reference an entry needs is applied by an earlier op or the same one (K13.3); interactive ops run between them, so a first fetch of 100,000 records never holds the writer for long (R2006). The one exception is a complete structural generation, which is applied in one op whatever its size (K19.5, R2053): with more than 500 entries it is submitted as the catalog op `cloud_incoming_apply` (`engine/op-model#O8`, `#O9`), a declared long write whose cancel between batches of 500 rolls the whole op back, and the generation is applied again later.

An entry whose references are not present locally and not in the same apply waits with reason `reference`. It is checked again whenever a record it references arrives. Waiting entries are counted in status; they never apply partly.

## K14. Identity collisions

### K14.1 Detection

Two devices can create different objects with the same id: ids come from the clock (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#I2`). Two devices can also bring in the same object with the same id: the same `.apkg` imported on both. For an id-keyed kind, a server version of key K, met by fetch or as a save conflict, whose `origin` differs from the local entity's while the local entity has never been acked, is one of:

- **The same object** when: note, equal `guid`; card, equal `nid` and `ord` (notes are applied first, so `nid` already reflects any note re-key); revlog, equal `cid`; deck, equal name (`unicase`); preset, equal name; notetype, equal lists of field names and of template names. The local entity takes the server origin and is merged with the server version under K15 without a base. Nothing is re-keyed.
- **A collision** otherwise: the local object is re-keyed (K14.2).

The same origin mismatch for an acked local entity is refused (K20).

### K14.2 Re-key

- A `CloudRekey` op (`engine/op-model#O1`) gives the local object a new id: the smallest id greater than the old id that no row of its table uses and no entity of the binding's tracking holds, within I3's cap (`storage/ids-usn-mtime#I5`). If no such id exists, the re-key fails. Decided 2026-10-27: a re-key that fails so, or a `CloudRekey` op that rolls back for a reason other than a cancel, gives the inbox entry whose server version needed it the reason `apply_failed` with failure `rekey` (K10.3), and the entry is retried as K13.2's "Failed applies" says; status reports `rekeyFailed` with the kind and key (`sync/policy#P14.4`). The op rewrites the object's references in the same op and stamps the changed rows as local changes. The object keeps its origin.
- Keeping the new id just above the old one keeps a review's instant (`revlog.id`, `storage/ids-usn-mtime#I1`), a note's or card's added date, and the sort order, all to within a few milliseconds. A re-keyed card's interval fuzz, which is seeded from its id (I2), can change; this is accepted.
- Before its COMMIT the op durably records a re-key intent (kind, old key, new key, origin). Once the writer's flush covers the op, tracking moves the entity to the new key and record name and removes the intent, in one transaction. At open, an intent left by a crash is settled before any send: when the collection holds the object under the new key, finish the move; otherwise the op did not commit and the intent is removed.
- Then the server version is applied under K. Undo steps that touch a rewritten key are dropped (`engine/undo#M9.8`).

| Re-keyed | References rewritten |
|---|---|
| note | `cards.nid` of its cards |
| card | `revlog.cid` of its revlog rows |
| revlog | none |
| deck | `cards.did` and `cards.odid`; the value of `curDeck`; the entries of `activeDecks`; key names beginning `_deck_<id>_`; values of keys `_nt_<id>_lastDeck`; template deck overrides in notetype `templates.config` (a notetype change) |
| preset | the preset id in the `kind` blob of each normal deck that uses it |
| notetype | `notes.mid`; `fields.ntid` and `templates.ntid`; the value of `curModel`; key names beginning `_nt_<id>_`; values of keys `_deck_<id>_lastNotetype` |

### K14.3 Re-creation under a tombstone

A live local row under the key of an acked tombstone of an id-keyed kind (for example an import that keeps ids) is re-keyed the same way. Name-keyed kinds (tag, config, media name) have no collisions: the same key is the same entity, and K15 merges it.

## K15. Conflicts

### K15.1 The merge

- Merges run on the device that meets the conflict: at a save conflict (K12.3) or when an incoming version meets a pending local entity (K13.2). A clean entity takes the incoming version without a merge. This device's own landed save is neither merged nor taken (K13.2, "Own landed saves").
- Input: base B (K11.2), local L, server S. Output: result R, and at most one conflict copy (K16). Both sides are first mapped to the newest structural generation they reference (K19.5).
- Each kind is split into parts. Stamp columns (`mod`, `mtime_secs`) are not parts. Per part: changed on one side only, that side; changed on both to the same value, that value; changed on both to different values, the kind's rule. An acked entity with no captured base uses its acked payload as base (K11.2). Decided 2026-10-27: only an entity that was never acked merges without a base (K14.1's same object, a name-keyed entity first met under another origin, K20); then every part that differs between L and S counts as changed on both sides.
- The merge is symmetric in content: swapping L and S gives the same parts of R. The exceptions where the server side wins by position are K14 (the unacked side is re-keyed) and K15.9 (S keeps the name).
- Stamp for "later" (K1): `mod` for notes and cards, `mtime_secs` for decks, presets, notetypes and config; none for revlog, tags and media names, which then order by payload digest alone.
- Result: R whose parts all equal S's is S, which is applied and the entity is clean. Otherwise R is written locally, stamped as a local change (`usn` -1, modification time now) when it differs from L, and stays pending. It is sent only through K12.1, eligibility and hold-back included: while `sync/policy` holds the entity back, nothing of it is sent until hold-back ends (`sync/policy#P7.2`, `#P8.3`; R2047).
- Wall-clock order decides only between two different values of the same part, and the losing content survives in a conflict copy, in the revlog, or in the trash (R2052).

### K15.2 Notes

| Part | Both changed differently |
|---|---|
| each field (by `ord` after mapping) | later version's value; conflict copy of the losing version |
| tags | set merge, no copy: R = tags in B kept by both, plus tags either side added; a tag either side removed is gone. Tags compare by the `unicase` fold; when both added one tag with different case, the later spelling |
| `guid`, `flags`, `data` | later version's value; no copy |
| `mid` | changes only through a structural generation (K19) |

`sfld` and `csum` of R are computed from R's fields (`text/checksum-and-sort-field`). The tags column is rewritten in its stored form.

### K15.3 Cards

- A card's **home due** is `odue` when `odid` ≠ 0, otherwise `due`. Its **filtered due** is `due` when `odid` ≠ 0, otherwise none. Both are compared as stored values.

| Part | Columns | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| scheduling | `type`, home due, `ivl`, `factor`, `reps`, `lapses`, `left`, `data`, and `queue` when it is not a hold | the side with the larger `last_review_id` wins the whole part; equal: part-wise three-way, both changed means the later version's |
| hold | `queue` -1 (suspended), -2 (buried by the scheduler), -3 (buried by the user), or none | three-way; both changed: later version's |
| placement | `did`, `odid`, filtered due | three-way on the whole part; both changed: later version's |
| `flags` | | three-way; both changed: later version's |
| `nid`, `ord` | | change only through a structural generation |

- R's `due` and `odue` are rebuilt from the merged parts: when R's `odid` ≠ 0, `due` = R's filtered due and `odue` = R's home due; otherwise `due` = R's home due and `odue` 0. So a card reviewed on one device while another empties its filtered deck comes home with the reviewed home due.
- `queue` of R is the hold when there is one. Otherwise it is the scheduling winner's `queue`, or, when that is a hold, the queue `sched/card-data#K3` gives for R's `type` and `due`.
- This is the deterministic history policy of R2052: the card state comes from the side whose newest review is the newest in the merged review history. FSRS memory state in `data` is not recomputed.
- Cards get no conflict copy: both reviews remain as revlog entities (R2052).

### K15.4 Revlog

- A revlog row is created once. Two different rows with one id are a collision (K14), never a merge.
- A later update (a repair) merges column by column; both changed means the version with the greater payload digest. No copy.

### K15.5 Decks

| Part | Rule when both changed |
|---|---|
| leaf name (last name component) | later version's |
| parent (`parent_id`) | later version's |
| `common` | later version's; conflict copy |
| `kind` | later version's; conflict copy |

### K15.6 The deck tree

- A deck's stored name is computed: the current name of the deck `parent_id` names, U+001F, the leaf; or the leaf alone when `parent_id` is 0. A receiver recomputes the names of a deck and its descendants after it applies any deck, so a child created on one device follows a rename made on another.
- A deck whose parent is a known deletion (K13.3) becomes top-level: `parent_id` 0, name = leaf. A deck whose parent has not arrived waits (K13.3).
- Cycles: concurrent moves can make decks each other's ancestors (X under Y on one device, Y under X on another). After applying decks and before computing names, every deck on a `parent_id` cycle with the largest id on that cycle becomes top-level, until no cycle is left.
- Names stay unique (`idx_decks_name`, `unicase`). When two decks end with the same name, the one with the larger id is renamed by the unique-name rule of `decks/decks#D2` (R61: `+` appended to its leaf until the name is unique). A filtered parent never takes a normal child: such a child becomes top-level (`decks/decks` D3). The nesting limit of D3 does not apply: every ancestor exists.
- Each change these rules make is a local change, sent onward. Every device computes the same result from the same decks.

### K15.7 Presets and notetypes

- Preset parts: `name`; `config`. Both changed: later version's; a `config` conflict keeps a conflict copy.
- Notetype parts, between versions with the same `struct_gen`: `name`; `config` (CSS and other options); the list of field names; each field's `config`; the list of template names; each template's `config` (its question and answer text). Both changed: later version's, with a conflict copy of the losing notetype (`notetype_conflict`).
- Notetype names stay unique (`idx_notetypes_name`): the notetype with the larger id is renamed by `notetypes/records` N4 step 7 (`+` appended). Field and template name lists are single parts, so a merge never produces duplicate names within a notetype.
- Different `struct_gen`: K19.5 and K19.6.

### K15.8 Tags and config

- Tag parts: stored spelling, `collapsed`, `config`. Both changed: the version with the greater payload digest. No copy.
- Config parts: the value. Both changed: later version's, no copy. Exceptions, when both values read as integers: for `nextPos` and `lastUnburied`, R holds the larger value.
- Tags and config keys are names: a version whose base is the tombstone of the same key is a re-creation and replaces the tombstone.

### K15.9 Media names

- These rules compare only a local file that is a local change of the binding. A name flagged `file_missing`, `superseded` or `withheld` is never compared here: K9.4 decides it. An incoming tombstone is K9.4's "Incoming tombstone".
- Every file move these rules make (a local file to its hash name, a trash) is a store call of the media apply, under its intent, with the absent state it leaves between calls listed as one of the apply's own states (K9.3).
- Same name, both created with different content: S keeps the name. The local file moves to the hash name of its lowercase candidate (`media/media-files#M4`), which becomes a new media-name entity, and every local note whose fields reference the old name (`text/media-references`) is rewritten to the new name. Notes that reference a name never acked are held until it is acked (K12.1 rule 6), so no note published with the old name expects the local content. A conflict copy (`media_content`) names the renamed file. Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): the new name is the name M4 step 4 stores under (a present case variant with content H included), computed before the intent is recorded. When M4's hash name is one registration never tracks (it ends with a space or `.`, `media/media-files#M4`), the file moves instead to that name with its trailing spaces and dots removed (`readme` → `readme-<H>`), and M4 step 4's lookup is made for that name, so the new name always has an entity (K9.4). When the binding already holds an entity for the new name's case key, the settle updates it instead of creating one: it stays clean when it is live, its acked content is H and it is not `withheld` (a `file_missing` or `superseded` flag clears, since the file now holds H); otherwise it becomes pending as a local change, and a `withheld` flag clears, since the user's notes now reference it (K9.4 lift 2).
  - **Existing clean destination and case spelling.** In that stay-clean case, the chosen file and the acked `MediaName` must have the same case key and matching SHA-256, SHA-1 and size for content H. Their exact names may differ because M4 reused a present case variant, including one renamed locally before the resolution. The entity's canonical CloudKit image remains the complete acked `MediaName`, with its acked spelling and digests unchanged. The file, journal and media file facts keep M4's actual stored spelling; tracking records those facts as already observed by this settle. A feed replay, reconcile scan or relaunch that finds those same facts must not discover another local CloudKit edit or replace the canonical image with the stored case variant. This applies only to the existing destination kept clean by this resolution: a later local change to those facts follows the ordinary dirty-mark and reconcile rules. A content or metadata difference cannot be made clean by comparing SHA-256 alone. Q5's canonical entity comparison uses the unchanged acked image; its media comparison finds the file by case key and checks its content, while the resolution's exact stored name must still be M4's chosen name.
- Same name, both changed content: later version's (payload digest). The losing bytes stay in the media trash (`media/media-files#M10`) and a conflict copy (`media_content`) names that trash entry.
- Same case key, other exact spelling (the install's `case_collision`, K9.3; `A.png` locally, `a.png` on the server): the spelling is not merged, and S keeps it. When the local file holds the acked content or S's content, nothing local is lost: K9.3 trashes it and installs S. Otherwise the local file holds a local change, and it is handled as "both created with different content" above, whatever the base: it moves to the hash name of its lowercase candidate as a new media-name entity, local notes that reference the old name are rewritten, a conflict copy (`media_content`) names it, and S installs under its spelling. Decided 2026-10-27: a local content change under another spelling never wins in place, because keeping it would need a second spelling of one case key.
- **One resolution, one intent** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4). A resolution that moves the local file to its hash name (the two cases above) writes the media store and the collection, so it is one apply with one apply intent (K10.3), recorded durably before its first store call. The intent lists the names with their expected states (K9.3: the old name and the hash name) and every key of the `CloudIncoming` op that rewrites the notes, as `side_effect` keys with their before and after digests (K13.2). The parts run in this order: the move; the note-rewrite op; the install of S. Once the move has run, the op runs whatever the later store calls find, so the notes always name the file that holds the content they were written with.
  - One tracking transaction settles the whole apply, once the install has returned and the writer's flush covers the op's seq: it deletes the inbox entry, updates the entity, creates the hash name's entity (pending as a new entity, so K12.1 rule 6 holds the rewritten notes until it is acked) or updates the one the binding already holds for it ("Same name, both created with different content"), makes the note keys pending as K13.2's `side_effect` keys, and removes the intent. No part settles alone.
  - At open (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.4` step 8) the intent is settled as one (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review; order corrected in the review of the P24 corrections). First the move is decided from the names' expected states (K9.3). When the old name and the hash name hold their before states, the move did not run: the apply continues in its normal order (move, note-rewrite op, install), and the op is not run before the move. When the old name holds no expected state and the hash name holds its before state, a local write landed before the move: the apply is abandoned with nothing run, the intent is removed, the note keys are not touched, the old name's current facts are a local change and the inbox entry stays ready. Any other state of the names is K9.3's. Only when the move has run (the hash name holds its after state) are the op's keys decided, as one and never by K13.2's "any other mix" rule. The op ran when any key holds its after digest, or when a key whose before row did not reference the new name (the intent records this per note key, K10.3) now references it. A key at neither digest is a local edit made after the op, or since the crash, since study and editing do not wait for V2.4 step 8. Otherwise the op did not run and runs again, taking each present key's current row as its before and skipping a key whose row is absent. Before that rerun's COMMIT, the intent durably records its new before and after digests and the before-images of its clean keys (K13.2), so a kill after it finds the keys at the recorded after digests. An edit made since is kept, and only its references to the old name are rewritten. Then the names as K9.3 says, continuing in order. A K15.9 intent is never applied again from the start, since its names hold the apply's own intermediate states.
  - When K9.3 abandons the install after the move has run (a local write at the old name after the move), the op has run (at open, step 8 has run it again when needed), and once the writer's flush covers its seq one transaction removes the intent and settles the parts that ran: the hash name becomes a pending new entity with the moved content, as a local change, so its blob is uploaded; the note keys become pending as `side_effect` keys; the old name's current facts are a local change; and the inbox entry stays ready, so its next apply meets that change under these rules.

## K16. Conflict copies

- A conflict copy is made exactly where K9.3, K9.4, K13.3, K15, K17.1, K17.3, K19.4, K19.5 and K19.6 say. Its row in `conflicts` holds the losing version's full envelope (payload included), the winning version's payload digest, the kind, the key, the reason, and the copy-time names K10.3 lists (field and template names, and a note's deck). It commits in tracking before the collection op that replaces the losing content; when no local content is replaced (a server version that loses to a local tombstone, K17.1), it commits with the apply intent. A copy for bytes a media apply already replaced (K9.3) commits in the transaction that consumes the feed item that reveals it.
- **Made once** (decided 2026-10-27). An apply that runs again (K13.2 at open, a replay after a crash or a power loss, an entry retried after `apply_failed`) computes the same copy again. A copy is not made when a `conflicts` row of the binding, resolved or not, already holds the same kind, key, reason, losing payload digest (the losing envelope's `payload_sha256`) and winning payload digest; the apply uses that row. So a replay never makes a second copy, and the `conflicts` attention item counts one.
- Copies are device-local and never sent to CloudKit. They survive relaunch until the user resolves them (R2054). Listing, inspection, recovery through a normal validated edit and resolution are `sync/cloudkit-recovery`.
- **Media copies whose bytes are not on the device** (decided 2026-10-27). A media-name copy can name content this device never held: the deleting device's `edit_vs_delete` copy of K17.1, whose losing version is another device's edit that was never downloaded. The copy commits with its envelope as usual. The device then downloads its blob (read by record ID, K9.3, with the download queue's bounds) into the cloud staging directory and keeps the file while the copy's `conflicts` row exists, resolved or not, so `unresolve` still finds the bytes; it is deleted after the commit that deletes the row (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V6`, `#V17.3`), and the staging sweep keeps files that a `conflicts` row names. While the copy is unresolved, this device never deletes that blob (K9.5). When the media zone no longer holds the blob, the copy lists its bytes as unavailable; the editing device keeps them in its media trash (K9.4, "Incoming tombstone").

| Reason | Kinds |
|---|---|
| `field_conflict` | note |
| `edit_vs_delete` | note, deck, preset, notetype, media name |
| `blob_conflict` | deck, preset |
| `notetype_conflict` | notetype (any K15.7 part) |
| `missing_notetype` | note, card |
| `structure_rollback` | notetype and its notes (K19.4, K19.6) |
| `unmapped_structure` | note, card (K19.5) |
| `media_content` | media name (renamed file or bytes in the trash) |

## K17. Tombstones

### K17.1 Representation

- A deletion saves the entity's `MGEntity` record as a new version with `tombstone` true and `deleted_at_ms`, never as a CloudKit record deletion. It goes through the same change tags and conflicts as any edit.
- An entity created and deleted while `sent` is false sends nothing and leaves tracking.
- An entity deleted while `sent` is true and never acked becomes a pending tombstone, saved as a new record. Its save can meet a server-record-changed result:
  - the server version's `writer` is this binding and its payload digest equals an in-flight digest: it is the version this device sent (K12.3, second row). It counts as saved, and the tombstone is saved again on its change tag;
  - same origin, other content (or the same content from another binding): another device fetched this device's version and changed it. Decided 2026-10-27: it follows K12.3's same-origin row like any other save conflict, through the inbox. Deletion wins (K17.3): the server's version is kept as an `edit_vs_delete` conflict copy for the kinds K17.3 lists, the apply writes no row (the local row is already gone), the tombstone takes the server's change tag and `erev` when that apply settles, and it is sent again. A media-name tombstone sent again carries the server version's `MediaName` payload, since that is now the last live version (K17.2); its copy follows K16 for bytes not on the device;
  - another origin: the local object never reached the server: the tombstone is dropped and the server version is a new entity.

### K17.2 Payload and graves

- Tombstones of media names carry the `MediaName` payload of the last live version that this device acked or applied, the server's version after a K17.1 conflict (K9.4). The payload is informative: a receiver decides by its own entity, not by it (K9.4, "Incoming tombstone"). Other tombstones have the empty payload (K1).
- Applying a tombstone of a note, card or deck inserts the grave (`oid`, `type`, `usn` -1) with insert-or-ignore (`storage/graves#G2`), so a later manual Anki transfer from this device sends the deletion. Graves are Anki bookkeeping: they are not compared for convergence, and a manual transfer that rewrites them sends nothing to CloudKit.

### K17.3 Apply and conflicts

- A tombstone of an id-keyed kind applies only to a local entity with the same origin. Met against a local entity with another origin, it is a collision (K14), not a deletion: the local entity is re-keyed when unacked, and the tombstone never removes it.
- An incoming tombstone removes the local row. A note tombstone also removes local cards of that note that the deleting device did not know; they get pending tombstones. A deck tombstone removes no cards the deleting device did not send as tombstones: those follow K13.3. A notetype tombstone removes its remaining local notes and cards, each kept as a conflict copy (`missing_notetype`).
- Deletion wins over a concurrent edit. The local edit is kept as a conflict copy (`edit_vs_delete`) for notes, decks, presets, notetypes and media names. A card's edit keeps no copy: its revlog stays.
- For id-keyed kinds an acked or applied tombstone is final (R2052): no live version replaces it (K14.3). No undo or redo restores a row whose tombstone is acked or applied (`engine/undo#M9.9`).

### K17.4 Purge and absent records

- A tombstone record whose `deleted_at_ms` is more than 180 days (15,552,000,000 ms) before now is deleted from CloudKit by any device bound to the active epoch that holds it acked. Purging runs only while A5 and A9b hold, so it is off until the signed runs confirm A9b (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V10.3`). When it runs is `sync/policy#P10.3` (`RunCleanup` sets `purge_due`, K10.3); K12.4 hands the deletion to the engine.
- An acked entity is absent from the server when a fetch reports its record deleted, when a completed absence pass did not see it (below), or when a save fails with unknown item. Each makes a `deletion` inbox entry (K13.1), except a pass's finding while purging is off (below), applied by K13.2: a tombstoned entity is dropped from tracking, with no op; a live one gets a deletion under K17.3, through a `CloudIncoming` op and its apply intent. An entity never acked is new and is not affected.
- **Absence pass** (decided 2026-10-27). Absence is computed over a durable record of what one fetch from no cursor delivered, never over what one process or one fetch cycle saw:
  - A pass starts only when an engine instance that Meguri created with no saved state (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V3.1` rule 5, V9.2, V14.2 step 9) starts its first fetch of the bound collection zone (A9a). Tracking writes an `absence_passes` row with a new pass id, replacing any earlier pass of that zone, before the first batch of that fetch is committed. Decided 2026-10-27: the engine's own restart after an expired change token is not detectable from its events (V10.3) and starts no pass. No heuristic may guess it: a pass over a fetch that was not complete would make false deletions. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 3): neither does an engine account event, `signIn` with the binding's identity included, after which the engine may have reset its change tokens (a no-state point, `sync/policy#P3`, A12): Meguri cannot tell whether that reset happened or whether the fetch after it is complete. A pass that runs continues through such a restart or reset, and records delivered again are marked again.
  - While the pass runs, K13.1 step 2 sets `seen_pass` to the pass id on every entity whose record the fetch delivered, as a record, a refused record or a deletion, whether or not it is applied or equal. An acknowledgement recorded during the pass (K12.3) sets it too.
  - Decided 2026-10-27: an entity that tracking does not hold when its record is delivered has no row for step 2 to mark, so the mark also travels with the inbox entry. Every inbox entry inserted while the pass runs (K13.1 step 2, or a save conflict, K12.3) records the pass id, and every transaction that gives an entity acked fields from such an entry while that pass still runs sets its `seen_pass` to that id: the K13.2 settle (an entity the apply creates included), the apply under K after a re-key (K14.2), and an own landed save (K13.2). An entry fetched before the pass started records no pass id and marks nothing. So the pass can find absent only an acked entity whose record the pass did not deliver and that took no acknowledgement from the pass (a save result during it, or an entry fetched in it); an entity created by the pass's own fetch, also in a later batch, after a kill or by a new instance that continues the pass, is never absent.
  - The pass survives a kill, a failed fetch and a new engine instance: every saved cursor of the zone was saved after K13.1 step 2 marked what it covers (K13.1 step 3), so an instance that continues from such a cursor continues the same pass, and records delivered again are marked again.
  - When the engine reports that a fetch of the zone has drained (A7) with no error and fetched the zone (`sync/policy#P3` `FetchDrained{collection, fetched: true}`) while the pass runs, one tracking transaction completes it: every acked entity of the zone whose `seen_pass` differs from the pass id is absent, and the pass row is removed. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): while tombstone purging is off (the first bullet), no Meguri build deletes an `MGEntity` record from a bound zone, so every absence is a false one, and a drain reported before the engine read the zone to its end (A9a is not proven) would otherwise delete live rows on this device. So that transaction makes no `deletion` entry: it logs the number of absent entities and their record names for Copy Debug Info. Before purging is turned on, each absent entity must first be confirmed by a record-ID read of its `e` that returns unknown item, and only then make its `deletion` entry (K23 A9b). A fetch that ends with an error completes nothing, and neither does a drain that fetched nothing (`fetched` false): the pass waits for a later drained fetch that fetches the zone. A bound zone that the first fetch of an instance with no saved state, or the first fetch after an engine account event (a no-state point, K12.5), does not list is never treated as empty, so it never makes an absence (A7): U114 reads `epoch` by record ID, and only a zone-not-found result, or unknown item (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4: the zone holds no `MGEpoch`), makes it K12.5's plain `zoneNotFound` row (K12.5, "A zone missing from a first fetch"; that row then reads `MGActive`). A read that finds `epoch` isolates nothing and decides: the call then gets `FetchDrained{collection, fetched: false}`, which completes no pass (above).
  - A pass ends without a check when its binding is isolated or deleted, or its `engine_state` row is deleted (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V9.2`); the next instance Meguri creates with no saved state starts a new pass. A gate that closes for a while (a scan, a lifecycle state, an account read) does not end it. So a fetch interrupted and resumed never treats the records delivered before the interruption as absent.
- A purge therefore never brings a deleted object back: a device that was offline longer than the retention still learns the deletion from the record deletion or from the absence.
- **Assumption A9a** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4: not proven): Meguri knows when it creates an engine instance with no saved state, so it knows that instance's first fetch of the zone starts from no cursor; that the engine reports that fetch's drain with no error (A7) only after it has delivered every record of the zone is Apple behavior the SDK headers do not promise (the engine may split a zone fetch over many requests, end a scheduled fetch early, or move to another zone). A9a gates the absence pass. While purging is off a pass makes no deletion, so a false A9a costs nothing; the signed runs check it (K22.3).
- **Assumption A9b** (unverified; decided 2026-10-27, V10.3): the engine's own restart after an expired change token can be detected. No public engine event says so today, so A9b is taken as false: such a restart starts no pass, and A9b gates only tombstone purging, which stays off until a signed device run shows A9b holds.

## K18. Epochs

### K18.1 Definition

An epoch is one published cloud collection: one collection zone, one `MGEpoch`, one record key. The publishing device's binding links the epoch id to the publication identity of its local collection (`engine/collection-replace#R6.1`). A device that adopts the epoch gives its new local collection a publication identity and binds it to the same epoch id.

### K18.2 What starts one

- Initial publication: the R2046 connection action on a device with no binding in that account and environment, when the account has no `MGActive`.
- Replace iCloud collection (R125), including the cloud choice after a restore, a collection package import or a manual Anki import (R1548, R2056). That explicit choice is the only thing that clears `cloudPublicationPending` for a new publication.
- Nothing else. Structural changes, package (`.apkg`) imports and Check Database repairs are structural generations (K19).
- R1548's "remains local" choice unbinds the collection: it publishes nothing and adopts nothing, and the old binding stays isolated with its pending work.
- A bound device whose bound zone, `meguri-root` or `MGActive` disappeared never starts an initial publication: K2 applies, and only the user's V15 choice publishes again.

### K18.3 Publishing

Before step 1, the `publish` op creates the binding of the new epoch and isolates the binding it replaces, in one tracking commit (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 2). Then:

1. Create the zones `c-<epoch id>`, `meguri-root` and `meguri-media` in one zone creation, always, whether or not they exist; then save `MGMediaRoot` `root` with a new media key as a new record, always, except after an encrypted data reset, when the op reads `root` first (K2); a `root` that exists gives its key through the save conflict (K9.2), and `MGEpoch` with state `publishing` (decided 2026-10-27, K2). These are U114's own operations (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), not engine sends: `MGEpoch` and `MGMediaRoot` are saved only after their zone's creation succeeded, and the binding's engine instance starts only once this step is done (V15.2, step `zones`).
2. Run the binding's reconcile scan: every live entity becomes pending. Tombstones are not published. Media names take the binding before's state as K9.4's "Carried names" says: a name the old epoch held live whose local file is missing is published from its acked `MediaName` payload, and a `withheld` name stays withheld.
3. Send every entity; upload the blobs the media zone lacks, after the binding holds an acked `MGMediaRoot` (K9.2).
4. Save `MGEpoch` with state `complete` and the live counts per kind.
5. Save `MGActive` naming the epoch, conditional on the change tag of the `MGActive` version the user confirmed replacing, or as a new record when there was none. A conflict means another device switched meanwhile: nothing is overwritten and the user decides (`sync/cloudkit-recovery`). Decided 2026-10-27: right before each attempt of this save, the op reads its own `epoch` by record ID with an own operation (V26.1), and the save runs only when the read returns this epoch's `MGEpoch` with state `complete`. Zone not found, or unknown item (the zone holds no `epoch`), means the zone was deleted (K18.5): the save is not made, and the result is V26.1's zone-not-found row for the bound collection zone: K12.5 isolates the new binding, its acknowledgements in that zone are never trusted again, and the op stops at its step with Cancel. Any other result leaves the op at its step with V26.1's outcome for that error. So `MGActive` never names a zone that is gone. Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): the saved `Active` holds `epoch_id` = the new epoch; `previous_epoch_id` = the epoch that the `MGActive` version this save is conditional on names (the op row's active epoch id, K10.3: the version the user confirmed replacing, or after a V15.2 step 5 Reuse the version the user then confirmed), absent for a new record and when the version it replaces could not be read (the op row's active epoch id is none, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.3`); and `activated_at_ms` = the publisher's device clock when it builds that attempt; `replaced_through_ms` = the op row's replaced through, absent when it holds none (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V2.3`, V10.2). An earlier attempt that landed is accepted with the server record (V26.1), so the values every device reads are those of the version that landed. So the epoch this switch retires is always the previous epoch, also after a Reuse, and K18.5 and `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V14.1` count from this switch.

- Local edits made from the binding's creation to step 5 are tracked under the new binding and sent like any change.
- Steps 1, 4 and 5 write through own operations (V26.1), with V26.1's outcomes; the entities and blobs of step 3 go through the engine. Step 3 is done when V15.2's condition ("K18.3 step 3 is done") holds, and the op's recorded steps are V15.2's (`confirmed`, `bound`, `zones`, `sent`, `complete`, `done`, and `cancelling`).
- The publisher records the step it reached in tracking. After a restart it resumes from that step, or the user abandons the publication through `sync/cloudkit-recovery`.
- The replaced epoch's zone is the cloud side's recoverable copy (R2056): for its 30 days (K18.5) `sync/cloudkit-recovery` can stage it as a local collection the way K18.4 does.

### K18.4 Detecting and adopting

- Every device fetches `meguri-root`. When `MGActive` names an epoch other than the bound one, the device stops sending to the old zone, keeps its pending work isolated, and reports needs attention (`sync/cloudkit-recovery`). The publisher of a new epoch during K18.3 steps 1–4 is the K2 exception.
- Adoption replaces the local collection and so needs the explicit confirmation of R2056: it shows the direction, the epoch's counts (`MGEpoch.counts`), the local collection's counts and this device's pending and isolated work. It starts only when `MGEpoch.state` is `complete`.
- Before the swap the device takes a safety backup of its local collection (`package/backup-triggers` T6.1).
- The device fetches the whole zone into a new staged collection: a new schema-18 file whose `col` row has `crt` and `ver` from `MGEpoch`, `mod` = now_ms, `scm` = now_ms, `ls` 0, `usn` 0, `dty` 0 and the empty legacy text columns (`model/row-records#R2`); every live entity's rows, with `usn` -1 (K11.3); an empty `graves` table; open structural generations handled as K19.5.
- The staged file passes the open gate, the integrity check and K13.3's reference checks, and replaces the local collection through `engine/collection-replace` (reason `CloudAdopt`, `engine/collection-replace#R1`). The new binding starts with every fetched entity acked, tombstones included as acked tombstone entities with no row, and no saved cursor: its first engine instance fetches the zone again from the start (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V14.2` step 9); later changes arrive normally. Media follows in the background (R1592).
- A device with a local collection and no binding for the active epoch (a new device, a reinstall, a lost tracking file) never merges into the epoch: the user chooses which side replaces the other (R2055).

### K18.5 Old epochs

- The old collection zone stays for 30 days after the later of the switch (`MGActive.activated_at_ms`) and the `zones` row's first-seen instant (K10.3), then a device bound to the new epoch deletes it. Decided 2026-10-27: `activated_at_ms` is the publisher's clock, and the first-seen instant is the deleting device's own, never earlier than the switch it learned of, so a publisher whose clock is slow never shortens the 30 days that the previous epoch is kept (R2056).
- A collection zone that `MGActive` has never named (a publication abandoned, interrupted for good, or lost in K18.3 step 5) is deleted by any device bound to the active epoch when it is more than 30 days old, and never while `MGActive` names it: by its `MGEpoch.published_at_ms` when this device saved that `MGEpoch` (its publisher), otherwise by the `zones` row's first-seen instant (K10.3). Decided 2026-10-27: CloudKit gives a zone no creation time, and no device fetches another `c-` zone (K20), so a device other than the publisher has no other source. It learns of such a zone only from the engine's database changes, which must report zones outside the engine's fetch scope (**Assumption A21**, K23, which gives the fallback). The first-seen instant is never earlier than the zone's creation, so this rule never deletes a zone younger than 30 days.
- Decided 2026-10-27: `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 5's "replace it again" reuses the uploaded zone only while its `MGEpoch.published_at_ms` is less than 20 days before now, and only after a record-ID read of its `epoch` (an own operation, V26.1) returns it. Otherwise it starts a new epoch, with a new epoch id and zone, and the old zone is left to the deletions of this section. The 10-day margin leaves room for the time the reused publication needs to reach its switch and for clock differences between devices. A zone deleted meanwhile is found by K18.3 step 5's read or by a send's zone-not-found result (K12.5), and is never switched to.
- The zone of a publication cancelled before switch sent is deleted as `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.3` says: by the binding before, through a `zones` row with deletion due, which stays due until the deletion's result (`sync/policy#P10.3` item 3), or once by U114's own operation; otherwise this section's 30-day rule applies. Decided 2026-10-27: a publication cancelled after switch sent, or ended by a `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V15.2` step 5 conflict whose zone "replace it again" does not reuse (above), leaves its zone to the 30-day rule: an `MGActive` save of it may have landed with its result lost, so other devices may be bound to it (V15.2 step 5, V15.3, "Switch sent").
- Tracking keeps these zones in `zones` (K10.3). When the deletions are marked due is `sync/policy#P10.3` (`RunCleanup`). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): they are `DeleteZones` own operations (`sync/policy#P4`, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V26.1`), never engine work: each run reads `MGActive` first and deletes a zone only when that read names the bound epoch, and it runs from a drained collection fetch, so with auto off from Sync Now's fetch steps. A result of deleted or zone not found (another device deleted it first) deletes the zone's `zones` row with its due mark; a failed deletion stays due.
- No device writes to a collection zone other than its bound epoch's, except these deletions.

## K19. Structural generations

This section is the CloudKit record layout of a schema change that `notetypes/schema-changes` G1 and G7 refer to: which ops publish a generation (K19.1), the `MGGeneration` record and its maps (K19.2), how it is claimed and completed (K19.3, K19.4), and how a receiver applies it (K19.5), setting `col.scm` as G1 says (`cloudkit_structure_applied`, K11.3).

### K19.1 What publishes as one

| Op | Reason code and name (`notetypes/schema-changes` G2 reasons; `apkg_apply` is the `engine/op-model#O9` catalog name) |
|---|---|
| note type save that adds, removes or reorders fields or templates, or changes the sort field | 1 `fields_changed`, 2 `templates_changed`, 3 `sort_field_changed` |
| note type deletion; Restore to Default that changes structure | 4 `notetype_removed`, 5 `notetype_restored` |
| Change Note Type | 6 `notes_notetype_changed` |
| preset deletion | 7 `preset_removed` |
| package import, whatever its size | 8 `apkg_apply`; also 3 `sort_field_changed` when an update without a merge rewrote a note type's sort field (`package/apkg-import#I7`), and 9 `notetypes_merged` when a merge has a structural effect (`package/notetype-merge#J9`) |
| Check Database repairs that are schema changes | 10 `check_database_repair` |

Decided 2026-10-27: a package import reports 8 always; 3, with that note type's identity layout, only for I7's sort-field rewrite; an identity layout, with no reason, for each updated note type whose field or template names changed; and 9 only for a merge with a structural effect (J9), so a merge without one reports 8 alone (`engine/change-stream#S2.5`). Check Database's recovery of a missing note type (`maintenance/check-database-notes#N4`) reports the recovered note type as an added one under 10, with no type change for the notes it takes.

`collection_replaced`, `full_upload`, `sync_sanity_failed` and `force_one_way` are Anki bookkeeping with no CloudKit meaning. A whole replacement is an epoch (K18). `cloudkit_structure_applied` is the receiver's apply of a generation (K19.5) and publishes nothing.

### K19.2 Identity and manifest

- A generation id is 16 random bytes.
- Each notetype has a layout counter `struct_gen`. Tracking keeps, per notetype, the local value and the acked server value (`notetype_layouts`). A notetype payload's `struct_gen` is the local value. A note's and a card's `nt_struct_gen` is the local value of the note's notetype when the payload is built: the collection always holds a note in its notetype's current layout.
- A local op that changes a notetype's structure sets its local value to max(local value, acked server value) + 1 when it commits. So two local generations on one notetype get different values; two that are both unclaimed exist only as "Joining" below allows. Notes and cards of that notetype are not sendable until the generation is claimed (K12.1 rule 7), so no record under the new value reaches the server before the value is the server's.
- `MGGeneration.e` holds `meguri.cloud.v1.Generation`:

  | No. | Field | Type | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | `format` | uint32 | 1 |
  | 2 | `generation_id` | bytes (16) | |
  | 3 | `reasons` | repeated uint32 | K19.1 reason codes, ascending |
  | 4 | `state` | uint32 | 1 `claimed`, 2 `complete`, 3 `abandoned` |
  | 5 | `anchors` | repeated string | record names of the notetypes whose `struct_gen` changes, ascending |
  | 6 | `members` | repeated `Member` | inline member list, by record name; empty when the list is in `ma` |
  | 7 | `maps` | repeated `LayoutMap` | by notetype id |
  | 8 | `type_changes` | repeated `TypeChange` | Change Note Type and an import's merge: one per (old, new) notetype pair, by old notetype id, then new notetype id |
  | 9 | `members_sha256` | optional bytes (32) | present when the member list is in `ma` |
  | 10 | `members_size` | optional uint64 | present when the member list is in `ma` |

  | Message | Fields |
  |---|---|
  | `Member` | 1 `record_name` string; 2 `erev` uint64 (the listed `erev`; anchors included) |
  | `MemberList` (the content of `ma`) | 1 `members` repeated `Member` |
  | `LayoutMap` | 1 `notetype_id` int64; 2 `from_struct_gen` uint64; 3 `to_struct_gen` uint64; 4 `field_map` repeated sint32; 5 `template_map` repeated sint32 |
  | `TypeChange` | 1 `old_notetype_id` int64; 2 `old_struct_gen` uint64; 3 `new_notetype_id` int64; 4 `new_struct_gen` uint64; 5 `field_map` repeated sint32; 6 `template_map` repeated sint32 |

- A map's entry i is the old `ord` that new `ord` i comes from, or -1 for none.
- **No template map** (decided 2026-10-27). A type change (Change Note Type, or an import merge's pair, `package/notetype-merge#J5` step 5) whose old or new notetype is a cloze notetype has no template map (`notetypes/change-notetype#M1`): cards keep their ordinals (`notetypes/change-notetype#M9.2`, `#M9.3`). Decided 2026-10-27 (review of this bullet): a cloze card's ordinal is its cloze number, not a template position, so the `template_map` of such a `TypeChange`, and of a `LayoutMap` of a cloze notetype, is a **prefix map**. For i below its length, entry i is the old ordinal that new ordinal i comes from, or -1, as for every map. A new ordinal i at or past its length comes from old ordinal i when the map's old notetype is a cloze notetype (a standard new notetype has new ordinals only below its template count, so a cloze card at or above it comes to none and is deleted, as M9.2 does), and from none when the old notetype is standard (each of its cards has an ordinal below its template count, which the list covers). The op that makes a type change reports, for it, an empty `template_map` when the old notetype is a cloze notetype, and the identity list of the old notetype's template count (0, 1, …, n-1) when it is standard, so cards keep their ordinals either way. Joining (below) composes prefix maps into other prefix maps. K19.5's mapping reads every template map so. The undo of such a change (`engine/undo#M9.11`) also has a cloze side: its reversed entry follows the same rule, not M9.11's inversion (empty when the reversed entry's old notetype is a cloze notetype, the identity list of its template count when it is standard). Only a map whose old notetype is a cloze notetype can be empty: a map between two standard notetypes has one entry per new template, and a standard notetype has at least one.
- The op that makes the change reports its maps (K24); tracking stores the generation with state `local` when the op commits, its members being the entities the op changed.
- **Joining** (decided 2026-10-27). A claim batch holds every anchor's version as of the snapshot it is built from (K19.3), so its manifest must hold every local change of those anchors up to that snapshot. A device therefore holds at most one generation per anchor that is `local` (its claim batch not handed yet, K19.7). A structural op on a notetype that is an anchor of such a generation, or of a generation in state `lost` (K19.7, not yet `rolled_back`; review of the P24 corrections), joins it, in the tracking commit that would otherwise store a new generation, and a rollback (K19.6) covers the joined whole:
  - the op's map of a notetype is composed with the generation's map of that notetype: entry i of the result is the generation's entry at the op's entry i, or -1 when either entry is -1. The generation's map then runs from its first `from_struct_gen` to the new local value;
  - a type change composes the same way, with the maps of its old notetype that came before it and of its new notetype that come after it;
  - decided 2026-10-27: when either map is a prefix map (above), each is read with its rule past its length; the result is a prefix map as long as the longer of the two, and past its length it follows the rule of its own old notetype. That is the same as applying the two maps one after the other, since a type change composes only with maps of one of its two notetypes. For example: a standard notetype of 3 templates whose still-local generation removed template 2 (map [0, 1]), whose notes then go to a cloze notetype (map [0, 1]), gets [0, 1], so receivers renumber its cards from the old layout and the card of old ordinal 2 comes to none; a cloze notetype whose notes go to a standard notetype of 2 templates (empty map) that then swaps its templates (map [1, 0]) gets [1, 0], and a cloze card of ordinal 2 or more comes to none, as M9.2 deletes it;
  - the op's anchors, members, reasons and type changes are added. An op whose anchors belong to two such generations merges them into the older one, which keeps its place in the claim order (K19.3).

  A joined generation is never split, and it is claimed even when its composed maps are all identity (an edit and its undo): receivers then change only `struct_gen`. So a member belongs to at most one unclaimed generation, and the envelope's single `generation` id (K12.1) is defined.
- When a claim batch of such a generation was built from a barrier snapshot (`sync/policy#P7.1`) that came before the op, and its result is not in, the op starts a new `local` generation instead, which is not claimed before that result. When the claim succeeds, the new generation is claimed after it, in commit order (K19.3). When the claim returns the first generation to `local` (K19.4 retry) or makes it `lost`, the new one joins it in the tracking transaction that records the result, and a rollback (K19.6) covers the joined whole. A member of both, once both are claimed, carries the newer generation's id.

### K19.3 Publishing

1. **Claim**: one batch, atomic in the collection zone, holds the manifest (state `claimed`) and every anchor's new version. The manifest is the first record of its claim batch (P24 review). A generation without anchors claims with the manifest alone. The claim must fit K7's request budget in one batch (after `limitExceeded`, the halved byte bound with K7's 250 records, K12.3): when it would not, every anchor in it carries its payload as the asset `pa` and the manifest its member list as `ma`, whatever their size (K5.1), which leaves a few KB of non-asset data per record. A generation with more than 249 anchors cannot be claimed (250 records per request) and reports `claimStuck`. **Assumption A8** (documented in the SDK headers, 2026-10-27: a batch built with its atomic-by-zone flag set saves all or none of its records in a zone, and the others fail with `batchRequestFailed`): the sync engine can send one batch atomically within one zone.
2. **Members**: after the claim is acked, sent in any batches, each with `generation` set. A member edited again locally before the generation is complete is sent in its latest state, still with `generation` set. Notes and cards of an anchor become sendable again under the claimed value.
3. **Complete**: the manifest is saved with state `complete` when every member is acked, with the system fields of the acked claimed manifest (K10.3 `generations`), so the save is conditional on that version (K12.2).

- The manifest saves of steps 1 and 3 are control records (K12.6): a full update never removes them from the engine's pending list while they are due.

- A generation cannot be claimed while one of its members is oversize or unreadable; status reports needs attention.
- A device claims its own generations in the order their ops committed.

### K19.4 Claim results

A claim batch is atomic: when any record in it fails, none is saved.

- **Claim landed** (decided 2026-10-27, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V7`). The manifest's result is read first. Its record name comes from the generation id (K4.2), which only this device made (K19.2), so a server-record-changed result for the manifest means an earlier attempt of this claim landed with its result lost (K19.7 `claiming` sends it again; a kill at `cloud.claim.after_send`, a network failure). By A8 every anchor then landed with it. No read is needed: the resend's results decide, and a fetch that delivers the manifest first decides the same way (K13.1). The server manifest's state decides:
  - `claimed`: the claim counts as saved. The generation becomes `claimed`, and the commit that records this result stores the server manifest's system fields (K10.3 `generations`), which K19.3 step 3 uses. Each anchor whose result carries its server version is acked by K12.3's in-flight digest row (its `writer` is this binding). An anchor whose result was batch request failed stays pending, is sent as a member (K19.3 step 2) and meets that row then. The table below does not apply.
  - `abandoned` (another device abandoned it, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18`): K19.8, as K12.6 says for a `complete` save that meets an `abandoned` manifest.

Otherwise the anchors' results decide (decided 2026-10-27, review round 3, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V7`). This applies only when the batch shows that no manifest `g<generation id>` exists on the server: the manifest's own result is not server record changed (normally batch request failed: another record of the atomic batch failed). Then no earlier attempt of this claim landed, since A8 would have saved the manifest with its anchors, so no anchor version on the server is this claim's, and every row below applies, the first included. A resend whose manifest meets server record changed is always decided by "Claim landed" above, never by an anchor's `struct_gen`, origin or `writer`.

| Anchor's server version | Result |
|---|---|
| a larger `struct_gen` than the local generation's `from_struct_gen` (another device's generation came first; with no manifest of this generation on the server, the version cannot be this claim's) | claim conflict: the generation becomes `lost`; the other generation is adopted (K19.5), then this one is rolled back (K19.6) |
| a tombstone (the notetype was deleted elsewhere) | claim conflict: deletion wins (K17.3). The local generation's changes to that notetype are kept as conflict copies (`structure_rollback`), and its notes and cards follow the tombstone (`missing_notetype`) |
| the same `struct_gen`, other content (a non-structural change elsewhere: CSS, a rename) | not a conflict. The server version goes through the inbox as K12.3's same-origin row says, and K13.2 applies it: its non-structural parts are merged into the local anchor (K15.7), keeping the local layout. The anchor takes the server's change tag only when that apply settles after the WAL flush, and only then is the claim sent again: until then its claim batch is not due (K12.6). Decided 2026-10-27 (review round 4): an attempt that fails only by this row does not count toward the 5 failed attempts, since each one needs a new change on another device. After 5 failed attempts (any other failed claim, `limitExceeded` included) the generation stays `local` and status reports `claimStuck` |
| the local generation tombstones the anchor, and the server version changed | the tombstone takes the new change tag and the claim is sent again (deletion wins, K17.3); the other device's changes are kept as `edit_vs_delete` copies |

- **Another generation fetched first** (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review). An inbox entry may be inserted, fetched or from a save conflict, for an anchor of a local generation in state `local`, whose version has a `struct_gen` above that generation's `from_struct_gen` for that notetype, or is a tombstone. It does not apply to a `local` generation that waits, under K19.2, for the result of a `claiming` generation of the same anchor: that generation's resend decides it, and a `lost` result joins it (K19.2; review of the P24 corrections). The commit that inserts it, or that settles it out of reason `asset` so that its payload, and with it its `struct_gen`, can be read (K13.1), makes the generation follow the table's first row (`lost`) or second row, as a claim result would. No attempt of a `local` generation's claim landed (it was never handed, or its last attempt showed no manifest), so that version is another device's. The other generation is then adopted and this one rolled back (K19.6). Decided 2026-10-27 (review of the P24 corrections): the same check runs in the tracking commit that stores a `local` generation or joins an op to one (K19.2), for each of its anchors that already has an inbox entry, of any reason (an `asset` entry is checked when it leaves `asset`), whose version has a `struct_gen` above the generation's `from_struct_gen` for that notetype, or is a tombstone: that commit makes the generation `lost` (first row) or follows the second row. So a structural edit made while another device's generation waits in the inbox for its members never leaves both waiting on each other (K19.5's hold, K12.6's claim row). A `claiming` generation is decided by its resend (K12.6).
- **Stuck claims** (decided 2026-10-27). A generation is stuck while it has more than 249 anchors (K19.3), or after its fifth failed claim attempt. A stuck generation stays `local`, its claim batch is not due (K12.6), and status reports `claimStuck` (`sync/policy#P14.4`); the generations after it in claim order wait. Each collection open resets the claim attempts of a generation stuck by failed attempts to 0, so its claim is tried again once per open, at the first full update; `claimStuck` clears when a claim is acked. A generation with too many anchors stays stuck, and status keeps reporting it.
- **Refused anchor** (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review). An anchor result whose server version K20 refuses (K12.3, "Refused save conflicts") leaves the generation `local`, and its claim is not due while that `refused` row exists (K12.6). The attempt does not count toward the 5 failed attempts, and status reports `refused`, not `claimStuck`.
- **Complete landed** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4). A `complete` save (K19.3 step 3) whose result is server record changed carries the server manifest of this generation id, which only this device makes (K19.2), and its state decides: `complete` means an earlier attempt landed with its result lost, so the save counts as saved: the generation becomes `complete` and the commit stores the server manifest's system fields; `claimed` gives the generation that version's system fields, and the save stays due; `abandoned` is K19.8. A fetched manifest of a local `claimed` generation has the same outcome in the K13.1 step 2 transaction.
- Records of the batch whose own result is "batch request failed" (K12.3) keep their state.
- A claim that fails only because a member was edited elsewhere is not possible: members are not in the claim batch. They merge under K15 when they are sent.

### K19.5 Adopting

- Records with a `generation` this device has not adopted wait in the inbox (reason `generation`), and so do notes and cards with `nt_struct_gen` above the local value of their notetype. A version whose `generation` this device holds as `abandoned` does not wait for it ("An abandoned manifest", below).
- A generation is complete for a receiver when it holds the manifest and, for every member, a server version that has this `generation` or an `erev` above the listed one (a tombstone counts the same way). A later version without `generation` is decodable: its `nt_struct_gen` names its layout, and a device writes such a version only after it has adopted this generation (K12.3).
- A complete generation is applied in one `CloudIncoming` op: anchors and the latest version of every member, merged with local pending changes after mapping (K15.1). Its maps are stored in `generation_maps`; the local notetypes take the anchors' `struct_gen` as both local and acked server value. When the apply changes local structure by `notetypes/schema-changes` G1's test (a note type's stored field or template positions or its sort field, a removed note type or preset, notes moved to another note type), the same op sets `col.scm` to now (`cloudkit_structure_applied`, K11.3); otherwise it leaves `col.scm`.
- Decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): a remote generation one of whose anchors is an anchor of a local generation in state `local` or `claiming` is not applied, even when complete, until that local generation is `claimed`, `lost` or `rolled_back` (K19.7). A `lost` one is rolled back through K19.6, which adopts it.
- A note or card written under an older `struct_gen` is mapped through the stored maps, oldest first. When a step of that chain is missing, the version is not applied and is kept as a conflict copy (`unmapped_structure`).
- Receivers never show part of a generation (R2053). A claimed generation that never completes stays waiting; abandoning it is the explicit action of `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18`, which never saves `abandoned` over a `complete` manifest.
- Tracking records a waiting generation's last progress instant: when a drained fetch last brought its manifest or one of its members (`generations`, K10.3; V18).
- **An abandoned manifest** (decided 2026-10-27, review round 3, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18`). On every device, the tracking commit that records a manifest with state `abandoned` for a generation it holds as remote `waiting`, or has not seen yet (a fetched version, V18 step 2's read on the device that abandons it), records the generation `abandoned` (K19.7) and, in the same commit, stores the manifest's maps in `generation_maps`, so a device still at the earlier layout can map through the restoring generation that V18 step 4 publishes, and turns every inbox entry that waits with reason `generation` for that id into a conflict copy (`structure_rollback` for an anchor, `unmapped_structure` for any other member), deleting the entry, with the entity updates V18 gives. A version that names a generation this device holds as `abandoned` and comes later, fetched or in a save conflict (K12.3), is handled the same way in the commit that records it: it is never held in the inbox for that id. A local generation whose manifest is abandoned follows K19.8 (K12.6, K19.4).

### K19.6 Rolling back a lost generation

On the device whose claim failed (a generation whose manifest another device abandoned is never rolled back here: K19.8):

- The other generation is adopted. For each conflicting anchor, the local notetype becomes the server's, and every local note and card of that notetype is rebuilt under the adopted layout, members or not: the collection holds them all in the lost layout.
- A clean note or card takes the server version. A pending note's content is mapped back through the inverse of the local map, then forward through the adopted map, and merged under K15.2. Content in a field that has no source in the base layout (a field the local change added) cannot be mapped and is kept, with the local notetype, as a conflict copy (`structure_rollback`). Cards of a template the local change added, which no adopted template maps to, are removed locally; they were never sent.
- Members of the local generation that do not depend on a conflicting anchor are published again as a new generation. The lost generation was never claimed, so the server holds nothing of it, and nothing that depends on it was sent (K12.1 rule 7).

### K19.8 An abandoned own generation (decided 2026-10-27, review round 4)

A local generation whose manifest another device abandoned (`sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18`), met by a resent claim (K19.4 "Claim landed"), a `complete` save (K19.4 "Complete landed") or a fetch (K13.1), is not handled by K19.6, whose assumptions do not hold: its claim landed, the server holds its anchors in its layout, and members may have been sent with its id. - the generation becomes `rolled_back` (K19.7), with no inverse mapping, no rollback copies and no new publication of its members;
- its anchors' `notetype_layouts` are not changed (decided 2026-10-27, P24 review): before this device adopts the restoring generation they hold this generation's layout, or that of a later local generation of the same anchor that was claimed once this one was claimed (K19.2) and that `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18` abandons with it (its chain; review of the P24 corrections). - its members that are pending stay pending, and no later envelope carries its id (K12.1);
- for each anchor (decided 2026-10-27, review of the P24 corrections: the hold ends per anchor, not for the whole generation), until this device has adopted a remote generation whose `LayoutMap` for that anchor has a `from_struct_gen` at or above this generation's `to_struct_gen` (the restoring generation of its chain, K19.5, `sync/cloudkit-recovery#V18`), or a later local generation of that anchor is `complete` (every device then maps to its layout through this generation's stored maps, K19.5), or the last bullet has rebuilt that anchor's notetype, that anchor and the notes and cards of its notetype are not sendable (K12.1 rule 7), and no claim of a local generation with that anchor is due (K12.6), so nothing in the abandoned layout reaches a device that has left it;
- the restoring generation is then adopted through K19.5 like any remote generation: its maps take this device's notes from the abandoned layout to the restored one in one step, merged with local pending changes (K15.1);
- decided 2026-10-27 (P24 review): when a remote generation whose `LayoutMap` for one of these anchors starts below this generation's `to_struct_gen` is adopted first (another device's change from the earlier layout claimed before the restoring generation, whose claim then fails on its device, K19.4), this device rebuilds its notes and cards of that notetype as K19.6's first two bullets say: from the layout they are in, back through the inverse maps of each local generation of that anchor that this rule or K19.6 rolls back, newest first, to the layout the adopted map starts from, then forward through the adopted maps, as one rebuild, with `structure_rollback` copies for content that cannot be mapped. - carried media names (K9.4, K18.3 step 2): a device bound to epoch E0 that has not downloaded 300 images replaces the iCloud collection: E1 holds the 300 names from the binding before's acked payloads, an adopter that holds the files classifies them clean and uploads any blob the media zone lacks, a device without them downloads them or reports `mediaMissing`, and a name `withheld` in the binding before is not sent;
- previous epoch retention (K18.5): a publisher whose clock is 40 days slow switches to E1; a device bound to E1 deletes E0's zone only 30 days after its `zones` row's first-seen instant; after a V15.2 step 5 Reuse, device A's switch to E_A writes `previous_epoch_id` = E_B (the epoch B published 25 days earlier, K18.3 step 5), every device keeps E_B's zone 30 days from A's switch, and V14.1 offers E_B for restore;
- an abandoned own generation (K19.8): device A reorders the fields of notetype T, claims, sends some members and has a pending note edit; device B abandons the generation and publishes the restoring one; A records the `abandoned` manifest, sends nothing of T until it adopts the restoring generation, maps its pending note once, and every device ends with T in the restored layout and A's edit under the right field names;
- the bound collection zone's `MGActive` read (K12.5): after a send's plain `zoneNotFound` with the device offline, the read fails and the binding is isolated `zone_missing`; when the read returns an `MGActive` naming another epoch, `epoch_switched`; a `publish` op's binding before `done` is isolated `zone_missing` with no read.

### K22.3 Properties and checks

- Random interleavings of local ops on two or three simulated devices with random delivery order converge to invariant 3.
- Acknowledgement of revision N while revision N+1 is pending leaves N+1 pending.
- Identity collisions for each id-keyed kind re-key the unacked side and keep both objects; a re-keyed revlog row keeps its review instant to within milliseconds.
- The same `.apkg` imported on two offline devices converges with no duplicate notes, cards, notetypes or decks.
- Two devices racing to claim the same notetype: one claim wins, the other device rolls back (K19.6), and no receiver ever applies a note or card under the losing layout.
- A claim against an anchor changed elsewhere without a structural change is retried and succeeds; a claim against a tombstoned anchor ends as K19.4 says.
- Adopting a generation whose map chain has a missing step keeps the version as `unmapped_structure`.
- A card whose note's save fails (conflict, oversize, not eligible) is not sent, and no receiver moves it to deck 1.
- A tombstone purged while a device is offline is never brought back, via record deletion events and, once purging is on with K17.4's record-ID check, via a cursor-less fetch.
- An oversize, an unreadable, an unsyncable and a newer-format entity are never sent or overwritten, and others continue.
- Media names (K9.3, K9.4, K15.9): a file deleted outside Meguri makes no tombstone and is downloaded again, or reported missing when the media zone has no blob for it; a file that Delete Unused trashes makes one; a file that leaves the size range is marked unsyncable and makes none; an incoming spelling `a.png` over a clean local `A.png` ends with `a.png` on both devices and the old bytes in the trash, and over a changed local `A.png` keeps the local bytes under their hash name with a `media_content` copy; a crash between a media apply's trash and its install makes no tombstone and completes the install at restart; a download interrupted by a relaunch fetches only the blobs not yet installed.
- Media flags against incoming versions (K9.4): an evicted `file_missing` file that meets another device's new content takes it and downloads it, and sends no tombstone; a `file_missing` name that meets a tombstone trashes nothing and makes no copy; a `superseded` file is never sent, also when the media zone has no blob for the epoch's content; a `withheld` file that meets another device's new content for that name is trashed with a `media_content` copy, is never moved to a hash name, and no note is rewritten; a `withheld` file that meets a tombstone stays; a note of the epoch that references a withheld name syncs its edits.
- Incoming media tombstones (K9.4): over a clean file, the file is trashed with no copy; over a local content change, deletion wins with an `edit_vs_delete` copy naming the trash entry; after a K17.1 conflict the tombstone sent again carries the server version's `MediaName`, the editing device trashes its acked file, and the deleting device downloads the bytes its copy names.
- Two builds of different containers sharing one data directory (K10.2, K10.3): each has its own active binding; neither writes the other's rows; a removal that one build has not consumed survives the other build consuming the feed and becomes a tombstone when the first build consumes it.
- Canonical `Stored` values (K5.3): a card with `odue` integer 0 and `data` empty text, and a note whose `sfld` is integer 0, round-trip with their storage classes unchanged (R2045); `Stored{integer: 0}` and `Stored{text: ""}` encode to different bytes; a payload with a `Stored` field absent, or a `Stored` with no oneof member, is refused as non-canonical.
- Stale blob state (K9.3, K9.5): device A deletes blob C while device B caches it `present`; B names C again (an import, or a new name for the same content); B reads C, finds it absent, uploads it, and no device ends with `mediaMissing`; the same when A's deletion lands after B's version is sent and before B's next drained collection fetch. Fresh deletions (K9.5): A computes the deletion of C at a drained fetch and goes offline before sending it; B names C, reads it present and gets its name acked, and its first drain still finds C; A comes back online within 1 h and sends the deletion; B's drain at least 24 h after the ack reads C, finds it absent and uploads it, and no device ends with `mediaMissing`. When A comes back after more than 1 h, the deletion is not sent and A's next `RunCleanup`, after a fetch that holds B's name, computes none. Names waiting in the inbox (K9.5): B, on a build with a newer envelope format, names C; A fetches that name, which waits with reason `format`, and A's `RunCleanup` computes no deletion of C; with auto on, A's engine asks for a batch after the full update of a drain that fetched B's name and before its apply settles, and the deletion is neither computed nor built; a name fetched after the deletion was computed and before it is built makes the builder skip it.
- Held card images (K6.1, `sync/policy#P7.2`): device A answers card c at t1 and at t3, with R the t3 answer; device B answers c at t2 (t1 < t2 < t3). A barrier sends c's image as after t1 with `last_review_id` t1, so B's merge keeps its t2 scheduling (K15.3) and B sends it; when A then undoes t3 before it fetches B's version, every device ends with the scheduling of t2, and the review-log rows of t1 and t2 remain.
- Joined generations (K19.2): offline, a field reorder on notetype T and its undo make one generation, whose claim carries the original layout with an identity map and is applied by a receiver without an `unmapped_structure` copy; two reorders offline make one generation with the composed map, and a receiver's pending edit in the first layout merges through it; a reorder committed after a claim batch was built makes a second generation, claimed after the first succeeds, and joined to the first when the first claim fails.
- Feed reads (K11.1): with auto on, a picture added through the editor and a Delete Unused each reach tracking at once as a local change, start a read round or a send, and a Sync Now pressed right after Delete Unused selects the tombstone.
- Removal before complete registration (K9.4): the journal is recreated (new `feed_id`), and during its first registration the user runs Delete Unused on an acked name; the name is not downloaded and not reported missing, nothing is sent while the feed is not complete, and the tombstone is sent once it is complete, also after a relaunch in between; a clone mismatch's `mediaChanged` survives a relaunch and clears at the next feed item of that name.
- Bindings (K10.2, K10.3): bound to account A, then switched to B with an upload of this collection (a newer binding of B), then back to A with Resume: A's binding is "the binding", the gate opens and A's edits are sent; after a Resume with no cursor, a new note's record name is computed from the stored record key before any fetch; a `root` read that returns another media key sets `media_zone_missing`, and no blob is saved afterwards.
- Epoch publication and adoption: the adopted collection's counts equal `MGEpoch.counts`, and queued records of the replaced epoch are never applied to the new one.
- Fetch drains (K13.1, A7, `sync/policy#P3`, `#P4`): `RequestFetch{root, collection}` is one `fetchChanges` call with `meguri-root` as a priority zone; a call that returns with no error after a will-fetch-changes event and then a did-fetch-changes event gives one `FetchDrained` per scope zone with the right `fetched` flag; a call that returns with no error but without both (short-circuited, or joined to a running fetch with only that fetch's did-fetch-changes event) gives none, confirms no root and is followed by exactly one more call; a no-state instance's first call that does not fetch a zone gives no `FetchDrained` for it, and the zone is reported not found only after the record-ID read of `active` or `epoch` returns zone not found, or `epoch` returns unknown item (K12.5). Zone deletions (K12.4, K18.5): no full update adds a zone deletion to the engine's pending list, and one restored from a saved engine state is removed; a `DeleteZones` result of zone not found deletes the `zones` row; a fetched deletion of another `c-` zone deletes its row (K12.5); a tombstone or blob deletion that meets unknown item drops the entity or sets the blob `deleted`, makes no `deletion` entry, and is not handed again.
